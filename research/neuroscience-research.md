# Neuroscience Career Roadmap: Research Notes (US, 2026)

This file backs `artifacts/neuroscience-roadmap.jsx`. It was written on 2026-11-23 after the roadmap was first built, so it combines the original research pass with a gap-filling pass. Sources from the first pass were read from search-result excerpts. Sources from the second pass were fetched in full where the site allowed it. Every source below was accessed on 2026-11-23. Figures are US figures unless stated. Source tags like [S7] point to the Sources section.

## TL;DR

- A neuroscience bachelor's is a gateway degree. It is interdisciplinary (biology, chemistry, physics, statistics, then molecular and cellular, systems, and cognitive neuroscience), and most of the careers people picture need a PhD, MD, or other graduate training. [S1-S10]
- The master's is optional. Funded PhD programs take applicants with a bachelor's, and the master's programs checked here are largely self-funded. [S14, S29, S30]
- The PhD is almost always funded (about $31K to $52K stipend), takes five to six years, and starts with lab rotations. [S11-S20]
- Tenure-track jobs are scarce. Only about 14% of biological-science PhDs held one five to six years out, and Harvard's neuroscience program sends more graduates into biotech (18.9%) than into faculty roles (13.2%). [S21, S23]
- Pay depends on sector more than on field. BLS puts medical scientists in life-science R&D at a median of $125,700, against about $75K to $81K at universities. [S41]

## Key Findings

1. The degree is a pointer, not a destination. Bachelor's-level work exists (research assistant, clinical research coordinator, neurodiagnostic technologist, data analyst), but BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as the typical entry education for medical scientists. [S25, S41]
2. The master's is the odd rung. PhD programs fund their students, while master's programs mostly do not. One program reports well over half of its master's alumni went on to PhD programs, and about a quarter went into industry research. [S29, S30]
3. A PhD is paid training. Stipends run from about $31K to $52K, and recent postings run about $39K to $52K. Rotations, a qualifying exam, and a dissertation fill five to six years. [S11-S20]
4. The tenure-track math is hard. Only about 14% of biological-science PhDs held a tenure-track post five to six years after graduating, in a study with 2015 data collection. Across all STEM fields the share within five years fell from 27.0% (1993) to 17.7% (2015). [S23, S59]
5. Industry pays more than universities. BLS medical-scientist medians: $125,700 in life-science R&D, $98,620 in hospitals, $81,370 at private universities, $74,970 at state universities. [S41]
6. Neurotech is small. A 2025 report counted roughly 100 people worldwide using brain-computer interfaces in clinical trials. Neuralink pay on Glassdoor runs from about $115K (engineer) to about $206K (member of technical staff). [S37, S39]
7. Patent work does not require a law degree. A registered patent agent needs a technical degree and the USPTO patent bar exam, and one estimate puts agents with advanced life-sciences degrees at $120K to $160K or more. [S50, S51]
8. In AI for neuroscience, prediction is not mechanism. Foundation models for brain data are advancing fast, but practitioners warn that a model can predict well and still be wrong about how neurons work. [S33-S35]
9. Postdoc pay has a published floor. The NIH FY2026 postdoctoral scale runs from $63,480 (year 0) to $77,076 (year 7 or more). [S55]

## Undergraduate

### Core curriculum

Programs share a spine. Required pieces seen across Duke, Yale, Oregon, Virginia Tech, Berkeley, Penn, Pitt, UT Dallas, Michigan, and Northwestern: [S1-S10]

- A gateway course first (Duke asks for it as the first neuroscience class; Penn requires its intro course to declare). [S1, S6]
- Molecular and cellular neuroscience, then systems, circuits, and behavior, then cognitive neuroscience (Berkeley's two-semester core spans all of these). [S1, S5]
- Statistics (Duke, Oregon, Michigan, Northwestern, Penn). [S1, S3, S6, S9, S10]
- A methods or laboratory course (Duke, Yale, Northwestern). [S1, S2, S10]
- Neuroanatomy, often with a lab (Oregon names a dedicated neuroanatomy course as its preferred option). [S3]
- A senior research, thesis, or capstone element (Yale's BS needs two credits of empirical research at 10 hours a week; Pitt requires a capstone; Berkeley offers an honors thesis). [S2, S5, S7]
- Programming or data skills (Northwestern has a computer programming requirement; Berkeley recommends a data science, statistics, or programming class). [S5, S10]
- Support courses: biology, chemistry, physics, and calculus (Berkeley, Oregon, Penn). Organic chemistry appears in Penn's and Pitt's lists but not in Berkeley's listed prerequisites. [S3, S5, S6, S7]

UT Dallas splits the degree into Medical, Research, and Industrial tracks, which matches the three real destinations: health school, PhD, and industry. [S8]

### Hard courses and grade gates

Direct evidence of weeder courses is thin. A CollegeVine contributor names general chemistry, general biology, and organic chemistry as intentionally difficult and memorization heavy, and says the first two years are designed to filter students, with no pass-rate data. [S26] Structural evidence is firmer:

- Pitt requires a B- or better in its intro course before any other core course, and a C in biology and chemistry to declare. [S7]
- Oregon requires a C or better in every major course. [S3]
- Virginia Tech requires a C- or better in core courses and allows three attempts, withdrawals included, in its core science and math courses. [S4]

### Skills and tools

- Job guides list fMRI and EEG proficiency, Python or MATLAB, and advanced statistics as core competencies. [S24]
- Bench techniques named in entry-level guidance include PCR and microscopy, plus data analysis software. [S25]
- Which techniques a student actually learns depends on the lab they join.

### Beyond the classroom

- Research experience. Columbia's PhD FAQ calls research experience arguably the most important factor in an application and says there is no hard GPA cutoff, and an upward grade trend is acceptable. [S16]
- NSF REUs pay stipends and often help with housing, meals, and travel. They are limited to US citizens, permanent residents, and nationals. The NSF page does not mention neuroscience, but neuroscience-specific sites exist (for example FAU's Summer Integrative Neuroscience Experience, found by title only). [S27, S58]
- Honors theses (Yale, Pitt, Berkeley). Pitt's departmental honors require a 3.50 overall and major GPA, a research project, a presentation, and a senior thesis. [S2, S5, S7]
- The Society for Neuroscience runs NeuroJobs, with on-site recruiting at its annual meeting. SfN's own claims are "the world's largest source of neuroscience opportunities" and a candidate pool of nearly 36,000 neuroscientists. [S28]

## Master's

### Program types

- Research or thesis master's. Stony Brook's program requires a research thesis, takes 1.5 to 2 years, and reports that well over half of alumni entered PhD programs. [S30]
- Standalone coursework master's. Brandeis designs its program for one year full time (1.5 to 2 years with a thesis, which is optional). Students can apply to its PhD program, but admission is not guaranteed, and MS courses and time can count toward the PhD if they are admitted. [S29]
- Accelerated bachelor's-to-master's. UT Dallas lets strong students take up to 15 graduate credits toward both degrees in Applied Cognition and Neuroscience. [S8]
- Computational or quantitative master's. Not verified. Offerings vary by school, so students should check the curriculum.

### Funding and outcomes

- Brandeis states its master's is self-funded: no stipends, merit scholarships, or assistantships, with rare, non-guaranteed TA or lab positions. [S29]
- Stony Brook does not offer direct financial aid, but many students find teaching assistantships with a stipend and a one-course tuition waiver. [S30]
- Stony Brook outcomes: well over half to PhD programs, about a quarter to biomedical industry research, plus medical school, medical writing, lab management, research coordination, and education. [S30]
- Brandeis's PhD FAQ notes that applicants can ask to be considered for the master's program if they are not admitted to the PhD. [S14]

### Terminal, bridge, or skipped

It is often skipped. Funded PhD programs (Columbia, Brandeis, UT Austin, Utah, BU) take applications from bachelor's holders, though no program page states this in so many words. It works as a bridge for applicants who need a research record, and as a terminal credential for industry research and adjacent work.

### Advanced skills (general domain knowledge, not individually sourced)

- Modeling and theory for neurons and networks.
- Advanced neuroimaging analysis (fMRI, EEG, MEG pipelines, connectivity, machine learning).
- Regulatory and clinical-trial literacy. A Good Clinical Practice certificate is named for entry-level research coordination and regulatory affairs. [S25]

## PhD / Research

### How doctoral training works

- Funding. Columbia says tuition, fees, and stipend are fully funded for all students. Utah, Brandeis, BU, and UCSD describe full support while students make progress. [S12-S16]
- Stipends. Harvard $51,500 over 12 months (2025-26); Utah $39,254 (2026-27); UT Austin $40,000 guaranteed for five years; Brandeis $38,496 (2023-24). Older figures from a 2022 aggregator: Stanford $48,216, UC Davis about $33,902, Rochester $32,300, Buffalo $31,000. [S11, S13, S14, S18, S20]
- Boot camp. UT Austin runs a two-week boot camp before year one, and UCSD has one. [S11, S12]
- Rotations. UT Austin 2 to 3 (eight weeks each), UCSD 3, Utah 3, Tulane 3, BU 2 to 4, Brandeis 4. The goal is to choose a home lab. [S11-S15, S17]
- Qualifying exam. UT Austin embeds a breadth exam in first-year course finals and adds a review paper; UCSD examines the dissertation proposal; UCSF uses an NIH-grant-format proposal; Utah and Brandeis hold it at the end of year two; Tulane holds written and oral components in year three. [S11, S12, S13, S14, S17, S19]
- Duration. UT Austin averages five years; BU and Brandeis say five to six; UCSD expects under six, requires completion by the end of year six, and stops support in year seven for non-MSTP students. [S11, S12, S14, S15]
- Fellowships. UT Austin expects students to submit a predoctoral NRSA or similar fellowship in years three to five. Harvard students apply for NSF GRFP, NIH F30/F31, and HHMI Gilliam fellowships. [S11, S18]

### Current research frontiers

- Connectomics. In October 2024 the FlyWire consortium published the complete wiring diagram of an adult fruit-fly brain, nearly 140,000 neurons and more than 50 million synapses, in a nine-paper *Nature* package. NIH calls it the largest and most complex connectome to date and a stepping stone to maps in larger animals. [S31]
- Brain organoids and assembloids. iPSC-derived 3D models, including cortical-striatal, cortical-thalamic, and midline assembloids, are used for development and disease modeling. Limits: restricted vascularization, variability between organoids, and unresolved ethical concerns. [S32]
- NeuroAI and brain foundation models. BrainLM (trained on UK Biobank and Human Connectome Project data), POYO (spike tokenization for neural recordings), and TRIBE v2 (a model aligning AI representations to fMRI responses across hundreds of individuals). [S33, S34]
- Brain-computer interfaces. Neuralink, Synchron, and Neuracle were recruiting volunteers in a 2025 report. Implants let paralyzed people control a cursor and, in a few cases, produce speech. Neuralink job text also mentions a visual prosthesis (Blindsight). [S37, S38]
- Circuit tools. Neuropixels probes had shipped to more than 450 labs by 2021. Single-cell omics appears in the organoid literature. Optogenetics and calcium imaging are standard circuit tools by general domain knowledge, but no fetched source confirmed this. [S32, S36]
- Neurodegeneration and precision psychiatry. Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Huntington's disease are central organoid targets, and a 2026 career guide lists precision psychiatry, LLM interpretability, and BCIs among the fastest-moving applied areas. [S24, S32]

## Tools and AI

### Standard tools by level

- Undergraduate: fMRI and EEG analysis, statistics software, Python or MATLAB, basic bench techniques. [S24, S25]
- Research: Neuropixels (nearly 1,000 recording sites per shank, about a third usable at once on version 1.0, commercial since 2018), single-cell omics, automated organoid culture platforms. [S32, S36]
- Writing and funding: NIH-format proposals are part of training at programs such as UCSF. [S19]

### AI adoption and the practitioner view

- Foundation models for fMRI and neural recordings are an active area, and authors describe a possible paradigm shift toward unified predictive models of the brain. They also acknowledge the black-box problem. [S33, S34]
- A 2022 Nautilus feature quotes researchers on the limits. In synthetic data with known dynamics, some of the best-predicting models were the least faithful to the true system. Prediction accuracy is not proof of mechanism, and model comparisons can be fragile. [S35]
- Net view: AI is clearly useful for prediction, decoding, and analysis, and still unsettled for explaining how circuits work. The Nautilus piece is from 2022, so newer practitioner commentary may differ.

## Career Paths

Each entry gives titles, reality, salary, and sources. The AI-exposure lines are analyst judgement and are not drawn from a source.

### 1. Academic Research

- Titles: PhD student, postdoc, research scientist, assistant professor, tenured professor.
- Reality: About 14% of biological-science PhDs held a tenure-track post five to six years out. Harvard's neuroscience program reports 13.2% of recent graduates in faculty roles (mostly tenured or tenure-track) and 26.3% in postdocs. Fewer than half of postdocs land tenure-track posts, grant writing takes a third or more of a professor's time, and tenure can take more than a decade after the PhD. A study of 781 neuroscience PhDs found academia is the default path, with interest in other careers rising over time. [S21-S24]
- Salary: PhD stipend about $31K to $52K. Postdoc about $56K to $77K (the NIH FY2026 scale is $63,480 to $77,076). BLS median for postsecondary biological science teachers is $84,620 across all ranks (May 2025). No neuroscience-specific faculty salary by rank was found. [S20, S24, S54, S55]
- AI exposure (judgement): low. AI is a research tool here.

### 2. Biotech and Pharma R&D

- Titles: research associate, scientist, senior scientist, principal scientist, director.
- Reality: 18.9% of Harvard's recent neuroscience PhD graduates work in biotech and therapeutics research, more than the 13.2% in faculty roles. BLS lists a doctoral or professional degree as typical entry education for medical scientists, with sometimes a master's plus experience. Bachelor's-level lab roles exist. [S21, S25, S41]
- Salary: BLS medical scientists, May 2025: median $103,410, 10th to 90th percentile $64,800 to $177,780. Life-science R&D median $125,700 versus $74,970 to $81,370 at universities. Projected growth 13% from 2025 to 2035. Senior and director pay was not found. [S41]
- AI exposure (judgement): moderate. Computational skills widen options.

### 3. Neurotech and BCI

- Titles: neuroengineer, neural data scientist, BCI engineer, research scientist, tech lead.
- Reality: Neuralink, Synchron, Blackrock Neurotech, and Neuracle are the named players. Neuralink hubs include San Francisco, Fremont, and Austin; Blackrock is in Salt Lake City. Job text asks for neuroscience, biomedical engineering, electrical engineering, or computer science, often with an advanced degree. The field is small: about 100 people worldwide in BCI trials in a 2025 report. [S37, S38]
- Salary: Neuralink on Glassdoor (134 salaries, June 2026): about $114,781 (engineer) to $206,382 (member of technical staff). Salary.com average $120,538. Single-company data with small samples. One low-quality aggregator reports software-engineer total compensation up to $315K, which is not used. [S39, S40]
- AI exposure (judgement): low. Decoding neural signals is a machine-learning problem.

### 4. Clinical and Health

- Titles: neurodiagnostic technologist, clinical research coordinator, neuropsychologist, neurologist or physician assistant.
- Reality: With a bachelor's, neurodiagnostic technologist and clinical research coordinator roles are open. Clinical neuropsychology needs a psychology doctorate and a two-year postdoctoral fellowship; board certification is associated with 15% to 25% higher pay. Neurology needs an MD and residency. [S25, S43]
- Salary: neurodiagnostic technologist about $60K to $90K and clinical research coordinator about $50K to $90K (Research.com pages disagree, a weak source). Neuropsychologist median about $108,500 (a survey-based estimate; the BLS "psychologists, all other" median is $106,420 per a secondary source; Glassdoor shows $179,973 but from six salaries). Neurologist median $248,560 (BLS, May 2025; a physician-salary site reports $286,310, which conflicts and is not used). [S25, S42, S43, S44, S56]
- AI exposure (judgement): low to moderate. Licensure protects clinicians.

### 5. Data Science and Analytics

- Titles: data analyst, data scientist, senior data scientist, ML engineer, analytics lead.
- Reality: BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education for data scientists and projects 35% growth from 2025 to 2035. A neuroscience background brings statistics and large messy datasets. Competition comes from computer science and statistics graduates. [S45]
- Salary: BLS median $120,230 (May 2025), 10th to 90th percentile $67,240 to $199,130. Biotech or healthcare analyst about $65K to $100K; neuroscience data analyst starting about $55K to $75K (Research.com). [S25, S45]
- AI exposure (judgement): moderate. Routine analysis is easier to automate than pipeline and model engineering.

### 6. Science Communication and Policy

- Titles: science writer, medical writer, communications lead, policy analyst, program officer.
- Reality: Medical writing is the best-paid branch. BLS projects technical writing to grow only 1% from 2025 to 2035. Policy fellowships such as AAAS's pay a stipend, and agencies and funders shape which research happens. Stony Brook lists medical writing among its master's outcomes. [S30, S46-S49]
- Salary: medical writer about $79,727 average entry-level (PayScale, range $61K to $103K, March 2025) and AMWA 2023 means for employed medical communicators of roughly $110K to $166K across bands. Technical writer median $90,390 (BLS, May 2025). AAAS policy fellow stipend roughly $101,401 to $131,826 (the page's summary says "from $94K"). [S46-S49]
- AI exposure (judgement): moderate. Drafting is automatable, and accuracy and credibility are the human part.

### 7. Patent Law and Consulting

- Titles: technical specialist, patent agent, patent attorney, consultant, partner.
- Reality: A patent attorney needs a JD, the USPTO patent bar, and a state bar. A patent agent needs a technical bachelor's, master's, or PhD (or concentrated science coursework) and the patent bar, but no law degree. Advanced life-sciences degrees sit at the top of the agent pay scale. For consulting, BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education for management analysts, and top-tier PhD hiring was not researched. [S50, S51, S53]
- Salary: patent agent about $70K to $110K before registration, $90K to $140K registered in engineering or software, and $120K to $160K or more with an advanced life-sciences degree (2026 estimates). Lawyers overall median $159,670, 10th to 90th percentile $78,360 to $351,600 (BLS, May 2025). Management analysts median $101,860, 10th to 90th percentile $60,640 to $171,640. Patent-attorney-specific pay and top-firm consulting pay were not found. [S51-S53]
- AI exposure (judgement): low.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- US only. UK, Canadian, and European degree structures (for example three-year bachelor's degrees and research master's routes into the PhD) were not researched. BLS data is US only.
- BLS has no neuroscientist category. Medical scientists is a proxy, and it includes many other life scientists.
- Single-program and small-sample data. Harvard's career outcomes cover one elite program, and the page excerpt was cut off after 10.3%, so the listed categories sum to 87.6%. Neuralink Glassdoor and Salary.com figures come from small samples at one company. The Glassdoor neuropsychologist figure comes from six salaries.
- The 14% tenure-track figure comes from data collected in 2015, covers several biological-science programs, and excludes MD-PhD students. [S23]
- Stipends vary with cost of living. The low end ($31K to $34K) comes from a 2022 aggregator and 2023-24 postings, and recent postings run higher. [S20]
- Research.com figures are inconsistent between its own pages (for example clinical research coordinator at $50K to $80K, $55K to $85K, and $60K to $90K). Treat them as indicative.
- Not found: neuroscience-specific faculty pay by rank, senior and director biotech pay, patent-attorney-specific pay, top-tier consulting pay for PhDs, and any computational neuroscience master's program details.
- Not confirmed by a fetched source: optogenetics and calcium imaging as standard tools (the page fetch was blocked by a CAPTCHA), and the claim that funded PhD programs admit bachelor's holders directly (inferred from program FAQs).
- The AAAS stipend page contradicts itself ($101K to $131K in the body, "from $94K" in the summary) and gives no cohort year.
- A physician-salary site reports neurologists at $286,310, against BLS at $248,560. The roadmap uses BLS.
- AI-exposure lines in Career Paths are analyst judgement, not sourced.
