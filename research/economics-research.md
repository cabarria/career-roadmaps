## TL;DR

- The economics BA is highly standardized (principles, then intermediate micro, intermediate macro, and econometrics, plus electives). The decision that matters most is how much proof-based math you take, because PhD admissions treat real analysis as a near requirement.
- The US bridge to an elite PhD is increasingly a paid 1-2 year predoc, not a master's degree. Over 40% of admits to top programs have predoc experience and over 75% of predocs go on to a PhD.
- The PhD is a 5-6 year, funded apprenticeship with a first-year core, qualifying exams, field courses, and a job market paper sold on one centralized annual market.
- Pay is high but wide. BLS reports a median of $115,440 for "economists" and only 1% projected growth, but that narrow title misses most PhD hires in tech, consulting, finance, and central banks.
- Pay figures from aggregator sites conflict badly. Prefer the AEA, BLS, IMF, and Tradermath numbers used below.

## Key Findings

- **Core curriculum is near-identical across schools.** Intermediate micro, intermediate macro, and econometrics, with calculus (and often statistics) as prerequisites (S1, S2, S3, S4, S6).
- **Two math tracks exist at the same school.** Princeton offers each core course in a standard and a more mathematical version (S2). Penn offers a Math-Econ major (S8).
- **Real analysis is the de facto PhD gate.** Multiple departments and the AEA advise it (S10, S11, S12, S14, S17).
- **Predocs have become the main on-ramp.** More than 40% of top-program admits and over 75% of predocs continuing to a PhD (S20). Fed Board RA programs serve the same role (S25).
- **Half of entering PhD students already hold a master's** according to the AEA (S12), so a master's is common but not the elite route.
- **PhD length:** 5-6 years in the US, 3-4 years in Europe after a research master's (S18, S32). Johns Hopkins describes a six-year program (S31).
- **Funding is standard.** Stated stipends at four programs run from about $43k to $52k (S76 to S79), and one secondary source gives about $34.5k at Johns Hopkins (S31).
- **Academic pay depends heavily on institution type.** 2025-26 average assistant professor salaries are $164,898 at PhD-granting departments, $133,210 at MA-granting, and $113,460 at BA-granting (S45).
- **Entry-level quant trading pay is far above everything else here.** Tradermath reports new-grad base of $300k at Jane Street, SIG, and Hudson River Trading (S80).
- **The Fed Board alone employs just over 400 PhD economists** (S81).
- **AI sources document automation of research micro-tasks** (coding, literature review, derivations) (S37, S38). The claim that predoc-type work is the most exposed rung is this author's inference, not a finding in those sources.

## Undergraduate

### Core structure

- **Principles of micro and macro** come first, often with a precalculus prerequisite and a C-or-better requirement (S3).
- **Three-course core:** intermediate microeconomics, intermediate macroeconomics, and econometrics (S1, S3, S4). Berkeley computes the major GPA from these three plus five upper-division electives (S1).
- **Rutgers sequence:** 01:220:320 (micro), 321 (macro), and 322 (econometrics, which also needs Calculus I and a statistics course), plus seven electives of which at least four are upper-level (S3, S4).
- **Princeton** requires three core courses taken by junior year, each in two math levels. The standard versions need MAT 175 or equivalent, and the econometrics-heavy mathematical version expects MAT 201/202 (S2).
- **Duke** requires its Econ 101 for all majors starting Fall 2023 and no longer accepts AP or transfer credit for it (S5).
- **Northwestern** requires at minimum MATH 220-1, strongly advises the full first-year calculus sequence, and requires coverage of at least six topic areas (S6).
- **Yale's B.S.** is 14 term courses plus a senior project (S7).
- **Ohio State** adds a required writing course because many jobs for economics majors depend on writing (S9).
- **Electives** span labor, industrial organization, public, international, development, financial, behavioral, game theory, and economic history (S1, S8).

### The weeder question

None of the retrieved sources call a specific economics course a "weeder." What they show is that intermediate micro, macro, and econometrics are gated by calculus and, at Rutgers, by C-or-better grades (S3, S4). Any statement that a given course "convinces many majors to switch" is anecdote that is not sourced here.

### The math fork

- **Industry track:** calculus plus statistics meets the stated requirements (S3, S6).
- **PhD track:** multivariable calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, probability, mathematical statistics, and real analysis (S10, S11, S12, S14, S15). The AEA says many applicants have taken real analysis and that 1-2 math courses per semester is the advised load (S12).
- **First-year graduate courses draw heavily on real analysis**, and real analysis is described as the first fully rigorous proof course (S17).
- **UCSD** says the department's own major math is not enough for a top PhD and advises proofs, linear algebra, real analysis, probability, and statistics, and advises considering the joint math-economics major (S11). UCLA similarly lists linear algebra, real analysis, and differential equations (S14).
- **Graduate courses as an undergraduate** are a strong signal but risky, since grades matter and graduate cohorts form study groups (S11).
- **GPA:** Binghamton reports GPAs of successful top-program applicants are usually 3.85 or higher (S18).

### Tools

- **Stata** is the field's entrenched default and appears in nearly every predoc ad (S22, a 2020 snapshot republished 2023). **R, Python, and MATLAB** are the other common tools (S10, S11).
- Every predoc job description reviewed by one source named a programming language as a basic requirement (S10).

### Beyond the classroom

- **RA work for faculty** is called a strong predictor of predoc success and produces informed recommendation letters (S11, S19).
- **Honors thesis:** UCSD, UCLA, and Binghamton all recommend one (S11, S14, S18).
- **Named programs:** the AEA Summer Training Program and Scholarship Program (S13), UChicago's Expanding Discovery in Economics+ (S23), and the PREDOC.org Student Research Conference (S21).
- **Societies and competitions:** Rutgers lists Fed Challenge, the ODE international honor society, and RECONS in its department navigation (S3). Nothing retrieved describes how Fed Challenge works.
- **Internships:** Federal Reserve bank research internships and Brookings' paid internship cohorts (about 150 students per year) appear on PREDOC.org's part-time list (S19). Cornerstone runs 8-week summer analyst programs that closely mirror full-time work (S56).

## Master's and Predoc

### The predoc path

- **Definition:** full-time jobs supporting faculty research, typically lasting two years (S21).
- **Outcomes:** over 75% of predocs go on to a PhD, and over 40% of accepted applicants at top PhD programs have predoc experience (S20).
- **Structure example:** the University of Zurich's predoc is 70% research and 30% on-the-job learning (S24).
- **Employers:** Chicago's Becker Friedman Institute PREP (S23), MIT Blueprint Labs, Chicago Booth, Dartmouth Tuck, the Minneapolis Fed, AEI, and regional Federal Reserve banks (S19).
- **Visa limits:** several listings state no visa sponsorship and require US work authorization (S19).
- **Selection:** a skills test usually follows first review (S22). Prior RA experience is one of the strongest indicators of success (S21).
- **Fed Board RA program:** two years, about 150 RAs across seven divisions, tuition assistance that many use for math and statistics courses, and more than half of RAs enter graduate programs (S25). No salary figure was found for this role.

### Master's options

- **MA/MS in economics or applied economics:** Hopkins, Michigan, Duke, and Georgetown are named as offering applied programs (S28, a forum post). Duke lists an M.S. in Economics and Computation and an M.A. in Analytical Political Economy (S5).
- **MPP versus MPA:** an MPP leans quantitative (economics, statistics, data) and an MPA leans on management and implementation. Both take 1-2 years full-time (S27). Pepperdine's applied economic policy MPP specialization includes micro, macro, and econometrics (S26).
- **Opinion on marketability:** forum commenters say an MPP is more marketable than a US MA in economics outside PhD pathways (S28). This is anonymous opinion, not data.
- **Europe:** research master's programs are the normal PhD entry, after which the PhD can run 3-4 years (S32, S33). The retrieved sources name no specific European master's programs.
- **PhD admission context:** about half of entering PhD students hold a master's (S12). Predocs and direct entry are alternatives.

## PhD / Research

### Structure

- **Year 1:** core courses in micro, macro, and econometrics, then comprehensive exams (S34, S35, S36). Stony Brook sits the exams in June with a retake in late July or August (S35). Georgia requires passing two of three exams (S36). WashU lets a student qualify with an A- or higher in first-year courses instead of the exam (S34).
- **Years 2-3:** field courses plus a second-year or third-year research paper at many programs (S29, S30, S36). MIT requires two major and two minor fields and a 13-course minimum (S29).
- **Dissertation:** MIT describes a thesis as normally three publishable papers (S29). One of them becomes the job market paper (S32, S33).
- **Duration:** five to six years including the job search (S18). Johns Hopkins describes six years with five years of guaranteed funding (S31).
- **Funding:** tuition waiver plus stipend tied to TA or RA work (S18, S31). Stated annual stipends:
  - Yale: minimum $52,046 for humanities and social sciences, 2026-27, all PhD fields (S76)
  - Brown: $52,198 for 12 months, FY26 (S77)
  - UChicago Social Sciences: $46,350 minimum, 2025-26 (S78)
  - Emory economics: $43,260, with a raise noted for 9/2026 (S79)
  - Johns Hopkins: about $34,500, from a secondary guide (S31)
- **Job market cycle:** summer finish of the job market paper, applications September-November, first-round interviews December-January around the AEA/ASSA meetings, flyouts January-February, offers February-March, start July-September (S32, S33).
- **Time to tenure:** often a 6-8 year window to build a record (S32).

### Research frontiers (as supported by retrieved sources)

- **Causal machine learning:** double/debiased machine learning, causal forests, generalized random forests, and heterogeneous treatment effects (S41). Foundational citations include Athey and Imbens and Chernozhukov and coauthors (S41).
- **Deep learning for economic models:** Fernandez-Villaverde's NBER work on deep learning for solving economic models and on taming the curse of dimensionality (S42), including deep multi-agent reinforcement learning for heterogeneous RBC models (S42).
- **Market and platform design:** auctions (Wilson, 1960s), pricing, reputation systems, search costs, and ad auctions (S54).
- **Economics of AI and LLM use in research:** Korinek's JEL article and 2025 agentic update (S37, S38), plus work on AI exposure of occupations (S40).
- **Not found in sources:** "HANK" as a named model class, a general description of the RCT movement, and a list of identification strategies (difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, synthetic control). These appear in the JSX from background knowledge only.

### Placement

- Graduating institution is a large factor in placement according to the literature the AEA 2025 slides cite (S16).
- econphdplacements.com aggregates 8,000+ placements from 40 top departments over 30+ years, splitting tenure-track, temporary academic, central bank, and international organization outcomes, and warns its tenure-track share may be biased upward (S75).

## Tools and AI

- **Standard tools:** Stata, R, Python, MATLAB (S10, S11, S22).
- **Korinek (JEL 2023, updated August 2025):** describes dozens of LLM use cases in six areas (ideation and feedback, writing, background research, data analysis, coding, mathematical derivations) and argues economists can gain productivity by automating micro-tasks, with gains rising as models improve (S37). The 2025 update adds agentic systems that scan literature, write and debug code, interpret statistical output, and help edit manuscripts, including multi-agent frameworks (S38).
- **Labor-market exposure literature:** Eloundou et al. (2023) estimate up to 80% of US workers could see at least 10% of tasks affected by LLMs. A CEA study estimates about 10% of the workforce is potentially vulnerable (S40).
- **Employer stance:** Cornerstone says generative AI supports its work and complements rather than replaces expertise and judgment (S55).
- **BLS context:** BLS expects AI to limit demand in several office and administrative occupations. It does not single out economists (S44).
- **Not established by sources:** the claim that predoc/RA tasks are the single most automatable rung. This is an inference and is labeled as such wherever it appears.
- **Practitioner hype view:** nothing retrieved gives a skeptical practitioner assessment. Research.com's AI piece is a general advice article (S39).

## Career Paths

All salary figures are US dollars unless stated. See the audit section for figures that changed after gap-fill checks.

### 1. Academia

- **Titles:** Assistant Professor, Associate Professor, Full Professor. Teaching-track roles such as "assistant professor of the practice" exist (Boston College posting, S87).
- **Reality:** Entry is stratified by PhD program rank (S16), and building a record is a 6-8 year window (S32). Teaching-track and liberal-arts jobs trade research prestige for different workloads.
- **Salary:** AEA 2025-26 averages (S45):
  - Assistant: $164,898 (PhD-granting), $133,210 (MA-granting), $113,460 (BA-granting)
  - Associate: $195,262 / $140,050 / $125,558
  - Full: $262,782 / $147,670 / $150,513
  - Average starting salary for new assistant professors at PhD-granting institutions: $158,593 (S45)
- **Conflicting source:** ZipRecruiter reports an average of about $80,057, with a 90th percentile of $118,000 (S74). It is not credible against AEA survey data.

### 2. Central banking and government

- **Titles:** Research Assistant, Economist, Senior Economist (the ladder above Economist is not confirmed by a Fed page).
- **Reality:** The Federal Reserve Board employs just over 400 PhD economists (S81). An older Fed brochure says more than 300 (S82, undated). All Board economist positions are in Washington, D.C. (S81). Roughly 150 RAs work across seven divisions (S25).
- **Salary:** One closed Board posting, "Economist, Industrial Output," listed $170,000-$220,000 (closed January 30, 2023, S83). No Fed RA salary figure was found. The BLS median for the occupation is $115,440 for May 2024 (S43). Glassdoor's $141,636-$259,388 band for "Economist" appears identically on its BLS, Amazon, and IMF pages, so it is a generic estimate and not Fed-specific (S46, S47, S48).
- **Outlook:** BLS projects 1% growth from 2024 to 2034, with about 900 openings per year (S43).

### 3. Economic consulting

- **Titles:** Analyst, Senior Consultant, Manager, Vice President or Principal (S58). A university career page lists Analyst, Associate, Senior Economist, Manager, Vice President, Director or Partner (S62).
- **Reality:** Firms hire analysts from bachelor's programs and associates from master's programs, but expert-testimony roles require a PhD (S57, S62). Analysts take 3-5 years to reach Senior Consultant and 2-3 more to reach Manager (S58). Cornerstone case teams include academic or industry experts (S56).
- **Salary:**
  - Analysis Group analyst base about $96,000, signing bonus about $17,500, performance bonus up to $24,000 (S57)
  - Cornerstone analyst average $104,367 (S61), WallStreetOasis average base $85,000 plus $12,500 bonus (S60)
  - Cornerstone Principal up to $297,007 (S59)
  - Hacking the Case Interview reports a median total pay of about $170,000, with a range of about $127,000-$233,000 (S57)
- **Conflicting source:** the same page says BLS projects 13% growth for economists. BLS says 1% (S43).

### 4. Tech economist

- **Titles:** Economist, Senior Economist, Principal Economist, Chief Economist (S52, S54).
- **Reality:** Amazon hires PhD economists for pricing, market design, forecasting, program evaluation, and advertising (S52). An August 2023 article counted over 250 PhD economists or close fields at Amazon (S53). The Athey and Luca paper names Amazon, Wealthfront, Uber, Airbnb, Microsoft, Netflix, Coursera, Expedia, Pandora, Yelp, and Zillow, and recounts Google's chief economist (S54). Meta does not appear in any retrieved source. Uber's own posting says "We hire economists to be economists, not generic data scientists" (S50).
- **Salary:**
  - Amazon: L4 $219,000 to L5 $284,000, median $248,000, updated 2026-05-23 (S49)
  - Uber PhD Scientist II base: $155,000-$172,000 (S50) or $161,000-$179,000 (S51) in New York, San Francisco, and Seattle, plus bonus and possible equity

### 5. International organizations

- **Titles:** Economist Program (EP) economist, Economist, Senior Economist, Mission Chief (EP details from S63, other titles from background knowledge).
- **Reality:** The IMF Economist Program is a three-year program with two departmental assignments (S63). Applications open in late September, interviews run October-January, and offers go out in early February (S63). The IMF's official benefits page lists leave, pension, and expatriate education support, and states no salary (S85).
- **Salary:**
  - IMF salary structure effective May 1, 2026: grade A11 $123,420-$185,130, grade A12 $142,240-$213,360. Gross versus net is not stated in the retrieved text (S84).
  - A secondary site says most new PhD economists enter at A11 and describes pay as tax-free (S64).
  - Anonymous forum posts quote about $115,000 net at entry and about $225,000 for senior economists (S65). These are unverified.
  - No official World Bank figure was retrieved.

### 6. Finance and quantitative research

- **Titles:** Analyst, Senior Analyst, Portfolio Manager, Quant Researcher (S67).
- **Reality:** Global macro funds favor economists (S67). Quant postings ask for advanced coursework in mathematics, sciences, economics, and programming (S91, undated).
- **Salary:**
  - Hedge fund analyst base $100,000-$150,000 with bonuses of 100-200% of base in strong years, total $200,000-$600,000 (S67). Junior analyst "total" quoted at $100,000-$150,000 on the same site (S67), an internal inconsistency.
  - Quant trading new-grad base about $300,000 at Jane Street, SIG, and Hudson River Trading, total estimated $310,000-$450,000, and $355,000-$600,000 at 1-3 years (S80, Tradermath estimates updated 2026-09-28).
  - A ZipRecruiter average of $133,877 for "hedge fund quantitative analyst" (S88) conflicts and is an aggregator figure.
  - London data scientists and quants with 1-3 years: GBP 120,000 and GBP 144,000 total at hedge funds (S68, 2024).

### 7. Data, policy, and think tanks (mostly bachelor's-accessible)

- **Titles:** Data Analyst, Policy Analyst, Economic Analyst, Budget Analyst, Research Associate.
- **Reality:** Roles span agencies, companies, and think tanks such as Brookings and AEI (S19). Policy analyst roles at agencies and think tanks generally prefer a master's in economics or public policy (S72).
- **Salary:** Starting figures conflict between Research.com pages: a median start of about $60,000 on some (S69, S71) and $74,000 on another (S70). Entry ranges quoted are $55,000-$75,000 (S69). Nexford's average for policy analysts is $81,139 and for financial analysts $76,582 (S73). Research.com also gives budget analyst $86,200 and data scientist $126,830 averages (S72).
- **Not established:** that junior descriptive analytics is the segment most exposed to AI. No retrieved source says this.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **US focus.** Degree structure, funding, and salaries here are US. Europe uses research master's programs and shorter PhDs (S32, S33). The University of Zurich runs 1-2 year predocs (S24).
- **Aggregator quality.** Glassdoor, Research.com, ZipRecruiter, and PayScale figures are self-reported or modeled and often conflict with official sources. Several of those sites also quote outdated BLS growth numbers.
- **Self-contradictory secondary sources.** The Hacking the Case Interview page and one Research.com page cite 13-14% BLS growth, against BLS's 1% (S43, S57).
- **Snippet-only evidence.** Most university requirement pages and salary pages were read as search excerpts, not full fetches (see "Seen as").
- **Dated items.** The Fed economist posting is from 2023 (S83). The Amazon headcount is from 2023 (S53). The Chattergoon predoc article is from 2023 and cites a 2020 data snapshot (S22).
- **Missing figures.** No official Fed RA salary (S25, S86), no Fed Board PhD economist salary beyond one 2023 posting (S83), no World Bank YPP salary (S90 had no figures), no official IMF tax status (S85).
- **Visa and citizenship.** Many predoc and some Fed postings require US work authorization (S19).
- **Location.** Fed Board roles are in Washington, D.C. (S81). Tech postings cluster in New York, San Francisco, Seattle (S50, S51).
- **Quant caveat.** Tradermath's totals are estimates, not reported pay (S80).
