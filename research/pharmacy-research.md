# Pharmacy: Research Notes for the Roadmap

Prepared for the Pharmacy career-roadmap artifact (`artifacts/pharmacy-roadmap.jsx`). Every figure below traces to a source listed at the end. Where the first draft of the artifact said something the sources do not support, that is recorded under Uncertainties.

Version 1. The first draft of the roadmap was built from eight search passes in mid-2026 and no saved research file. This version adds a verification pass on 2026-11-23 (32 lookups, mostly page fetches; several searches returned only links and one fetch was blocked) and replaces or removes first-draft claims the sources did not support.

Access dates: sources marked "fetched" were retrieved on 2026-11-23 through a fetch tool that returns an extract of the page, not the raw text, so details beyond the extract were not checked. Sources marked "excerpt" were seen only as search-result excerpts during the original build session (mid-2026; the exact day was not recorded). Drug Channels' June 2026 post appeared as "3 weeks ago" in that session, which suggests early July 2026.

## TL;DR

- Pharmacy has no bachelor's-then-master's arc. A graduate completes pre-pharmacy coursework, then a typically four-year Doctor of Pharmacy (PharmD), which BLS lists as the typical entry credential with no work experience or on-the-job training required. Licensure needs the NAPLEX and a state law exam (MPJE). [S1] [E3] [E6]
- Pay and outlook (BLS, May 2025 data): median $140,910; projected growth 5% for 2025 to 2035 (+17,100 jobs); about 12,500 openings a year; 325,200 jobs. [S1]
- The market is moving by setting. Retail pharmacist employment was about 162,000 in May 2025, down 4.8% and the lowest since Drug Channels began tracking in 2010. Hospitals employed about 102,000, up 3.0%. BLS medians: hospitals $157,290; ambulatory healthcare services $153,920; general merchandise retailers $151,370; food and beverage retailers $144,000; pharmacies and drug retailers $132,940. [S1] [S2]
- Admission is not very selective. The PCAT was retired in January 2024. AACP reports applications rose 6% from fall 2024 to fall 2025 (the second straight yearly rise) and that programs accept more than 80% of applicants. Total enrollment fell 4.7% in fall 2024, and attrition rose to 19.7% from 15.3%. [E9] [S6] [S7]
- NAPLEX first-time pass rates for ACPE-accredited graduates were 87.0%, 88.2%, and 87.0% in 2017 to 2019, fell to 75.9% by 2024, then rose to 86.8% in 2025. NABP gives no reason for the 2025 change. [S5]
- Postgraduate training is common, but its status as a job requirement is only partly documented. One PCOM faculty member says most hospitals require at least one year of residency and community and retail jobs generally do not. Pitt reports 59% of its class of 2024 went on to a residency or fellowship. No posting-level data was found. In the 2026 ASHP Match, 3,787 of 4,486 active applicants matched. [S9] [E13] [S8]
- The research doctorate is a separate track. Michigan reports 5.3 years on average to defense, and Michigan and Wisconsin both fund stipend and tuition. [S13] [S14]
- Evidence on AI and automation is descriptive. Hospitals widely use verification and analytics tools, but no source quantified AI job exposure for pharmacists. [E34]
- Eight career destinations were researched: community and retail, hospital, clinical and ambulatory care, industry and medical affairs, regulatory and government, managed care, nuclear and specialty, and informatics.

## Key Findings

- Mean and median are easy to confuse here. BLS reports a median of $140,910 (May 2025). Drug Channels reports a mean of $140,920 (up 2.7% from $137,210 in 2024). Becker's, working from May 2025 BLS data, reports a national mean of about $140,180. The roadmap uses the BLS median and labels it as such. [S1] [S2] [E20]
- Pharmacists earn about 30% more than the average healthcare practitioner ($108,700 in 2025), down from about 54% ten years earlier. [S2]
- Setting matters for pay. BLS industry medians (May 2025): hospitals $157,290; ambulatory healthcare services $153,920; general merchandise retailers $151,370; food and beverage retailers $144,000; pharmacies and drug retailers $132,940. Drug Channels means, as reported by Becker's: outpatient care centers $166,820; hospitals $150,840; physician offices $145,050; home health $142,180; mass merchants $143,020; supermarkets $137,680; drugstores and independents $130,900. [S1] [S4]
- Retail is shrinking and hospitals are growing. Retail employment fell by 8,200 in 2025 (-4.8%) to about 162,000. Hospital employment rose by 3,000 (+3.0%) to about 102,000 after adding more than 11,000 in 2023 and 2024 combined. The two remaining national drugstore chains have closed nearly 3,000 locations since 2018. [S2] [E19]
- Drug Channels notes its hospital counts likely understate hospital pharmacists because on-site institutional pharmacies are coded as retail. [E19]
- Admission is easy to get and outcomes are uneven. Programs accept more than 80% of applicants (AACP), while NAPLEX first-time pass rates for individual schools range widely: Pitt's class of 2023 passed at 91.5% against a 78.6% national figure Pitt cites; one Nova Southeastern campus posted 68.6% for 2024 graduates. [S6] [E13] [E18]
- Most graduates borrow: 81.5% of 5,761 respondents to AACP's 2026 graduating student survey said they borrowed for the PharmD. No current debt amount was found. [S17]
- Resident pay varies widely: PGY1 is $57,600 at Inova (2026-27 estimate) and $79,344 at UC San Diego (year not stated). [S11] [S12]
- ASHP Match 2026, Phases I and II combined: 4,486 active applicants with a rank list (4,982 registered), 3,787 matched, 699 unmatched. The 84% match rate is my calculation from those totals; ASHP's page does not state a rate. [S8]
- Board certification has expanded. The Board of Pharmacy Specialties recognized pharmacy informatics as its 16th specialty in December 2025. [S16]

## Undergraduate (Years 1 to 8: pre-pharmacy and the PharmD)

Routes and length:

- The PharmD is typically four years. Minnesota describes a four-year full-time program of six semesters of mostly classroom and lab instruction followed by a year of rotations. Binghamton describes four years of full-time professional study regardless of educational background. Maryland describes six semesters of coursework before rotations. [E3] [E6] [E8]
- Accelerated three-year programs exist (Research.com mentions the format; a marketing blog says UCSF offers one). Not researched in depth. [E2] [E42]
- Nearly half of applicants held a bachelor's degree or higher, and half of accepted applicants had worked as pharmacy technicians (AACP, fall 2024 to fall 2025 report). [S6]

Prerequisites and admission:

- One pre-pharmacy advising page lists four semesters of chemistry, calculus I, biology, microbiology, and anatomy and physiology. Mercer requires a C or better in science-major courses, prefers a cumulative and science GPA of at least 3.0, requires PharmCAS and two letters of recommendation, and weighs work and extracurricular experience. [E10] [E11]
- Whether physics and statistics are universal prerequisites, and how many years of prerequisites are typical, were not verified.
- The PCAT was retired on January 10, 2024, and no testing dates are offered from the 2024 to 2025 cycle on. Binghamton accepts GRE, MCAT, DAT, GMAT, and LSAT scores but says they are not determining. [E9] [E12]
- Applications rose 8.58% in fall 2024 (36,833 applications from 140 schools) and 6% from fall 2024 to fall 2025. An applicants-per-school average of 259 and an acceptance rate above 80% were reported for the 2024 to 2025 cycle (134 PharmCAS schools). [S6] [S7]
- Total PharmD enrollment was 42,312 in fall 2024, down 4.7% from 44,403. PharmD degrees conferred fell from 12,639 to 11,386 (-9.91%) between 2023 and 2024. The attrition rate rose to 19.7% from 15.3%. Faculty counts rose 3.22% to about 6,400. These come from a repost of AACP's Profile of Pharmacy Students. [S7]

Curriculum (examples from individual schools):

- Washington lists Pharmacotherapeutics I to IV, Chemical and Molecular Pharmacology, Pharmacy Safety and Law, and Population Health and Pharmacy Management. [E5]
- Binghamton lists pharmacotherapeutics, pharmacy management, pharmacoeconomics, public and global health, and interprofessional education, with a skills lab covering evaluating and filling prescriptions, the top 300 drugs, and medication counseling. [E6]
- Colorado lists compounding of dosage forms, acute-care pharmacotherapy, and research courses among its offerings, plus dual-degree, honors, and independent study options. [E7]
- Research.com says the hardest courses are difficult because they require integrating medicinal chemistry, pharmacology, pharmaceutics, and pharmacokinetics rather than memorizing in isolation. It names advanced pharmacokinetics, pharmaceutical biotechnology, and clinical toxicology as hard electives. Its statistics (for example, 23% of students considering changing majors) cite no source and were not relied on. [E1]
- One 2016 graduate described a first semester of 19 lecture hours a week with one to three quizzes or exams most weeks and called the jump from undergraduate the hardest academic transition of their schooling. This is a single account. [E4]

Experiential training:

- Washington runs a "Wednesdays in Practice" series from the start through fall of year three, two 3-week introductory rotations, and eight advanced rotations over 48 weeks. [E5]
- Maryland reports more than 700 preceptors across community pharmacies, hospitals, nursing homes, government agencies, and pharmaceutical companies. [E8]

Licensure:

- NABP first-time pass rates for ACPE-accredited graduates, NAPLEX: 2015 92.0%, 2016 86.0%, 2017 87.0%, 2018 88.2%, 2019 87.0%, 2020 86.2%, 2021 81.7%, 2022 77.3%, 2023 76.4%, 2024 75.9%, 2025 86.8%. MPJE first-time: 2022 74.2%, 2023 72.7%, 2024 75.3%, 2025 74.5%. NABP's table gives no explanation for the 2025 change. [S5]
- An NABP blog reports an average first-time NAPLEX pass rate of 76.08% for 2022 to 2024 and 82.32% for 2017 to 2019. The 2017 to 2019 figure does not match NABP's own table (about 87%); see Uncertainties. [E14]
- The same blog says students do better when they take the NAPLEX within 90 days of graduation and that nearly half of residents who did not complete a residency cited licensure issues. [E14]
- School-level examples: Pitt class of 2023 91.5%; Concordia class of 2024 82%; one Nova Southeastern campus 68.6% for 2024. [E13] [E16] [E18]

Cost and debt:

- Research.com puts PharmD tuition at roughly $30,000 to $50,000 a year. [E38]
- 81.5% of 2026 graduating-survey respondents borrowed for the PharmD. [S17]
- One 2018 graduate account says most pharmacists leave school with well over $100,000 in debt and that $200,000 or more is not uncommon. This is a single, dated account. [E4]

Beyond the classroom (thin evidence):

- Pharmacy technician work is the clearest documented pre-pharmacy experience (half of accepted applicants). A marketing blog says shadowing of about 50 to 100 hours is common. [S6] [E42]
- Not researched: pharmacy student organizations, undergraduate research programs, named summer research programs.

## Master's (Years about 5 to 7)

There is no standard master's stage. The PharmD is the professional doctorate and BLS lists it as the typical entry credential. [S1]

- Master's degrees exist alongside or after the PharmD. Lipscomb lists a Master of Science in pharmaceutical sciences and a master's or graduate certificate in health care informatics, with dual-degree options. Colorado and Maryland also advertise dual degrees. Research.com says master's degrees in pharmaceutical sciences or regulatory affairs often lead to salaries above $90,000, but that page is aimed at non-PharmD pathways and is a low-quality source. How common a master's is, and how it is funded, were not researched. [E17] [E7] [E8] [E38]
- The stage that takes the master's place is postgraduate training, covered next.

Postgraduate training (residency, fellowship, board certification):

- PGY1 residencies focus on pharmacotherapy skills for a broad patient population; PGY2 residencies and academic fellowships are more specialized. Residencies are typically about 80% clinical. [E22]
- PCOM faculty member Samuel John: most hospitals require at least one year of residency training; some institutions require a PGY2 for specialist roles such as critical care or infectious diseases; community and retail generally do not require a residency; some employers require PGY1 or PGY2 for clinical roles. This is one person's account, not posting data. [S9]
- Pitt, class of 2024 (116 reporting): 59% continued to a residency or fellowship, 22% took community or specialty practice positions, and 6% took hospital or long-term-care practice positions. This is one school. [E13]
- A Duquesne survey of 62 of 96 PGY2 internal medicine graduates (2015 to 2019) found all accepted a job within three months and 62% disagreed that they had trouble finding one. It says nothing about whether employers require a residency. [S10]
- ASHP Match 2026 (Phases I and II combined): 4,486 active applicants with a list, 3,787 matched, 699 unmatched. [S8]
- School-reported national Phase I PGY1 averages differ: Binghamton cites 74.2% for 2025; Lipscomb cites 81.17% for 2024. These are school press releases and may use different bases. [E15] [E17]
- Resident stipends: Inova PGY1 $57,600 and PGY2 $61,090 (2026-27); UC San Diego PGY1 $79,344 and PGY2 $85,608 (year not stated). [S11] [S12]
- Fellowships: most are one to two years. Academic fellowships are research-focused (up to 80% research) and usually require PGY1 and PGY2 first, then a year-long fellowship. Industry fellowships sit in companies and cover functions such as drug information, pharmacoeconomics, outcomes research, and clinical research, with rotations across functional areas. Most fellowships are not accredited, unlike most residencies. [E22] [E23]
- Board certification: BPS recognized 16 specialties as of December 2025, with pharmacy informatics the newest. Examples include Pharmacotherapy (BCPS), Oncology (BCOP), Infectious Diseases (BCIDP), and Psychiatric (BCPP). Required experience depends on whether a residency was completed. PCOM faculty say candidates need at least one year of residency or three or more years of practice, and that some employers require certification for clinical roles. [S16] [E24] [E25] [S9]

## PhD and Research (Years about 9 and later)

- Michigan's PhD in Pharmaceutical Sciences: average 5.3 years to defense; accepted students receive a stipend, benefits, fees, and tuition. Year 1 is coursework, research rotations, a pre-candidacy research exam, and choosing an advisor; later years are research, a dissertation, and defense. [S13]
- Wisconsin's program funds students through fellowships, teaching and research assistantships, and training grants, with stipend, full tuition remission, and most health insurance. A preliminary exam is expected before year three, and students then have five years to defend. It requires 51 credits. No typical time to degree is stated. [S14]
- Research areas named by Michigan: chemical and physical integrity of drugs and dosage forms; pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics; drug membrane and barrier crossing and controlled release; nanotechnology and materials-drug biointerfaces; pulmonary delivery; antibody engineering and antibody-drug conjugates; delivery of biologics and immunologic approaches; regulatory science; nucleic acid delivery and therapeutics. [S13]
- Research areas named by Wisconsin: drug discovery (medicinal chemistry, natural products, chemical biology, rational drug design); drug action (pharmacology, toxicology, disease mechanisms); drug delivery (pharmaceutics, formulation, drug targeting, solid-state chemistry, nanopharmacy, pharmacokinetics, molecular imaging). [S14]
- Dual pathways: Lipscomb runs a PharmD-to-PhD pathway into a pharmacological sciences PhD at Vanderbilt. [E17]
- A clinical pharmacy research fellowship is a post-residency program that trains a clinical pharmacist to become an independent investigator. It is not a PhD, a board certification, or a license. [E25]
- Pharmaceutical modeling (PK/PD, PBPK, quantitative systems pharmacology) appears as a career pathway in UConn's list. [E32]
- Faculty: PCOM faculty recommend a residency for faculty careers; a residency director says a research-heavy faculty role needs an academic fellowship. AACP counted about 6,400 faculty in fall 2024. No faculty salary or vacancy figures were found; AACP's Profile of Pharmacy Faculty and its vacancy survey are the places to look. [S9] [E22] [S7] [S15]
- Not researched: how AI and machine learning are used in pharmaceutical-sciences research, pharmacogenomics as a research area, and PhD stipend amounts.

## Tools and AI

- Hospitals use automated dispensing cabinets, robotic IV compounding, RFID inventory, and AI-driven analytics. A Pharmacy Times article reports 87.3% of hospitals use basic analytics (smart pumps, clinical decision support, dispensing and compounding automation) and up to 73% use verification tools that allow remote oversight and low-risk auto-approvals while routing high-alert medications for manual review. [E34]
- A 2025 literature review reports UCSF's robotic preparation system dispensed 350,000 doses without errors (an institution's own report). The review concludes AI shifts pharmacists' attention from dispensing toward patient-care services. [E33]
- Practitioner opinion is split. A Pharmacy Times commentary expects automation and more support staff to reduce retail pharmacist jobs. A 2020 review says chains are moving toward consolidation and automation to raise productivity and profit. A site that predicts dispensing pharmacists could be replaced within seven years gives no method and was not relied on. [E35] [E36] [E43]
- Informatics work covers electronic health records, e-prescribing, and automated dispensing cabinets. [E29] [E31]
- No source quantified AI exposure for pharmacists. The AI exposure lines on the career cards are editorial judgments built on the descriptive evidence above.

## Career Paths

Job titles below are illustrative and were not taken from a source. Salaries are the figures found; where none was found, that is stated.

### 1. Community and retail

- Titles (illustrative): staff pharmacist, pharmacy manager, district manager, independent owner.
- Reality: the largest setting but shrinking. Retail pharmacist employment fell 4.8% in 2025 to about 162,000, the lowest since 2010. The two remaining national chains closed nearly 3,000 locations since 2018, and Rite Aid liquidated. PCOM faculty say community and retail jobs generally do not require a residency. Pay differs by employer type (see salary). [S2] [E19] [S9]
- Salary: $132,940 median at pharmacies and drug retailers; $151,370 at general merchandise retailers; $144,000 at food and beverage retailers (BLS, May 2025). Drug Channels means via Becker's: drugstores and independents $130,900, supermarkets $137,680, mass merchants $143,020. [S1] [S4]
- AI read (editorial): moderate to high on the dispensing side. Robotic dispensing is established and commentary expects reduced retail staffing; counseling, immunizing, and clinical services remain human work. [E35]
- Sources: [S1] [S2] [S4] [S9] [E19] [E35]

### 2. Hospital and health system

- Titles (illustrative): staff pharmacist, clinical pharmacist, pharmacy supervisor, director of pharmacy.
- Reality: the growth setting. Hospital employment rose from about 68,000 in 2010 to nearly 100,000 in 2024, and to about 102,000 in 2025 (+3.0%), as health systems pursue specialty-pharmacy revenue. PCOM faculty say most hospitals require at least one year of residency; that is one account with no posting data behind it. At Pitt, 6% of the class of 2024 went straight into hospital or long-term-care practice. [S2] [E19] [S9] [E13]
- Salary: $157,290 median for hospitals (BLS, May 2025); about $151,000 mean (Drug Channels) or $150,840 (Becker's). [S1] [S2] [S4]
- AI read (editorial): moderate. Verification and inventory are being automated; the clinical-judgment core is not. [E34]
- Sources: [S1] [S2] [S4] [S9] [E13] [E19] [E34]

### 3. Clinical and ambulatory care

- Titles (illustrative): clinical pharmacist, ambulatory care pharmacist, medication therapy management pharmacist, board-certified specialist.
- Reality: patient-facing work managing chronic conditions such as diabetes, asthma, and heart disease in clinics. BLS has stated that demand is projected to increase for pharmacists in some settings, including hospitals and clinics (seen in an earlier excerpt; not rechecked on the current page). PCOM faculty say some employers require PGY1 or PGY2 training and some require board certification for clinical roles. [E31] [S9] [E44]
- Salary: $153,920 median for ambulatory healthcare services (BLS, May 2025; industry-level, not role-specific). Becker's reports $166,820 as the mean at outpatient care centers, the highest setting it lists. [S1] [S4]
- AI read (editorial): low. Clinical judgment and the patient relationship are the protected work.
- Sources: [S1] [S4] [S9] [E31] [E44]

### 4. Industry and medical affairs

- Titles (illustrative): medical science liaison, medical information specialist, drug safety pharmacist, clinical development.
- Reality: pharma and biotech roles in medical information, pharmacovigilance, research and development, clinical trials, regulatory affairs, and sales and marketing. Industry roles often prioritize candidates with extra expertise or research experience, and industry fellowships are one on-ramp. [E29] [E40] [E23]
- Salary: medical science liaison median about $181,000 and pharmaceutical sales about $163,000 (Glassdoor, as quoted by Sermo; self-reported and not verified). Pharmacy Times says industry pharmacists earn $130,000 or more, with references dated 2022. [E27] [E28]
- AI read (editorial): low to moderate. Relationship-building and scientific translation resist automation.
- Sources: [E23] [E27] [E28] [E29] [E40]

### 5. Regulatory and government

- Titles (illustrative): regulatory affairs specialist, FDA reviewer, VA or Public Health Service pharmacist, policy advisor.
- Reality: the U.S. government employs pharmacists at the FDA, VA, Indian Health Service, CMS, Public Health Service, NIH, and the armed forces. Regulatory science centers on making sure medicines and devices are safe, effective, and meet quality standards before distribution. A claim that the candidate pool is narrow comes from a low-quality source. [E29] [E31] [E40]
- Salary: no role-specific source found. All pharmacists: $140,910 median (BLS, May 2025). A BLS industry table for federal government or pharmaceutical manufacturing could not be retrieved. [S1] [S18]
- AI read (editorial): low. Regulatory accountability sits with people.
- Sources: [S1] [S18] [E29] [E31] [E40]

### 6. Managed care and pharmacy benefit management

- Titles (illustrative): managed care pharmacist, formulary manager, prior-authorization clinician, clinical advisor.
- Reality: formulary management, coverage determination and prior authorization, drug-utilization review, clinical advisory work, and specialty pharmacy, at insurers and pharmacy benefit managers. No source on hiring trend, competitiveness, or remote share was found. [E29]
- Salary: no role-specific source found. All pharmacists: $140,910 median (BLS, May 2025). [S1]
- AI read (editorial): moderate. Utilization-review and prior-authorization work is the most automatable part.
- Sources: [S1] [E29]

### 7. Nuclear and specialty pharmacy

- Titles (illustrative): nuclear pharmacist, infusion pharmacist, compounding pharmacist.
- Reality: nuclear pharmacists prepare and dispense radiopharmaceuticals for imaging and treatment at commercial radiopharmacies and hospitals with nuclear medicine, and nuclear pharmacy is a BPS-recognized specialty. Infusion pharmacists prepare sterile medications for patients who cannot take oral drugs; compounding pharmacists prepare patient-specific products. No source on competitiveness was found. [E30] [E31]
- Salary: about $128,952 average base salary, 10th to 90th percentile $99,000 to $147,000 (Payscale, 18 profiles, updated January 27, 2026; a small, self-reported sample). [S19]
- AI read (editorial): low. The work is hands-on and safety-critical.
- Sources: [S19] [E30] [E31]

### 8. Pharmacy informatics

- Titles (illustrative): informatics pharmacist, clinical analyst, medication-safety officer.
- Reality: work on electronic health records, e-prescribing, and automated dispensing cabinets to improve medication safety and efficiency. The Board of Pharmacy Specialties recognized pharmacy informatics as its 16th specialty in December 2025 (announced December 8, published December 11). [E29] [E31] [S16]
- Salary: Salary.com median $149,262, 25th to 75th percentile $142,964 to $157,701 (as of November 1, 2026; an aggregator estimate). [S20]
- AI read (editorial): the work is about selecting and governing these tools, so exposure looks low, but this is inference, not a measured trend.
- Sources: [S16] [S20] [E29] [E31]

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

Everything here is US-specific: the PharmD structure, NAPLEX and MPJE, ASHP residencies, BLS, and AACP data. Other countries use different degrees and licensure.

Regional pay: Becker's reports a 2025 national mean of about $140,180, California $162,340, and Colorado $150,510 (unadjusted for cost of living). Within a state, setting and employer matter more than the single figures shown here. [E20]

Where the first draft of the roadmap said something the sources do not support or contradict, and what changed:

- BLS figures were stale. The first draft said growth of 5% for 2024 to 2034 and about 14,200 openings a year. The current BLS page (last modified October 29, 2026) says 5% for 2025 to 2035 and about 12,500 openings. [S1]
- NAPLEX 2017 to 2019 was shown as about 82%. That figure came from an NABP blog average (82.32%) that does not match NABP's own table (87.0%, 88.2%, 87.0%). The roadmap now uses the table. [S5] [E14]
- The first draft attributed the NAPLEX decline to weaker programs expanding. No source says that, and NABP's table offers no reason for the decline or for the 2025 recovery. The claim was removed. [S5]
- The first draft said applications had fallen sharply, enrollment was down about 15% since 2019, and many schools had open or near-open acceptance. The 15% figure came from a low-quality page. AACP data show applications up in fall 2024 and fall 2025 and acceptance above 80%; enrollment fell 4.7% in fall 2024. [S6] [S7] [E37]
- The first draft said a residency is effectively required for hospital and clinical jobs. The support is one faculty member's statement (PCOM). Pitt's outcomes (6% straight into hospital or long-term-care practice) and BLS (no work experience required) do not contradict it but do not confirm it either. No posting-level data was found. The roadmap now attributes the claim. [S9] [E13] [S1]
- Resident stipend was shown as about $45,000 to $55,000. Two programs checked report PGY1 stipends of $57,600 and $79,344. [S11] [S12]
- Hospital pay was shown as about $120,000 to $145,000. BLS reports a hospital median of $157,290. [S1]
- Retail median was shown as $131,640 (Research.com). BLS reports $132,940 for pharmacies and drug retailers. [S1]
- PharmD debt was shown as $100,000 to $200,000 or more, resting on a 2018 personal blog. AACP's 2026 survey reports only that 81.5% borrowed. [S17] [E4]
- BPS was shown as recognizing 14 specialties (from an undated ACCP page). It recognizes 16 as of December 2025. [S16] [E24]
- Pharmacy faculty shortage and pay gap: this was carried over from the nursing roadmap. No pharmacy source was found, so it was removed. [S15]
- PhD "4 to 6 years, funded via assistantships" is now Michigan's reported 5.3 years and the funding both programs describe. [S13] [S14]
- "National PGY1 match rate 74 to 81%" was built from school press releases. It is replaced by ASHP's 2026 totals. [S8]
- Removed with no source behind them: the Rutgers fellowship model and fellowship competitiveness; MPJE being a separate exam for each state; "five-week" advanced rotations (Washington runs eight over 48 weeks); "top 200/300 drugs" (Binghamton says top 300) and immunization certification; prerequisite length of two to four years and physics and statistics as prerequisites; pre-dawn nuclear shifts; managed care being remote-friendly; the salary ranges for clinical, regulatory, managed care, and informatics roles; "time to license 6 to 8 years".
- Removed from one low-quality page (business20channel.tv) that the first draft leaned on: pharmacist burnout of 78%, the "highest burnout of any healthcare profession" claim, 200 to 300 prescriptions a day, and 35% of pharmacists reaching retirement age by 2030. None was independently verified. [E37]
- Government pay: the first draft said government roles trade lower base pay for benefits. One undated aggregator page says the opposite. Neither is backed by a primary source, so the card makes no claim. [E41]
- Removed research-frontier items with no retrieved source: pharmacogenomics and precision dosing, and AI in drug discovery. They may be real but were not verified. They were replaced with areas named by Michigan and Wisconsin.

Conflicts left unresolved:

- Drug Channels' text (as extracted) says physician offices paid about $167,000, the highest of any setting. Becker's, reading the same analysis, reports $166,820 for outpatient care centers and $145,050 for physician offices. The roadmap uses neither physician-office figure. [S2] [S3] [S4]
- Drug Channels counts about 322,000 pharmacists in May 2025; BLS counts 325,200. The difference is likely scope and method; not checked. [S1] [S2]
- Drug Channels' retail decline for 2023 and 2024 is 13,300 in its 2025 post and 11,700 in its 2026 post, so the series was revised. [E19] [S2]
- School-reported "national average" Phase I PGY1 match rates (74.2% for 2025, 81.17% for 2024) do not agree and are not ASHP figures. [E15] [E17]

Not researched:
