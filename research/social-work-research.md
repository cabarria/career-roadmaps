# Social Work: Career Roadmap Research

Compiled 2026-11-22. Scope: United States unless noted. All sources were accessed on 2026-11-22 (full list with URLs at the end). Source IDs in brackets, for example [S1], point to that list.

Reliability note: primary sources (BLS, ASWB, VA, NASW, university catalogs, HRSA, CSWE-affiliated journal) are used for anchor figures. Many other pages are degree-marketing sites or vendor blogs; they are labeled "secondary" wherever they carry a number, and BLS wins any conflict.

## TL;DR

- The MSW, not the doctorate, is the practice terminal degree. A CSWE-accredited BSW unlocks Advanced Standing, which cuts the master's from about 60 credits to roughly 30 to 45 credits and about one year [S41, S43, S47].
- Licensure gates everything clinical. The LMSW needs an MSW plus the ASWB Master's exam. The LCSW adds roughly 3,000 supervised hours over 2 or more years (state range 2,000 to 6,400) and the ASWB Clinical exam [S50, S51, S52].
- Licensure is changing now: ASWB launched redesigned exams on August 3, 2026 (110 scored questions, three content areas); the Social Work Licensure Compact has passed in 35 states but no multistate licenses exist yet; first-time pass-rate disparities have led some states to drop the exam for some license levels [S3, S4, S5, S53].
- BLS (May 2025): median pay $61,780, 6% growth from 2025 to 2035, about 69,100 openings a year. Specialty medians run from $59,550 (child, family, school) to $71,900 (all other) [S1].
- Doctorates split in two. The PhD is funded and takes 4 to 5 years in the one program with published completion data. The DSW is a 48-credit practice doctorate taking 2 to 3 years, costing roughly $29,000 to $61,000 at the two programs checked [S9, S10, S11, S12].
- AI is already in daily use: nearly two-thirds of surveyed social workers use it, mostly for documentation and correspondence, and two-thirds say ethical guidance is their most pressing need [S64].
- Eight career destinations are covered: clinical practice, healthcare, child welfare, school, military and VA, macro and nonprofit leadership, academia, and corporate/EAP/forensic.

## Key Findings

1. The BSW's real payoff is time and money. Advanced Standing credit counts found: 30 to 33 (several public universities), 32 and 37 (Marist, Westfield State), 45 (Michigan online), against 60 to 66 for traditional MSWs [S41, S47, S48, S49]. Baylor also advertises a five-year BSW plus MSW route [S38].
2. Field education is the profession's signature requirement. CSWE minimums are 400 hours for a BSW and 900 for an MSW; actual programs run 350 to 1,280 hours [S42, S43, S40].
3. Admission to the major itself is gatekept. Programs formally review academic record, readiness, and in some cases "emotional and behavioral stability" before allowing upper-division and field courses [S32, S36].
4. The licensure landscape is moving on three fronts at once: the new exam (August 2026), the Compact (35 states, not yet operational), and state moves to remove or relax the exam after published pass-rate gaps [S3, S5, S53].
5. Doctoral paths differ in funding and purpose. PhDs are typically funded assistantships with tuition waivers; DSWs are online practice doctorates with posted tuition and no funding described on the pages reviewed [S9, S10, S11, S12].
6. The best-documented AI story is practitioner adoption plus an ethics gap, not job loss. NASW's Code of Ethics revision workgroup is recommending supplemental AI guidance; the last technology standards date to 2017 [S64, S65, S66].
7. The VA is the single largest employer: more than 18,000 social workers per NASW (April 2025), though NASW also raised concern about 2025 VA workforce reductions [S18].
8. Pay claims from marketing sites often conflict with BLS. Where they conflict, this file uses BLS and says so.

## Undergraduate (BSW)

### Degree structure

- Total credits: about 120 to graduate at most schools (UConn, Hawaii); UT Austin requires 122. UConn requires 52 credits of social work coursework; Baylor requires more than 40 hours of core social work courses; Utah lists a 65-credit core including the practicum [S35, S36, S38, S39, S33].
- Content areas: practice, policy, research, human behavior, social justice, plus electives in fields such as violence prevention and substance use [S39]. Wisconsin lists aging, family and child welfare, poverty, mental health, developmental disabilities, substance use, race and ethnicity, criminal justice, oppression, and social, economic, and environmental justice [S37].
- Orientation: generalist practice with individuals, families, groups, organizations, and communities, with equal attention to micro, mezzo, and macro practice [S35, S39].
- Pre-major requirements example (Utah): Social Work and Social Welfare, General Psychology, Intro to Sociology, US Government, Intermediate Writing, and a math course, with C- or better in most [S33].

### Field education (hours differ widely)

| --- | --- | --- |
| Texas State | 400 hours, one-semester block internship after a Phase II competency exam | S32 |
| Utah | 400-hour practicum within a 65-credit core; three tracks (generalist, substance use disorder counseling, Title IV-E child welfare) | S33 |
| University of West Florida | Minimum 400 hours plus a senior seminar; 2.5 GPA in major to enter | S34 |
| UT Austin | 480 hours plus 60 service-learning hours on one page, 400 plus 30 on another (likely catalog-year difference) | S36 |

- Unpaid status: none of the program pages reviewed address pay for field placements. The claim that placements are almost always unpaid and controversial is background knowledge, not verified here.
- Background checks: Wisconsin requires a Caregiver Law background check before placement; McGill supports agency criminal-record and health requirements [S37, S40].

### Gatekeeping and hard courses

- Texas State: admissions committee screens academic record and suitability; students must pass a competency exam and meet Field Office requirements; faculty may advise a student to consider another major; "emotional and behavioral stability" is a stated criterion [S32].
- UT Austin: structured review each fall and spring before the practice sequence [S36]. UW-Madison: apply in spring of junior year [S37].
- Minimum grades: UWF needs 2.0 overall and 2.5 in the major; Baylor needs 2.7 overall once accepted; Hawaii needs C or better in knowledge-base courses and 2.5 cumulative [S34, S38, S35].
- Cohort limits: one program in a secondary guide selects about 50 students each February for upper-level coursework [S84].
- UWF will not consider a third placement after two failed attempts absent documented remediation [S34].
- Weeder courses: no source reviewed names a specific notorious course. The idea that Research Methods and Statistics is the surprise course is an editorial inference, though stats and methods are required parts of the major (for example STAT 301 or 371 in Wisconsin's upper-level list) [S37].

### Skills, tools, and extracurriculars

- Verified tools context: the VA is described as a leader in telehealth [S17]. Qualitative software appears in doctoral training (see Tools and AI).
- Not verified in this pass: agency case-management systems (HMIS, SACWIS, CCWIS) and SPSS or Excel as the BSW statistics stack.
- Title IV-E child welfare tracks: Utah offers one; UConn runs a Child Advocacy Center program (stipend $1,000) and a Child Welfare and Protection Track for Spanish-speaking BSW students (stipend or scholarship $2,750) tied to the state DCF [S33, S39].
- Service learning is built into some degrees (UT Austin 30 to 60 hours) [S36].
- Phi Alpha (honor society): at USF, undergraduates need a declared social work major, 9 semester hours of required social work courses, and top 35% class rank; lifetime membership fee $35 ($30 national, $5 local) [S25]. Other benefits claimed in the roadmap (NASW discounts, scholarships) are unverified.
- Language skills: the UConn Spanish-language track is verified; bilingual pay stipends are not.
- Crisis-line and shelter volunteering as an admissions advantage is background knowledge, not verified.

## Master's (MSW)

### Program types and tracks

- Traditional MSW: about 60 credits, two years, open to any bachelor's degree. Advanced Standing: about 30 to 45 credits, about one year, for CSWE-accredited BSW graduates [S41, S47, S48, S49, S50].
- Variants found: Westfield State 66 credits (37 advanced); Marist 64 (32 advanced); Michigan online 60 (45 advanced); BU 65 credits [S47, S49].
- Specializations: clinical or direct practice (the most widely offered), macro (administration, policy, program development, community organizing), and advanced generalist (a micro/macro hybrid) [S45, S46].
- Licensure implication: clinical tracks build in supervision that accelerates LCSW hours; macro graduates can still earn the LMSW, and the ASWB Advanced Generalist exam offers a non-clinical advanced path [S45].
- Named populations and settings: substance use, child and family, gerontology, school, healthcare, military, and criminal justice [S46]. About 20 schools offer military concentrations or on-base placements [S75].
- Dual degrees: BU offers MSW/MPH, MSW/EdM, and MSW/M.Div; other schools pair the MSW with JD, MPH, or public policy degrees [S47, S43].
- School social work often needs a separate state credential (for example California's PPSC-SSW) [S43].

### Cost and funding

- Reported total tuition examples: CSUN in-state full-time $18,592 and one-year advanced standing $12,185; Sacramento State $14,352 in-state, $38,112 out-of-state; Binghamton about $13,950 in-state versus $27,140 out-of-state [S43, S49].
- Per-credit examples: BU online $998; Westfield State $546; LSU $604 [S47, S49]. BU at 65 credits works out to about $64,870 (arithmetic from two sourced figures; BU advanced standing is cheaper).
- Stipends: CalSWEC Title IV-E stipends reach up to $18,500 a year in exchange for working in a qualifying public agency for a period commensurate with the support [S43, S44]. At Case Western Reserve, an HRSA training program pays MSW students a $10,000 stipend; the page lists no post-graduation obligation [S24].
- HRSA's Behavioral Health Workforce Education and Training program funds institutions (accredited master's and doctoral social work programs are eligible) to build internships and field placements; the FY25 page shows no direct student stipend [S23].
- Whether most MSW students self-fund, and how scarce assistantships are, is not quantified by any source reviewed (unverified).

### Advanced training and field hours

- CSWE minimum for MSW is 900 hours; advanced standing programs run roughly 600 to 720 hours or more [S42].
- Examples: Ohio State 248 generalist plus 672 advanced hours; Westfield State 400 plus 500; BU 1,200; UNCW 900; Sacramento State 1,280 [S48, S47, S49, S43].
- Coursework named: assessment of mental disorders, social policy, human behavior (micro and macro), diversity and oppression, advanced interventions, policy analysis, research [S48, S42].
- Clinical methods: the VA trainee program lists biopsychosocial and suicide risk assessment, CBT, and motivational interviewing [S83]. Michigan's online MSW has an integrated health, mental health, and substance use pathway [S49].
- Macro toolkit: needs assessment, fundraising and grant writing, program evaluation, team and project management [S46 (2019 source)].

### Licensure runway

- LMSW: MSW from a CSWE-accredited program plus the ASWB Master's exam; most states require no post-degree hours; at least 20 states use the LMSW title as of November 2025 [S82, S50].
- LCSW: typically about 3,000 supervised post-MSW hours over 2 or more years with at least 100 hours of direct supervision; the reported state range is 2,000 to 6,400 hours; plus the ASWB Clinical exam; CE of 20 to 40 hours per renewal cycle [S50, S51, S52].
- Overall timelines: 6 to 8 years from starting a bachelor's [S50, S51]; one source says 8 to 9 [S52].
- ASWB exam redesign (launch August 3, 2026): three content areas instead of four; scored questions down from 150 to 110 and pretest questions from 20 to 12; more three-option questions; more applied reasoning; four-hour limit, fees, and scheduling unchanged; no statement on scoring or pass rates [S3, S4]. Older figures in secondary guides (150 questions in four areas; a "170-question" clinical exam) predate the change [S50, S51].
- Fees: $260 per attempt as of December 2025 per one secondary source; a retake fee of $230 was cited in 2025 testimony; ASWB says fees did not change with the redesign; 90-day wait between attempts [S52, S53, S4, S82].
- Pass-rate disparities: ASWB data cited by the University of Maryland shows first-time Master's exam pass rates of 93% (white), 55.5% (Black), and 79% (Hispanic) from 2011 to 2021. Maryland's SB 379 (heard February 2025) would drop the exam requirement for BSW and MSW licensure while keeping supervision; NASW says four states have removed the requirement, including Illinois, which reportedly doubled its LSWs in two years. The outcome of SB 379 was not verified [S53].
- Social Work Licensure Compact: 28 states (June 2025), 30 (NASW Spring 2026 issue), 32 (May 18, 2026), 35 (July 7, 2026). As of July 7, 2026, multistate licenses were not yet available; the data system was estimated for Spring 2027 and the Commission cited 18 to 24 months before applications. A May 2026 FAQ had said 9 to 12 months, and one secondary article offers three conflicting timelines (late 2026 to mid-2028). Master's and clinical licenses qualify; provisional and associate licenses do not; one section mentions BSW [S5, S6, S7, S8].

## PhD / Research

### PhD versus DSW

| Feature | PhD | DSW |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Research capacity, interdisciplinary research, leadership in research and higher education | Clinical expertise, non-academic leadership, advancing practice [S54, S55] |
| Length | UW: 4 to 5 years full-time, median 5; 62% of 105 graduates since 1995 finished in 5 years [S9] | Simmons: under 2 years full-time, under 3 part-time, 48 credits; Illinois: 3 years part-time, 48 credit hours [S11, S12] |
| Funding | UW: assistantship or fellowship with tuition waiver, health benefits, half-time salary (20 hours a week), 9-month year; BU: up to five years of tuition, 8-month stipend, health care [S9, S10] | Pages reviewed describe cost only: Simmons $1,265 per credit ($60,720 total, 2026 to 2027); Illinois about $29,000 total [S11, S12]. Marketing sources say some funded DSW programs exist [S56] |
| Format | Full-time; assistantship cap of 50% FTE for the first 4 years at UW [S9] | Online, weekly live classes, working professionals; capstone or action-research project [S11, S12] |
| Admission | UW: a master's degree required (not necessarily an MSW); practice experience "a plus," not required; recent undergraduate statistics course [S9] | Not collected beyond MSW-level practice context |
| Milestones | UW lists first-year comprehensive exam series, general exam (qualifying scholarly paper), dissertation prospectus, dissertation; details not on the FAQ page [S9] | Capstone in the final term [S11, S12] |

- Employment evidence (CSWE/GADE survey, reported in Lee et al., 2023): PhD graduates took tenure-track jobs at R1 (n=32), R2 (n=35), and teaching universities (n=20), plus postdocs (n=20) and other academic roles (n=11); both PhD and DSW graduates were seeking tenure-track roles at teaching universities, showing some competition; external funding is rated more important by PhD programs (mean 3.06) than DSW programs (1.75); the paper describes a gradual tightening of the academic job market [S54].
- Statement in the roadmap that most doctoral students arrive with years of practice, and that going straight through is the exception, is not supported: UW requires a master's but not practice experience [S9].

### Research frontiers

- Grand Challenges for Social Work launched in 2016 with 12 challenges; the 13th (Eliminate Racism) was added in June 2020. Official names: Insuring Healthy Development for Youth; Closing the Health Gap; Building Healthy Relationships to End Violence; Advancing Long and Productive Lives; Eradicating Social Isolation; Ending Homelessness; Creating Social Responses to a Changing Environment; Harnessing Technology for Social Good; Promoting Smart Decarceration; Reducing Extreme Economic Inequality; Building Financial Capabilities and Assets for All; Achieving Equal Opportunity and Justice; and The Grand Challenge to Eliminate Racism [S13, S14].
- Algorithmic accountability in child welfare: the Allegheny Family Screening Tool scores referrals from 1 to 20 using a two-year removal outcome as a proxy for risk and has been used by Allegheny County DHS since 2016; the article describes it as one of about a dozen such tools in use. A 2019 study reported the Black-white screen-in disparity falling from 9% to 7%; an audit of 2016 to 2018 data found the tool would screen in 68% of Black children versus 50% of white children (18-point gap) while workers screened in 51% versus 43% (7-point gap), and hypothesizes that workers reduced disparities by overriding the tool. The tool's recommendations were 51% accurate versus 46.5% for workers, a comparison the author calls misleading [S15].
- Current faculty hiring emphases (evidence of active areas): BU clinical posting cites health and behavioral health, aging and older adults, disability justice, trauma, reproductive health, children and families, and policy and practice innovation for health equity, with strong quantitative skills preferred; Hunter College lists clinical practice and mental health, social welfare policy, immigration and global social work, school-based social work, health and disabilities, and criminal justice; another posting targets college student mental health [S59, S58].
- NIH institutes funding social work faculty projects (UT Austin page 4 of 6): NIA, NIMHD, NIMH, NCI, NIAAA, NIDA, and NICHD. HRSA funds workforce training (S23). ACF, Casey Family Programs, and RWJF as research funders are unverified [S26, S23].
- Implementation science as the hottest methodological frontier is unverified (not searched). Technology and AI scholarship is supported by the Grand Challenge "Harnessing Technology for Social Good" and by AFST audits [S13, S15].

### Academic market and pay

- Tenure-track postings: BU clinical practice $95,000 to $112,000 (posted October 29, 2025); Yeshiva social work policy $70,000 to $105,000; one wiki-reported offer of $91,000 for 10 months with a 2/2 load and $8,000 a year in research funds for 3 years [S59, S60, S57].
- Teaching loads: K-State five classes a year; California State University 4/4 reported by a wiki contributor [S58, S57].
- Non-tenure example: SUNY Brockport MSW instructor $59,266 to $65,505 [S63]. A career-guide estimate puts assistant professors at $60,000 to $90,000 and full professors above $120,000 at well-funded universities (secondary) [S61].
- Practice-experience requirements are posting-specific: one posting at Mercy University requires current licensure and at least two years of practice; K-State targets candidates with direct practice experience [S62, S58]. "Most postings require MSW plus 2 years" is overstated.
- Over 300 CSWE-accredited MSW programs exist (secondary source), supporting steady faculty demand even as tenure lines plateau [S45, S54].

## Tools and AI

### Tools by stage

- Undergraduate: telehealth is central at the VA [S17]; case-management systems and SPSS or Excel are not verified.
- Master's: CBT and motivational interviewing in VA trainee training [S83].
- Doctoral, quantitative: R is taught and promoted (a free online book, Using R in Social Work Research, by Jerry Bean of Ohio State, 2021, aims to encourage R in doctoral training; Michigan's meta-analysis course uses R for all analyses) [S28, S27]. Mplus is the primary tool in Michigan's Applied Structural Equation Modeling course, with R complementary [S27]. Stata is mentioned only as one proprietary alternative [S28]; its actual use is unverified.
- Doctoral, qualitative: UT Austin's doctoral qualitative methods course lists NVivo (installed in the doctoral student room), Dedoose, and Transcriva, and covers ethnography, grounded theory, narrative, case study, phenomenology, and mixed methods [S29].
- Community-based participatory research as a signature method is unverified.

### AI adoption: what practitioners report

- National survey (Moritz Center for Societal Impact with NASW; 1,179 social workers, October 2025 to February 2026): nearly two-thirds use AI in their current role, most often for correspondence, reports, and documentation; use is moving into clinical documentation and client-intervention tools; two-thirds say the most pressing need is ethical guidelines, stronger client protections, and more training [S64].
- NASW's Code of Ethics Revision Workgroup recommends supplemental guidance on AI; the last NASW, ASWB, CSWE, and CSWA technology standards (2017) predate generative AI [S65, S66].
- Ethics framing: disclosure and informed consent (Code of Ethics 1.03 and 1.04(d)), competence (4.01(b)), and self-determination, per a 2026 Journal of Evidence-Based Social Work article on AI-assisted documentation [S67].
- Practical guidance (NASW job board): AI can reduce documentation and administrative burden, but legitimate use stops short of clinical decision-making and needs HIPAA-grade controls; hallucinated citations are a named failure mode [S68].
- Known risks: bias from unrepresentative training data, privacy, and damage to the therapeutic relationship [S66, S67]. Consumer chatbots such as Woebot and Wysa exist [S66]. Woebot shut down its core app (STAT reported this July 2, 2025); founder Alison Darcy cited the cost of FDA marketing authorization and the difficulty of regulating large language models [S31].
- Employment effect: BLS projects 6% growth from 2025 to 2035 [S1]. No reliable source reviewed quantifies AI displacement for social work; vendor claims such as a "0.3% automation risk" and AI-related job growth forecasts come from low-reliability sources [S70, S71].

## Career Paths

Salary anchors use BLS May 2025 unless noted. BLS specialty categories mix license levels and settings.

### 1. Clinical Therapy and Private Practice

- **Titles (junior to senior):** Case Manager, LMSW Clinician, LCSW Therapist, Group Practice Owner or Clinical Director.
- **Reality:** The LCSW (about 3,000 supervised hours, 2 or more years, state range 2,000 to 6,400) gates independent practice, diagnosis, and insurance billing. Clinical social workers are described by NASW as one of the nation's largest groups of mental health service providers (no figures given). The unpaid or underpaid supervised-hours stretch is the bottleneck; first jobs that include qualified supervision matter.
- **Salary:** BLS mental health and substance abuse social workers median $60,280 (all license levels). A secondary estimate puts salaried LCSWs at a $68,000 median with a $42,000 to $105,000 range, and established private practices at $80,000 to $120,000 or more at $120 to $200 per session. That LCSW figure is derived by the vendor, not measured.
- **Sources:** [S1, S50, S51, S52, S30, S69]

### 2. Healthcare and Medical Social Work

- **Titles:** Hospital Social Worker, Hospice or Palliative Social Worker, Dialysis or Transplant Social Worker, Care Manager.
- **Reality:** Healthcare social workers number about 194,800 nationally; hospitals employ 10% and ambulatory healthcare 15% of all social workers. Pay is the highest among BLS's three named specialties. An MSW is the usual expectation; the LCSW adds behavioral-health roles.
- **Salary:** National median $67,880. California healthcare social workers have a reported median of $92,970 (10th percentile $51,720, 90th $141,510), from a secondary source.
- **Sources:** [S1, S73]

### 3. Child Welfare and Family Services

- **Titles:** CPS Investigator, Permanency Caseworker, Foster and Adoption Specialist, Unit Supervisor.
- **Reality:** The largest BLS specialty (about 405,100 workers in child, family, and school social work), with state and local government employing 27% of all social workers. Workforce strain is documented: about 30% annual caseworker turnover (range 20% to 60%), average caseloads near 24 children against recommended 12 to 15, an average time on the job of 1.8 years, and about $54,000 in agency cost per departure. Paperwork reportedly consumes about 4.3 hours of an 8-hour day (2025 federal snapshot, cited by a vendor). Title IV-E stipends can make the degree close to free in return for agency service.
- **Salary:** BLS child, family, and school median $59,550. BSW-level starts in the low to mid $40,000s (secondary). One secondary source cites a $78,890 CPS average, which conflicts with BLS and is not used.
- **Sources:** [S1, S72, S76, S77, S78, S79, S80, S81, S43]

### 4. School Social Work

- **Titles:** School Social Worker, Student Services Coordinator, District Lead Social Worker.
- **Reality:** BLS reports school social workers within the child, family, and school category, so a school-only median was not found. Some states require a credential beyond the MSW (California's PPSC-SSW). Higher-paying states cited by one secondary source for the combined category include New Jersey ($75,590), Connecticut ($71,970), and New York ($70,690); the data vintage of that source is unclear.
- **Salary:** BLS category median $59,550. A secondary source's school-specific $56,680 could not be matched to BLS May 2025 and is probably older.
- **Sources:** [S1, S43, S71]

### 5. Military and Veterans (VA and Federal)

- **Titles:** VA Social Worker, Active-Duty Social Work Officer, Vet Center Counselor, Program Coordinator.
- **Reality:** NASW (April 29, 2025) says more than 18,000 social workers work at the VA, calling it one of the biggest employers of master's-level social workers; VA's own pages call it the largest employer of social workers in the nation and the largest employer of master's-prepared social workers, and say its social work trainee program offers about 1,500 stipends a year. VA social workers appointed after August 14, 1991 must hold a master's-level license for independent practice. NASW raised concern about 2025 VA workforce reductions (no figures given). Programs include mental health, homeless programs, hospice and palliative care, primary care (PACT), and suicide prevention.
- **Salary:** 2026 GS base pay (before locality): GS-9 $52,727 to $68,549; GS-11 $63,795 to $82,938; GS-12 $76,463 to $99,404; locality adjustments run 15% to 40% or more in high-cost areas (secondary guides citing the federal tables). Active-duty O-1 base pay is about $45,914 a year and O-3 about $85,594, before untaxed housing and subsistence allowances (secondary).
- **Sources:** [S16, S17, S18, S74, S75]

### 6. Macro: Policy, Advocacy, and Nonprofit Leadership

- **Titles:** Community Organizer, Program Manager, Policy Analyst, Executive Director.
- **Reality:** BLS social and community service managers typically need a bachelor's degree in social work or a related field, with some roles requiring a master's, and are projected to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035 (about 16,600 jobs). Executive director pay is wide and depends on organization size. Funding is grant- and budget-cyclical (background, unverified).
- **Salary:** Managers' median $80,390 (10th percentile $53,150, 90th $132,260). Nonprofit executive directors: PayScale average $68,809 (10th to 90th percentile $40,000 to $116,000, 43 profiles, self-reported); IRS Form 990 data for 18,769 human-services nonprofits (2020 to 2024) shows median total compensation of $89,368, $97,792 for organizations with $1 million to $3 million in revenue (25th to 75th percentile $71,970 to $128,738), and $150,001 at the 75th percentile overall. A marketing source gives policy analysts $80,000 to $110,000 in metro areas.
- **Sources:** [S2, S21, S22, S85, S71]

### 7. Academia and Research

- **Titles:** Doctoral Student, Research Scientist or Program Evaluator, Assistant Professor, Associate and Full Professor.
- **Reality:** A funded PhD leads to R1 and R2 roles; DSW graduates increasingly compete for teaching-focused lines. New tenure lines have plateaued and non-tenure and adjunct work is common. Practice experience is required by some postings, not all.
- **Salary:** Tenure-track postings seen ran $70,000 to $112,000 for 9 to 10 months; non-tenure instructors about $59,000 to $66,000; full professors above $120,000 at well-funded schools (secondary).
- **Sources:** [S54, S59, S60, S57, S61, S62, S63]

### 8. Corporate, EAP, and Forensic

- **Titles:** EAP Counselor, Workplace Wellbeing Lead, Forensic Social Worker, Mitigation Specialist.
- **Reality:** Employee Assistance Program counselors provide short-term workplace counseling. Mitigation specialists build life-history investigations in death penalty defense; the Missouri State Public Defender posting requires a master's degree in psychology, social work, sociology, or a closely related field (not specifically a social work license). The claim that tech trust-and-safety teams recruit social workers is unverified.
- **Salary:** EAP counselor average $86,138 (10th to 90th percentile $60,000 to $129,000; 86 profiles; updated June 26, 2026; self-reported and not specific to social workers). Forensic social work about $57,600 (secondary, 2024). The Missouri mitigation specialist posting lists $4,188 a month (about $50,256 a year, annualized by arithmetic). The roadmap's statement that these paths pay above agency scale is not supported for forensic or mitigation work.
- **Sources:** [S20, S19, S70]

### Source conflicts

- UT Austin's BSW page lists two hour totals (480 plus 60, and 400 plus 30), likely different catalog years [S36].
- A secondary vendor places the national median near $68,930 and the average LCSW near $83,000; BLS reports $61,780. BLS is used [S71, S70, S1].
- California healthcare median: $88,380 (one secondary source) versus $92,970 (another) [S71, S73].
- CPS salary: BLS-aligned figures near $54K to $60K versus a $78,890 average [S77, S81].

### Regional variation

- Licensure titles and rules differ by state: LICSW (Massachusetts), LCSW-C (Maryland), LISW, CMSW, and others; some states have no LMSW tier; Rhode Island regulates only clinical licenses; Louisiana renews annually; California registers associates (ASW) with its Board of Behavioral Sciences before counting hours, and most California social workers need no license [S82, S51, S86, S87].
- Pay varies by setting and region. Secondary sources cite higher medians in California, New Jersey, Connecticut, New York, Washington, Rhode Island, and Washington DC, but these figures conflict with BLS-level numbers and were not verified against BLS state tables [S71, S73].
- Outside the US, degree structure and field hours differ (McGill's BSW requires 350 hours of concurrent placement) and none of the licensure or salary data here applies [S40].
