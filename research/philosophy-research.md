## TL;DR

- The major is organized around four areas: logic and formal methods, history of philosophy, ethics and value theory, and metaphysics and epistemology. The programs checked require 8 to 12 courses, most require a logic course, and most end in a seminar, thesis, or independent project (S1 to S10).
- The master's is mostly a funded bridge into PhD applications, but some programs serve students headed to teaching, law school, or government work. Funded MA stipends in the programs checked run from about $9,751 to $25,200 a year. Not every "funded" program is fully funded: Tufts averages about 60% tuition remission and Brandeis lists no amounts (S20 to S22).
- The PhD is funded for five to seven years at the programs checked, with stipends of $28,000 (Loyola Chicago), $31,981 (DePaul), and at least $43,050 (Penn, 2025-26). It ends in a book-length dissertation (S11 to S19).
- For PhDs graduating from 2015 on, APDA's coded data show 40.5% in permanent academic jobs (31.9% tenure-track or equivalent), 41.9% in temporary academic work, and 17.6% outside academia (S25). The older figure "approaches 30%" applies to graduates of top-15 programs reaching permanent posts at research-focused universities in 2012-2016 data, not to PhDs generally (S26).
- BLS counts 26,800 postsecondary philosophy and religion teaching jobs, a $80,260 median wage (May 2025), and 2% projected growth (about 500 jobs) from 2025 to 2035 (S28). APDA's 2023 survey averages were $111,564 for non-academic jobs, $81,507 for permanent academic jobs, and $51,314 for temporary academic jobs (S23).
- Satisfaction is no different between permanent academics and PhDs outside academia, and both exceed temporary academics (S25).
- Philosophy majors ranked second behind economics on the LSAT among majors with more than 2,000 test takers in 2017-18 (157.2 against 158.9) (S31). LSAC warns that major averages do not show that a major prepares students better (S54).
- AI governance is a new field, but the evidence that philosophers are favored for it is thin. IAPP's 2025 report does not mention philosophy, and puts AI governance mostly with legal and compliance (22%) and IT (17%) (S39). A widely repeated "30% of hires come from philosophy" figure traces to one aggregator with no source (S41).
- Three figures common in career guides could not be verified and are not used: a 3.2% unemployment rate attributed to the New York Fed (S37, S38), "mid-career median $81,000" attributed to Georgetown (S37), and a PayScale "$100,100" (S37). The $81,200 mid-career figure appears on a university page citing a 2008 PayScale release (S35).

## Key Findings

- **PhD placement (APDA), several cuts.** All figures are shares of graduates. APDA is Academic Philosophy Data and Analysis, which tracks graduates of roughly 150 English-language programs.

| Cut | Permanent academic | Temporary academic | Non-academic | Unknown | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2013-2023 graduates (5,998), 2023 post | 40% (2,379) | 39% (2,324) | 12% | 10% | S23 |
| 2013-2023 graduates (6,407), 2024 update as restated in 2026 | 41% | 38% | 12% | 10% | S24 |
| 2014-2024 graduates (6,314), February 2026 update | 40% | 34% | 17% | 10% | S24 |
| 2015+ graduates, coded data (5,521 outcomes), July 2026 post | 40.5% (tenure-track or equivalent 31.9%, permanent lecturer or similar 8.6%) | 41.9% | 17.6% | not shown | S25 |

- **Placement by graduation year.** Permanent academic employment was 55% for 2013 graduates and averaged 30% for 2019 to 2023 (37% in 2019, 26% in 2023). The 2023 post attributes part of this to the years it takes many graduates to find permanent work (S23). Excluding the last couple of years, tenure-track jobs stayed near 20% and permanent lectureships near 6%, while temporary academic jobs fell from 41% to 29% and non-academic jobs rose from 10% to 20% (S23, years not specified).
- **Prestige and time to job.** In the coded data, the academic share was 86.3% for graduates of programs ranked 1 to 20 in the Philosophical Gourmet Report, 83.0% for 21 to 50, and 80.1% for unranked programs. Mean time from graduation to a first tenure-track job was about 1.4 years in all three groups, with a maximum of 8, within a dataset limited to 2015 to 2025 graduates (S25).
- **Preference.** In the 2025 survey, 961 respondents preferred academic work, 105 had no strong preference, and 66 preferred non-academic work (S25). An earlier survey found 82% preferred academic jobs (S23).
- **Pay by job type (APDA 2023 survey averages).** Non-academic $111,564, permanent academic $81,507, temporary academic $51,314, with gaps of about $30,000 between each type. Respondents answering "no" to being able to access $1,000 in an emergency were 1% of non-academic, 5% of permanent academic, 13% of temporary academic, and 24% of current PhD students (S23).
- **Satisfaction.** Non-academic and permanent academic jobs showed equivalent satisfaction in 2023, and non-academic sectors including technology, law, consultancy, and nonprofit or NGO scored higher than permanent academic jobs (S23). The July 2026 analysis found no significant difference between academics and non-academics, none between permanent academics and non-academics, and significantly higher satisfaction for permanent than for temporary academics (p<0.0001). The post reports no means (S25).
- **Admission.** About 6% at Michigan (about 256 applicants a year, typically 10 to 16 admits), 8% at UCLA, and 4% to 5% at Yale (more than 300 applicants). These are 2018-19 figures from a 2019 article. The same article puts completion near 75% across six top programs and calls that "very uncertain" (S26).
- **Placement odds at the top.** The 2019 article says the chance of a permanent post at a research-focused university "approaches 30%" for graduates of top-15 programs, from APDA data for 2012 to 2016 that the authors call highly approximate, and that about 25% of top-program graduates take jobs at non-PhD-granting institutions (S26). A 2022 analysis found about 46% of PhDs since 2000 in tenure-track or permanent jobs and about 8% tenured, though it reports initial and current placement differently from APDA (S27, low).
- **Timeline.** Five to seven years of PhD, two to three years of postdocs or short-term jobs, then five to nine years on the tenure track before tenure (S26). Research share of time can exceed 50% at research universities and fall below 20% at teaching-oriented ones (S26).
- **Academic pay.** BLS puts the May 2025 median for postsecondary philosophy and religion teachers at $80,260, against $85,330 for all postsecondary teachers (S28). One tenure-track bioethics posting at CU Boulder listed $85,000 to $90,000 with a 2-2 teaching load (S29). Aggregator figures conflict by a factor of more than two and are not used (S30).
- **LSAT (LSAC 2017-18).** Mean scores and test takers: economics 158.9 (2,757), philosophy 157.2 (2,238), history 156.3 (3,138), engineering 156.2 (974), English 154.8 (3,151), political science 153.6 (11,947), psychology 152.5 (3,736). The APA document calls philosophy second behind economics among fields with more than 2,000 test takers (S31). A 2022 department page, citing Daily Nous, says philosophy tied with economics at an average of 159 (S32, secondhand). A 2014-15 table shows many small majors above philosophy, including astronomy (165.83), statistics (162.33), and physics (162.02) (S33), so "top" applies to large majors.
- **Funded MA terms.** See the table in the Master's section (S20).
- **AI governance.** IAPP's 2025 survey (more than 670 respondents, 45 countries) found only 10 of 671 said they would not need additional staff in the next 12 months. Legal and compliance held primary AI governance responsibility in 22% of organizations and IT in 17%, with ethics and compliance at 6%. The report does not give education backgrounds and does not mention philosophy (S39). IAPP's 2025-26 salary report (more than 1,600 respondents, 60+ countries) found half of AI governance respondents earn less than $151,800, half of privacy respondents less than $123,000, and half of combined privacy and AI governance respondents more than $169,700. Technical AI governance roles in the technology sector had a median of $221,000. It does not break pay down by seniority or say whether figures are base or total pay (S40).
- **Clinical ethics.** A 2023 survey of 133 clinical ethics consultants found full-time averages of $188,310 for clinical doctorate holders, $146,134 for nonclinical doctorate holders, and $113,625 for master's holders, with pay differences by degree not significant among full-time ethicists. About a quarter of respondents were uncompensated, 56% of clinical doctorate holders against 13% of nonclinical doctorate holders (S45).

## Undergraduate

### Curriculum

- **Four areas.** Utah organizes the major around Ethics and Values, Metaphysics and Epistemology, History of Philosophy, and Logic and Formal Methods, requires courses from each area, 36 units, at least three courses at the 5000 level, and a Senior Seminar that satisfies the advanced writing requirement (S1). Princeton divides courses into metaphysics, ethics and value, logic and philosophy of science, and history, requires eight courses spread over at least three areas, and adds a junior independent-work group, a spring junior paper, and a senior thesis (S7, S8).
- **Required sequence.** Columbia requires History I (ancient or medieval), History II (early modern), Symbolic Logic, one course in metaphysics, epistemology, language, science, or phenomenology, one in social and political philosophy, ethics, or aesthetics, and a major seminar. Students need four philosophy courses before a 4000-level course (S2). Yale requires 12 courses: three in history (including two intro survey courses), two in metaphysics and epistemology, two in ethics and value, one in logic (preferably by the fall of junior year), two seminars at the 3000 level or above, and a senior requirement (S10). NYU requires ten four-credit courses including Logic, Ancient, Early Modern, an ethics or values course, an epistemology, metaphysics, or science course, and a mind or language course (S5).
- **Size and logic.** Berkeley requires at least 12 courses and offers a logic minor whose core courses can be taken in Philosophy or Mathematics tracks (S6). MIT lists logic subjects and lets students use a logic course from another department such as Mathematics (S4). FSU requires 30 semester hours including one logic course, ancient and modern history, ethics, and contemporary metaphysics and epistemology (S9). FIU requires one logic course and 3 hours of non-Western philosophy, and offers a specialized track for law, religion, or psychology (S3).
- **Pairings built into programs.** Yale has a psychology concentration (7 philosophy and 5 psychology courses) (S10). Princeton has political philosophy, philosophy of science, and philosophy and linguistics thesis options (S7, S8). NYU has a cognitive science major shared with linguistics and psychology (S5). USC has a Philosophy, Politics and Law major (S34, low). MIT has a linguistics and philosophy track (S4). About a third of philosophy students at ETSU double major (S36, low).
- **Capstone.** Utah requires a Senior Seminar (S1). Princeton requires a senior thesis (S7). Yale's senior requirement is a third seminar or a one- or two-term independent project (S10). NYU has honors proseminar and thesis workshops (S5).

### Skills

- Princeton's department statement says philosophy is distinguished by a commitment to reasoned argument and the evidence of experience, without appeal to tradition or authority, and by being prepared to listen to arguments from the other side. It says courses in all areas teach students to write without advocating a view without an argument for it (S7).

### Tools and extracurriculars

- Many departments run Intercollegiate Ethics Bowl teams, including Boston College, Oklahoma, Vanderbilt, William and Mary, Tufts, Indiana, UC Santa Cruz, and UNC Greensboro (S51, department pages seen as a search listing).
- 80,000 Hours advises writing an original philosophy paper before graduate school, as a term paper, thesis, or in spare time, polishing it to 4,000 to 10,000 words (the length of a journal article), and asking professors for honest feedback (S26).
- No source reviewed names standard software for undergraduate coursework. Logic proof software was not researched.

### Not found

- Which philosophy courses are hardest. The first JSX named Kant, symbolic logic proofs, and seminar cold-calling as the hard parts without a source, so those claims were removed. The "Hard Parts" section now lists structural gates that curricula document: the logic requirement and its mathematical character, gated advanced seminars, independent work, and the department's stated writing standard.
- Undergraduate philosophy journals and essay prizes. The first JSX named them without a source.
- Reliable pre-med data. The only support for "strong med-school acceptance rates" was a university news page (S36, low), so the claim was removed.

## Master's

- **Types and purposes.** The APA lists 127 graduate programs offering a terminal master's (S21, 2022). Faculty and alumni comments there give three purposes. First, preparation for PhD applications: NIU and Virginia Tech describe writing seminars and feedback on writing samples, and Simon Fraser says its MA helps students decide whether academic philosophy suits them. Second, credentials for other work: CUNY's MA prepares students to teach at community colleges or secondary schools, SF State serves students who want to teach, advance, or change careers, and Montana reports placing students in law school and in government and nonprofit work. Third, funding varies, with many programs funding through teaching assistantships and others, such as CUNY and Texas A&M in 2022, offering little or none (S21).
- **Outcomes.** NIU's faculty report that 80 of 90 MA graduates (89%) who applied to PhD programs over the past decade were admitted, with full funding. Georgia State reports a graduation rate above 90% for every cohort since 2015. These are self-reports in a comment thread (S21, low). Brandeis lists PhD enrollments for application years 2010-11 to 2022-23, including Harvard, Berkeley, Brown, Stanford, Chicago, MIT, Oxford, UNC, and UCLA, plus law schools, but gives no totals (S22).
- **Funded MA terms** (as listed on a program-tracking page updated October 2, 2025, which warns that many entries are over five years old and advises checking with each program) (S20):

- **Applied and professional routes.** FSU offers a graduate certificate in bioethics (S17). Master's-level clinical ethics consultants averaged $113,625 in full-time work in a 2023 survey (S45). No source on funding or outcomes for standalone bioethics or public-policy master's programs was found.
- **Languages and logic.** Boston College requires proficiency in two foreign languages for the PhD (S12). DePaul requires competence in two languages and spends the spring of year two abroad on language study (S15). Loyola Chicago's MA has no language or research-tool requirement, while its PhD has one (S13). Syracuse has no program-wide language requirement (S18). Harvard sets a logic requirement to be met by the beginning of year four (S14), and Columbia gives an optional logic proficiency exam (S11).
- **Breadth versus focus.** Loyola Chicago's MA requires courses in five of seven distribution areas, including contemporary analytic philosophy, contemporary continental philosophy, ancient, medieval, and modern philosophy (S13). FSU offers a philosophy of science concentration within its MA (S17).
- **Not found.** Earnings or placement data for standalone MAs beyond program self-reports, and tuition for unfunded programs.

## PhD / Research

- **Structure.** Columbia: coursework in years one and two, then the M.Phil. (all requirements except the dissertation) by the end of year four, then the dissertation in year five and possibly six and seven, with a dissertation proposal defense as the final M.Phil. requirement (S11). Boston College: 16 courses (48 credits), two languages, five funded years (four with an MA), and requirements finished within eight years (S12). Loyola Chicago: 48 hours of coursework, a foundational paper and oral defense in fall of year three, and a candidacy exam on a dissertation proposal with a first chapter or extended literature review (S13). Harvard: 12 courses, a second-year paper, a logic requirement, and a topical exam, with all requirements except the dissertation done by the start of year five (S14). Cornell: 12 courses, an A exam (oral, on the prospectus) before the seventh semester, a B exam (dissertation defense), and no qualifying exam (S19). Syracuse: 12 three-credit courses plus 18 dissertation credits, a special paper before year three, and a book-length dissertation (S18). DePaul: 28 four-credit courses over four years, with candidacy within four years of admission and the dissertation eight months to two years after candidacy (S15).
- **Funding.** Columbia gives six-year packages (S11). Penn gives a five-year package with a stipend of at least $43,050 from 2025-26, with teaching in years two, three, and five (S16). DePaul gives six years with a stipend of $31,981 (S15). Loyola Chicago's stipend is $28,000 over nine months (S13). FSU offers five years with an MA and seven without (S17). Boston College funds five years (S12).
- **Teaching.** Harvard requires a year-long pedagogy seminar in the first year a student teaches (S14). DePaul has a teaching practicum in years two and three (S15). Boston College runs a professional development curriculum and a teaching seminar (S12).
- **Job market.** See Key Findings (S23 to S27). Doctoral training in Germany and some other places is often tied to a grant project and not to a department cohort, and Europeans spend more time in postdocs than North Americans (S24).
- **Research areas.**
  - AI and machine learning: a 2025 review argues new ethical theories of distributed agency, where responsibility is shared among designers, firms, and software, can advance AI ethics (S42). MIT Sloan authors quoted in an industry piece say philosophical perspectives on what AI models should achieve, what counts as knowledge, and how they represent reality shape value creation, beyond ethics alone (S44, low).
  - Formal epistemology: the Stanford Encyclopedia entry (first published 2015, revised 2021) describes using probability theory, modal logic, and computer simulation on questions such as confirming scientific theories and the problem of induction (S48). Course catalogs list Foundations of Probability, Rationality, and Rational Choice at MIT, Inductive Logic and Rational Choice Theory at Utah, and Philosophy and Probability at FIU (S1, S3, S4).
  - Experimental philosophy: the Stanford Encyclopedia entry (published April 8, 2026) describes a methodological naturalist approach using vignette questionnaires, corpus studies, and behavioral, qualitative, and brain-imaging methods to test empirical assumptions in philosophical theorizing (S47).
  - Consciousness: in 2025 an adversarial collaboration tested two leading consciousness theories and found no winner (S50, snippet), and researchers writing in Frontiers in Science argued that AI and neurotechnology make explaining consciousness urgent (S49).
  - Canon: FIU requires a non-Western philosophy course (S3), Loyola Chicago lists feminism and critical philosophy of race among its strengths (S13), and APDA program keywords include "Race" for five programs in 2025 (CUNY, Penn State, Memphis, Penn, Emory) (S24).
  - APDA's keyword data describe how survey participants characterized their programs, not research topics. Between 2023 and 2025, Political/Social rose from 3% to 5% of mentions, Analytic from 11% to 12%, and Technology from 0% to 1%, while Race fell from 1.4% to 0.8% and Logic/Formal from 5% to 4% (S24).

## Tools and AI

- **Citation and typesetting.** Philosophers in a 2025 comment thread named Zotero and LaTeX with BibLaTeX for citation management (S46).
- **AI in research and teaching.** The same thread (a self-selected set of reader comments, not a survey) shows enthusiasts using AI for teaching materials, editing, and as a sounding board for objections, cautious users restricting it to narrow tasks such as reformatting a bibliography or practice referee reports, and non-users who reject it on ethical grounds or doubt its writing. Several raised hallucinated citations, including a thesis bibliography with invented references. Tools named were ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, NotebookLM, and Perusall (S46).
- **AI as a subject.** See Research areas above (S42 to S44). A December 2025 review of trustworthy language models lists the EU AI Act, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and ISO/IEC 42001 among current governance frameworks (S43, snippet).
- **Formal tools.** Probability theory, modal logic, and simulation for formal epistemology (S48).
- **Not found.** A standard software stack for philosophers, logic proof software, and any survey of AI use among philosophers. PhilPapers and PhilPeople were not researched.

## Career Paths

### 1. Academia

- **Titles:** Adjunct or lecturer, visiting assistant professor, postdoctoral fellow, assistant professor (tenure-track), associate professor, full professor.
- **Reality:** A PhD from 2015 onward has about a 40% chance of a permanent academic job and about 42% of temporary academic work (S25). BLS counts 26,800 philosophy and religion teaching jobs and projects 2% growth through 2035 (S28). Permanent academics are more satisfied than temporary ones, and no more satisfied than PhDs outside academia (S25). Most PhDs (about 85% in the 2025 survey) prefer academic work (S25).
- **Salary:** $80,260 median for postsecondary philosophy and religion teachers (BLS, May 2025). APDA survey averages (2023): $51,314 temporary academic, $81,507 permanent academic. A 2025 tenure-track bioethics posting listed $85,000 to $90,000.
- **Sources:** S23, S25, S28, S29.

### 2. Law and legal practice

- **Titles:** Paralegal, law student (JD), associate attorney, counsel, partner.
- **Reality:** Philosophy ranked second behind economics among large majors on the LSAT in 2017-18, with LSAC cautioning that major averages do not show a major prepares students better. Practicing requires a JD (three years) and a bar exam. NALP's Class of 2025 had a median starting salary of $100,000 and 92.8% employment, with sharp differences by employer. Non-academic PhDs in law reported higher satisfaction than permanent academics in 2023.
- **Salary:** NALP Class of 2025 medians: $225,000 at firms of 500 or more lawyers, $84,000 at firms of 1 to 10, $81,000 in government, $75,000 in public interest, $100,000 overall. BLS lawyers median $159,670 (May 2025).
- **Sources:** S31, S54, S53, S23.

### 3. AI ethics and governance

- **Titles:** Trust and safety analyst, AI policy analyst, responsible AI lead, AI ethics officer. These come from low-reliability role lists and are representative, not verified in primary data.
- **Reality:** A new field. IAPP's 2025 survey found AI governance sitting mostly with legal and compliance (22%) and IT (17%), ethics and compliance primary at 6%, with no mention of philosophy backgrounds and a statement that qualified people need to understand AI. Nearly all of 671 respondents expected to need more staff (the fetched summary did not state which staff, so read the report before quoting this).
- **Salary:** Median $151,800 for AI governance only and $221,000 for technical AI governance roles in tech (IAPP 2025-26, global, not by seniority). Entry-level pay was not found.
- **Not used:** An aggregator's claim that backgrounds split "technology 40%, philosophy and ethics 30%, law 30%" and its "98.5% talent gap" (a relabeling of IAPP's 1.5% who need no staff), a "300% growth" claim from a career guide, and $120,000 to $180,000 ranges (S41, S37, low).
- **Sources:** S39, S40, S41, S37.

### 4. Consulting and strategy

- **Titles:** Analyst, consultant, strategy manager, corporate strategist.
- **Reality:** BLS lists a bachelor's as the typical entry degree for management analysts, the closest official category, so the path does not require graduate school. BLS pay covers the whole occupation, with the top 10% above $171,640. Non-academic PhDs in consultancies reported higher satisfaction than permanent academics. Top-firm hiring routes and pay were not researched.
- **Salary:** Management analysts median $101,860, 10th to 90th percentile $60,640 to $171,640 (BLS, May 2025).
- **Sources:** S55, S23.

### 5. Policy, nonprofit, and public sector

- **Titles:** Research assistant, policy analyst, program manager, director or advisor. Representative titles, not from a philosophy-specific source.
- **Reality:** Policy analyst roles at agencies and think tanks generally prefer a master's in public policy or economics (S58). BLS lists a master's as typical for its small "political scientist" category (about 6,100 jobs, a 2% projected decline) (S56). Nonprofit and NGO work was among the sectors where non-academic PhDs reported higher satisfaction than permanent academics (S23). No source describes a philosophy-specific route.
- **Salary:** Policy analyst entry pay is quoted around $55,000 to $75,000 on aggregator pages that conflict with each other (S58, low). BLS political scientists median $142,080 (May 2025, small occupation).
- **Sources:** S56, S58, S23.

### 6. Writing, media, and communications

- **Titles:** Editor, technical writer, content strategist, communications lead.
- **Reality:** BLS projects flat or falling employment through 2035: writers and authors 0%, editors -1%, technical writers 1%. Practitioners report AI now produces first drafts in technical writing and writers shift toward review (see the English research file for the sourcing).
- **Salary (BLS, May 2025):** Writers and authors $76,910 median (bottom 10% under $44,310), editors $77,920 (under $41,250), technical writers $90,390 (under $57,440). Top 10% above $139,870, $153,700, and $145,270 respectively.
- **Sources:** S54.

### 7. Bioethics and clinical ethics

- **Titles:** Clinical ethics fellow, clinical ethics consultant, assistant professor of bioethics.
- **Reality:** A defined professional track, though small and uneven. In a 2023 survey of 133 consultants, most worked full time, others held ethics as part of another job, and about a quarter were uncompensated, mostly clinical doctorate holders doing consults beside patient care. Respondents held master's or clinical or nonclinical doctorates, and nonclinical doctorate holders tended to have more formal ethics training, such as bioethics degrees or fellowships. The article does not state what employers require.
- **Salary:** Full-time averages $113,625 (master's), $146,134 (nonclinical doctorate), $188,310 (clinical doctorate), with differences not significant among full-time ethicists (2023 survey). A 2025 tenure-track bioethics posting listed $85,000 to $90,000.
- **Sources:** S45, S29.

### 8. Data, tech, and product

- **Titles:** UX researcher, product analyst, product manager, founder.
- **Reality:** No source shows a philosophy-specific route into product or research roles, so expect to compete on skills the major does not teach. Reid Hoffman (LinkedIn) has a master's in philosophy from Oxford and Stewart Butterfield (Slack) studied philosophy (S52, news profiles, seen as a search listing). Non-academic PhDs in technology reported higher satisfaction than permanent academics (S23).
- **Salary:** Entry associate product managers average about $77,044 base, with $55,000 to $108,000 total pay (Payscale, March 2025). One large employer, Meta, averages about $174,000 total at the entry level (Levels.fyi, 2026). Mid-career and senior pay were not researched for this field. Figures come from the business administration research file.
- **Sources:** S57, S23, S52.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **US focus.** Curricula, BLS, NALP, and the funding figures are US data. APDA covers English-language programs worldwide, and its permanent-job shares mix countries (S24). IAPP salary figures are global (S40). A UK PhD is typically 3 to 4 years after a master's (S26), and German doctoral students are often tied to grant projects (S24).
- **Censoring.** Recent graduates are still searching, so placement shares for the latest cohorts understate eventual permanent placement (S23). The 1.4-year time to a first tenure-track job covers only 2015 to 2025 graduates and only those who got one (S25).
- **Definitions differ across sources.** APDA's "permanent academic" includes tenure-track and permanent lectureships (S25). The 2022 analysis counts tenure-track and tenured separately and uses different cohorts (S27).
- **Self-reported figures.** NIU's 89% and Georgia State's graduation rate are department self-reports in comments (S21). The APDA survey is voluntary, and the 2026 post reports unreconciled counts across its tables (S25).
- **Dated figures.** The admission rates and the "approaches 30%" figure are from a 2019 article (S26). The funded-MA page warns many entries are over five years old (S20). Wayne State and other older stipends were not used.
- **Aggregators conflict.** Philosophy professor pay: Glassdoor average $172,365 from 8 salaries, Salary.com assistant professor $69,610, ZipRecruiter $116,801, Comparably $134,264 (S30). None is used.
- **Unverified.** The New York Fed unemployment figure, the Georgetown mid-career median, and the PayScale 2026 figure (S37, S38). University pages quoting top GRE and GMAT scores for philosophy majors (S34, S35) were not checked against ETS or GMAC data.
- **Not researched.** Top-tier consulting pay and hiring, philosophy-specific policy routes, UK and European labor markets, and logic software.
