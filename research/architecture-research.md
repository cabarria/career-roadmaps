# Architecture: Research Notes for the Roadmap

Prepared for the Architecture career-roadmap artifact (`artifacts/architecture-roadmap.jsx`). Every figure below traces to a source listed at the end. Where the first draft of the artifact said something the sources do not support, that is recorded under Uncertainties.

Version 1. The first draft of the roadmap was built from eight search passes in mid-2026 and no saved research file. This version adds a verification pass on 2026-11-23 (24 lookups: 8 searches, which returned links only, and 16 page fetches, of which 13 returned usable pages; one URL was refused by the fetch allowlist and then fetched under a different capitalization, one hit a bot-verification wall, and one failed) and replaces or removes first-draft claims the sources did not support.

Access dates: sources marked "fetched" were retrieved on 2026-11-23 through a fetch tool that returns an extract of the page, not the raw text, so details beyond the extract were not checked. Sources marked "excerpt" were seen only as search-result excerpts during the original build session (mid-2026; the exact day was not recorded). BLS pages fetched on 2026-11-23 carry newer data (May 2025 wages, 2025 to 2035 projections) than the BLS excerpts seen in the original session (May 2024 wages, 2024 to 2034 projections), so the roadmap now uses the newer figures.

## TL;DR

- Architecture has no bachelor's-then-master's arc. The accredited professional degree is a five-year B.Arch, a four-year pre-professional bachelor's followed by a roughly two-year M.Arch (the "4+2"), or a three-to-four-year M.Arch for someone whose bachelor's is in another field. NAAB lists 180 accredited programs and 10 candidate programs at 152 institutions. Most of the 55 licensing boards require a NAAB-accredited degree; 17 jurisdictions offer other routes. [E1] [E2] [E4]
- Licensure is the spine of the field. The path is the degree, then the Architectural Experience Program (AXP, 3,740 hours across six areas), then the six-division Architect Registration Exam (ARE). BLS says all states and D.C. license architects and that boards generally require about three years of paid internship. [S7] [S1] [E10]
- Licensure is slow and leaky. NCARB reports an average of 12.9 years from opening a record to licensure for people licensed in 2024, about 3,600 new architects that year, more than 39,000 active candidates, and 36% to 38% of candidates stopping over a 10-year period. The experience program is the most common pinch point. [E11]
- Pay and outlook (BLS, May 2025 data): median $99,280; 10th percentile under $62,300; 90th percentile over $161,420; projected growth 4% for 2025 to 2035 (+5,300 jobs); about 6,900 openings a year; 124,600 jobs. [S1]
- Pay is growing slowly and licensure pays through raises. AIA reports architect compensation rose under 3% a year from 2023 to 2025, while non-licensed recent graduates saw 7% total wage growth. Raises at licensure were given by 80% of small firms, 79% of mid-size firms, and 69% of large firms. [E22] [E23]
- Business conditions are weak. The AIA/Deltek Architecture Billings Index fell to 44.9 in October 2026, which AIA describes as continued contraction. U.S. firms generated $121.7 billion in gross billings in 2025. [S9]
- Studio is the workload outlier. Design studio runs every semester for five years; one college guide estimates 40 to 60 hours a week including class time. A 2017 ranking reported by Dezeen and cited in Places Journal put architecture students' hours above every other major. [E16] [E18]
- AI adoption is real but measured inconsistently. AIA reports 6% of the profession regularly using AI (March 2025), 8% of firms having implemented AI solutions with another 20% working on it (March 2025), and 74% of firms using AI in day-to-day operations in 2025 (November 2026 firm survey). No source quantified job loss for architects. [S8] [S9] [S10]
- A doctorate is a research degree for teaching and scholarship, not a practice credential. Georgia Tech reports about 90% of its history, theory, and culture graduates in teaching and research posts. BLS lists a doctorate as typical for postsecondary teachers and expects limited full-time tenure and tenure-track openings in most disciplines. [E35] [S6]
- Eight career destinations were researched: licensed practice, specialized and technical design, computational design and BIM, real estate development, construction management, urban planning and design, academia and research, and design-adjacent creative work.

## Key Findings

- The degree name is not enough. A NAAB-accredited B.Arch can go straight to AXP, ARE, and licensure; a plain B.S. or B.A. in architecture is usually pre-professional and needs an M.Arch first. A non-accredited degree is not recognized as an accredited degree by NAAB. D.Arch programs are rare (the only one found in a 2023 source was at Hawaii). [E4] [E5] [E3]
- A NAAB degree is not universal. NCARB says 17 jurisdictions offer pathways to initial licensure without one, typically by adding professional experience. State rules differ (one state guide says Idaho requires about eight years of documented experience instead of a NAAB degree). [E1] [E14]
- AXP is 3,740 hours. By area: Practice Management 160; Project Management 360; Programming and Analysis 260; Project Planning and Design 1,080; Project Development and Documentation 1,520; Construction and Evaluation 360. At least 1,860 hours must be in Setting A (practice of architecture, under direct supervision by a licensed architect), and Setting O (other opportunities) is capped at 1,860 hours. [S7]
- The first draft used BLS May 2024 wages. BLS now reports a May 2025 architect median of $99,280, up from $96,690, and a lower base of openings (about 6,900 a year, down from about 7,800). The roadmap uses the May 2025 figures. [S1] [E24]
- Construction managers out-earn architects at the median by about $15,700 (BLS May 2025: $114,990 against $99,280) and at the 90th percentile ($189,440 against $161,420). BLS projects 9% growth for construction managers against 4% for architects. BLS does not name architecture among the typical majors for construction managers (it lists construction, business, engineering, or a related field), so the claim that architecture graduates can move over without more education rests on career-guide commentary only. [S2] [S1] [E30]
- Urban planners earn less than architects at the median (BLS May 2025: $89,320 against $99,280), and BLS lists a master's degree as the typical entry credential. [S3] [S1]
- BIM management is not shown to out-earn practice. Payscale reports an average BIM manager base salary of $97,442 (10th to 90th percentile $66,000 to $127,000, 268 profiles, updated 2026-09-14), roughly level with the architect median. No salary figure was found for computational designers. [S11]
- Development pay data is thin. Two job postings were found: a Senior Development Associate in Dallas at $120,000 to $150,000 plus bonus (5+ years of development experience, no design background required), and a Hines Director of Development in Washington, D.C. at $150,000 to $174,999 (six or more years in construction management, architecture, engineering, or project management preferred, a master's degree or real estate certificate preferred). Neither is a representative range. [S12] [S13]
- Specialization shows up in AIA's growth numbers more than in a published premium. AIA reports medical planners' pay grew 14% and specification writers' 11% from 2023 to 2025. Percentage premiums for healthcare or data-center work appeared only on calculator sites and were not relied on. [E23] [E44]
- Postsecondary architecture teachers are a small group with a wide pay range. BLS OEWS (May 2023 edition, the only one retrievable) shows 8,350 employed, a median of $105,770, and a 10th to 90th percentile range of $60,610 to $174,390. [S5]
- AI figures disagree because they measure different things. 6% regularly use AI as individuals; 8% of firms have implemented AI solutions; 74% of firms use AI day to day. MarketScale's write-up stresses that using a tool and getting results from it are different measurements. A vendor-run survey (Chaos and Architizer, 1,200+ design professionals, 2024) reported 46% using AI tools. [S8] [S9] [S10] [E27]
- Reform of studio culture is contested. AIAS has campaigned against all-nighters since 2000, and a Harvard Design Magazine piece found firm leaders saying all-nighters in offices point to poor management, while also reporting that students still take pride in them. [E17]

## Undergraduate (Years 1 to 5)

Routes and length:

- The B.Arch is a five-year undergraduate professional degree for students coming from high school; Cornell's B.Arch is 154 credits. Drexel's version can run longer because of cooperative-education work terms. [E4] [E3]
- A four-year pre-professional B.S. or B.A. followed by an M.Arch is the "4+2." [E4]
- NAAB accredits three degree types: B.Arch, M.Arch, D.Arch. [E2]

Prerequisites and admission:

- Some M.Arch programs expect calculus, physics, and freehand drawing before enrollment. Undergraduate admission norms for B.Arch programs were not researched. [E4]

Curriculum and difficulty:

- One college guide says design studio runs every semester for five years and takes 15 to 30+ hours a week on top of other courses. [E16]
- Typical architecture curricula combine history and theory, building technology (construction methods, structures, materials, environmental systems), digital and manual representation, and general education including mathematics, physics, and writing. [E20]
- No source identified a single "weeder" course. Research.com and the college guide describe the difficulty as cumulative, public, and time-sensitive (open-ended problems, public critiques, sustained output for years) rather than concentrated in one class. [E19] [E16]
- Studio critiques are public and personal; design problems have no single right answer. [E16]

Workload and culture:

- Estimated 25 to 40 hours a week outside class in a normal semester, with spikes of 50+ around reviews, and a total commitment of 40 to 60 hours a week including scheduled class and studio time. These are one guide's estimates, not survey results. [E16]
- A Dezeen article (February 14, 2017), cited by Places Journal, reported architecture students work longer hours than any other major. [E18]
- AIAS has worked to end all-nighters since 2000. A Harvard Design Magazine essay reports that firm leaders at AECOM and SOM called all-night charrettes outdated in professional practice, while students still treat them as a point of pride. [E17]

Tools:

- Tools named across sources: AutoCAD and Revit (BIM), Rhino with the Grasshopper plugin, Python and Dynamo for scripting, Enscape, Autodesk Forma, TestFit, Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Adobe Firefly, 3ds Max, Unity, Unreal Engine, Blender. V-Ray, Lumion, and SketchUp appear only in a low-quality blog and are used here as names only. [E25] [E26] [E28] [E30] [E42]
- AI concept images are described as strong for mood boards and early exploration but lacking the precision needed for construction documentation. [E28]

Beyond the classroom:

- AXP credit can begin once a student is enrolled in a NAAB-accredited degree program or a pre-professional program at a school that offers one. [E12]
- AIA's 2025 compensation report says the share of firms hiring student interns and externs grew, with large firms especially likely to. [E23]
- AIAS is the student organization; design competitions, internships, and portfolio building are mentioned throughout the career-guide sources, but no source quantified their value. [E5]

## Master's (Years ~5 to 8, overlapping AXP and ARE)

Degree routes (examples from individual schools):

- Illinois (Urbana-Champaign): M.Arch for pre-professional degree holders (62 graduate credit hours) and an M.Arch 2+ for other bachelor's holders (65 prerequisite hours plus 54 graduate hours). [E6]
- UIC: M.Arch of 100 credits for non-architecture or pre-professional holders; advanced standing of 64 credits for pre-professional holders. [E7]
- Georgia Tech: 3.5-year M.Arch track (102 semester hours in seven semesters) for non-professional degrees; two-year track (60 hours in four semesters) for pre-professional degrees. [E8]
- Cornell: M.Arch of 113 graduate credits for a four-year degree from a U.S. institution or equivalent. [E3]
- A professional M.Arch for someone with an unrelated bachelor's is generally three to four years. [E4]
- Post-professional master's degrees exist for specialization, for example Columbia's M.S. in Computational Design Practices, M.S. in Advanced Architectural Design (for B.Arch or M.Arch holders), M.S. in Real Estate Development, M.S. in Historic Preservation, and M.S. in Urban Planning. [E37]
- Funding norms for the professional M.Arch were not researched. Doctoral funding is covered below.

Licensure steps:

- AXP: 3,740 hours across six areas (see Key Findings). On November 18, 2025, NCARB replaced the five-year reporting window with a policy giving 100% credit for experience reported within a year and 75% credit afterward, with no age limit. [S7] [E9]
- ARE: six divisions, pass/fail, structure unchanged by the 2025 updates, which adjusted 12 of 91 exam objectives to align with NCARB's Competency Standard (16 competencies). Each division costs roughly $235 in one guide's figures. [E10] [E9] [E13]
- One licensing guide estimates one to three years to pass all six ARE divisions and 2 to 3 years of full-time work for AXP; it gives a typical total of 8 to 12 years from the start of a professional degree and a fastest path of about 7 to 8 years. One AXP guide author finished in about two and a half years. [E13] [E15]
- NCARB retired the five-year expiration on passed ARE divisions, reinstated thousands of expired passes, and launched free practice exams, which NCARB says contributed to higher pass rates. [E11]
- Several state boards loosened testing eligibility: California (from October 1, 2025) lets candidates test before fully documenting experience; Texas (from January 26, 2025) dropped a six-month supervised-experience requirement before the ARE; Washington (from July 1, 2024) dropped the five-year rolling window. These come from a board page, a licensing guide, and an insurance-company state guide. [E13] [E14]
- Licensure raises pay at most firms (see TL;DR), and AIA found firms more likely in 2024 than 2022 to avoid hiring staff without a professional architecture degree. [E23]

Advanced skills and tools:

- Capstone or thesis norms were not researched and vary by program.
- Scripting and parametric work: Grasshopper, Dynamo, and Python are named for computational design; a career guide says firms doing advanced computational work will not hire on a studio portfolio alone and want parametric projects and scripts. [E30]
- Site-planning tools: TestFit generates layout options from zoning, setback, and parking rules (BSB Design is cited as an adopter); Autodesk Forma analyzes site constraints, environmental data, and zoning. Revit plugins are described as helping identify design clashes. [E26] [E25]

## PhD / Research (Years ~8+)

What it is:

- Doctoral programs train researchers and teachers. Yale says its tracks (History and Theory; Ecosystems in Architectural Sciences) prepare teachers, with careers in university teaching, cultural advocacy, museum curatorship, and publishing named for the history track. Harvard GSD says its PhD is aimed at academic teaching and research careers. [E34] [E36]
- Georgia Tech reports that about 90% of its graduates in History, Theory, and Criticism and in Architecture, Culture, and Behavior from the last five years hold teaching and research posts, and that about 40% of graduates in Design Computing and Building Technology went to industry and national research laboratories. [E35]
- Programs usually expect an M.Arch or related graduate degree: IIT generally requires an M.Arch or M.S.Arch (minimum 3.5 GPA), and Virginia Tech seeks master's holders in closely allied fields. [E39] [E33]

Structure and timeline:

- Princeton describes a five-year program with two years of coursework and independent study, then a general examination dossier. IIT requires at least 52 credit hours after a master's and completion within six years of admission to candidacy. [E38] [E39]
- Yale expects students to teach or serve as research assistants for four terms, normally in years three and four. Georgia Tech funds students through graduate research and teaching assistantships and offers one Presidents' Fellowship a year (U.S. citizens and permanent residents). [E34] [E35]
- BLS says postsecondary teachers typically need a doctorate, tenure can take up to seven years, and most disciplines are expected to have a limited number of full-time tenure and non-tenure positions, while colleges are likely to hire more part-time teachers. Projected employment growth for postsecondary teachers is 7% for 2025 to 2035. This is for all disciplines, not architecture specifically. [S6]
- Hard-to-find PhD funding relative to a junior architect's salary is asserted in one opinionated career guide. [E32]

Research areas named in current program pages:

- Virginia Tech: architectural representation and education, history and theory, historic preservation, computing and representation, building science, and architectural acoustics. [E33]
- Berkeley: building science, technology and sustainability, with faculty work on passive design, thermal comfort, post-occupancy evaluation, lightweight design and biomimetics, parametric design, digital fabrication and robotic manufacturing, computational design, generative design, and embodied carbon. [E40]
- Princeton: a technology track in embodied computation and new systems for energy and environmental performance, linked to engineering and computer science, with an industrial robotic arm in its Embodied Computation Lab. [E38]
- IIT: urban energy modeling, high-rise and long-span buildings, energy-conscious design, emerging urbanisms, housing, history and theory, and advanced computer applications. [E39]
- Harvard GSD: history and theory, architectural technology (green building, engineering, computation, digital simulation), landscape, and urban form. [E36]
- A 2026 Springer article on generative AI in architecture argues that generative tools change how architects explore and develop ideas, not only how fast they work. [E29]

## Tools and AI

- Tools at each level are listed under Undergraduate and Master's. At the research level, sources name acoustic measurement and simulation labs (Virginia Tech), robotic fabrication (Princeton, Berkeley), and computation and generative design (Princeton, Berkeley, Georgia Tech). [E33] [E38] [E40] [E35]
- AIA's March 2025 report: 6% of the profession regularly uses AI (chatbots, image generators, grammar and text analytics); 8% of firms have implemented AI solutions and 20% more are working on it, driven by large firms (50+ employees); 84% expect to automate manual tasks, 74% expect AI to help with product research, and 90% are concerned about inaccuracy, unintended consequences, security, authenticity, and transparency. The page does not state a survey name, sample, or fieldwork dates. [S8]
- AIA's November 2026 firm survey (nearly 900 firms): 74% of firms used AI in day-to-day operations in 2025, with no size or year breakdown on the page. [S9] [S10]
- Practitioner and trade accounts: AI concept tools (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion) are used for mood boards and early exploration; AI concept images are generated independently from prompts and lack the precision of production visualization built on one accurate 3D model; visualization artists who keep core 3D skills and use AI selectively are described as faring best. Several pieces say AI changes how architects work rather than replacing them; these are opinion and vendor pieces. [E28] [E25] [E26] [E46]
- A figure that 37% to 49% of architecture and engineering tasks are automatable, attributed to McKinsey, and a figure that 64% of large firms use AI, attributed to AIA, appeared only in a low-quality blog and could not be traced. They are not used. [E42]
- No source quantified AI exposure by architecture career path. The "AI exposure" lines on the roadmap's career cards are editorial judgments built from the descriptive sources above, not measured figures.

## Career Paths

Titles for the first path follow AIA's standardized compensation levels as listed by a salary-calculator site (titles only; its salary figures are not relied on). Titles for other paths come from the postings and career guides cited; where a title ladder is a set of common industry titles with no specific source, that is noted.

1. Licensed practice

- Titles: Architectural Intern or Associate, Architect (levels I to III), Project Architect, Principal or Partner. [E43]
- Reality: This is the path the degree is built around. There are 124,600 architect jobs, BLS projects 4% growth, and AIA counts about 116,000 licensed architects. Pay grows slowly (under 3% a year from 2023 to 2025 per AIA), firm leaders' pay was stagnant or declining, and billings are contracting. Licensure usually brings a raise (80%, 79%, and 69% of small, mid-size, and large firms respectively). Non-licensed recent graduates saw 7% total wage growth from 2023 to 2025. [S1] [E21] [E22] [E23] [S9]
- Salary: BLS May 2025 median $99,280; 10th percentile under $62,300; 90th percentile over $161,420. [S1]

2. Specialized and technical design

- Titles: Medical Planner, Healthcare Architect, Specification Writer, Lab Planner. AIA's firm survey notes project-type mix shifting among institutional, commercial, residential, and data center work. Title ladder is partly editorial. [E23] [S9]
- Reality: Specialist roles saw faster pay growth than general staff from 2023 to 2025 (medical planners +14%, specification writers +11%). No national salary range for these specialties was found. [E23]
- Salary: No sourced range. AIA reports medical planner pay +14% and specification writer pay +11% over 2023 to 2025. [E23]

3. Computational design and BIM

- Titles: BIM Coordinator or Technician, BIM Manager, Computational Designer, Design Technologist. BIM titles from a career guide; the others are common industry titles. [E32] [E30]
- Reality: Computational designers use parametric modeling and scripting to solve geometry, fabrication, and automation problems; BIM managers run a firm's digital model infrastructure. A career guide says firms want evidence of scripts and parametric projects, not only studio work. Whether AI helps or hurts this path was not measured. [E30] [E32]
- Salary: Payscale BIM manager average base $97,442 (10th to 90th percentile $66,000 to $127,000; 268 profiles; updated 2026-09-14). No computational-designer figure found. [S11]

4. Real estate development

- Titles: Development Associate, Senior Development Associate, Development Manager, Director of Development (titles seen in postings). [S12] [S13]
- Reality: Development sits on the owner's side. The two postings found do not require a design degree for the senior-associate role and prefer construction, architecture, engineering, or project-management experience for the director role; Hines prefers a master's degree or real estate certificate. Master's programs in real estate development exist (Columbia). The amount of pay above these postings, such as equity or deal profits, was not quantified. [S12] [S13] [E37]
- Salary: Senior associate posting $120,000 to $150,000 plus bonus (Dallas); director posting $150,000 to $174,999 (Washington, D.C.). No national dataset found. [S12] [S13]

5. Construction management

- Titles: Project Engineer, Construction Manager, Owner's Representative. Common industry titles; owner's representation is named in a career guide. [E31]
- Reality: BLS lists a bachelor's in construction, business, engineering, or a related field as typical and projects 9% growth for 2025 to 2035. Career guides say architecture graduates move into construction management because they read drawings and understand design intent; BLS does not name architecture as an entry route. [S2] [E30]
- Salary: BLS May 2025 median $114,990; 10th percentile under $69,690; 90th percentile over $189,440. [S2]

6. Urban planning and design

- Titles: Urban Designer, City Planner, Transportation Planner, Director of Planning. Urban design and transportation planning are named in career guides; the director title is a common industry title. [E31] [E30]
- Reality: BLS lists a bachelor's or master's from an accredited planning program with a master's as typical entry education, and projects 4% growth. A career guide says architecture graduates bring spatial-design skills that planning graduates often lack. Landscape architecture, a licensed sibling profession, has a BLS May 2025 median of $79,870 and 4% projected growth; all states require landscape architects to be licensed. [S3] [E30] [S4]
- Salary: BLS May 2025 median $89,320; 10th percentile under $60,010; 90th percentile over $134,490. [S3]

7. Academia and research

- Titles: Adjunct or Lecturer, Assistant to Full Professor, Researcher, Associate Dean. BLS names adjunct and the assistant, associate, and full professor ranks; the researcher and dean titles are common academic titles. [S6] [E40]
- Reality: A doctorate is typical (BLS); tenure can take up to seven years; BLS expects colleges to hire more part-time teachers and limited full-time tenure and non-tenure positions in most disciplines. Only about 8,350 people were employed as postsecondary architecture teachers in the May 2023 OEWS edition. Design faculty may enter on practice reputation, but this was not verified. [S6] [S5]
- Salary: BLS OEWS (May 2023 edition) architecture teachers, postsecondary: median $105,770; mean $114,900; 10th percentile $60,610; 90th percentile $174,390. [S5]

8. Design-adjacent and creative

- Titles: Interior Architect, Production Designer, Visualization Artist, Product or Furniture Designer. [E20] [E30] [E28]
- Reality: Career guides list interiors, production design, visualization, product design, and UX and spatial computing as destinations that use spatial reasoning and visual communication. Visualization is the area where the sources discuss AI most directly (concept imagery generated in minutes, but not precise enough for production work). [E30] [E28]
- Salary: No sourced range; pay varies widely by niche. [no source]

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- US only. Licensure rules, degree accreditation, and salaries are US-specific. Other countries use different bodies and routes; none were researched.
- Licensure varies by jurisdiction. NAAB-degree rules, experience requirements, and testing eligibility differ by state. The state-level changes cited (California, Texas, Washington) come from a board page and secondary guides; check the board before relying on them. [E13] [E14]
- Geography moves pay. AIA says Los Angeles and San Francisco offered notably higher starting salaries, offset by higher rents. Regional percentage premiums quoted by calculator sites were not relied on. [E23] [E43] [E44]
- BLS architecture-teacher wages are from May 2023 because the only copy retrievable under the allowlist was that edition. A newer edition likely exists. [S5]
- The AIA 2025 Compensation and Benefits Report is paywalled. Level-by-level figures (intern, Architect I to III, principal) were seen only on aggregator sites (Calcgator, CDCalculators, Stealth Agents) and are not used. The roadmap uses BLS percentiles for the licensed-practice range instead. [E43] [E44] [E45]
- AI adoption numbers (6%, 8%, 74%, 46%) are not comparable. See Key Findings. The 74% figure counts any day-to-day use and says nothing about benefit. [S10]
- The billings index figure (44.9, October 2026) is from AIA's firm-survey page, which cites a separate press release dated November 18, 2026 that was not fetched directly. [S9]
- Development pay rests on two job postings with no posted dates. A third development posting (Careerjet) hit a bot-verification wall and a fourth (The Ladders) failed to load. [S12] [S13]
- Role-title ladders for construction management, urban planning, and academia are partly common industry titles without a single source.
- First-draft claims removed or rewritten because no source supported them: McKinsey 37% to 49% task exposure; 64% of large firms using AI; healthcare 18% to 30% and data-center or lab 20% to 35% pay premiums; licensure adding 18% to 35%; principal median about $155,000; unlicensed associates at $52,000 to $78,000; adjunct pay of $45,000 to $70,000; tenured professor pay of $95,000 to $160,000+; development entry pay of $70,000 to $110,000 and $200,000+ at the top; computational design pay of $90,000 to $150,000+; design-adjacent pay of $55,000 to $120,000+; construction manager median $104,900 and planner range $81,800 to $83,720 (older BLS editions); "Structures is the weeder"; "Revit fluency is one of the most-requested entry-level skills"; "the 4+2 is the most common route"; "the PhD track growing fastest is computation"; mass timber, 3D-printed concrete, and digital twins as research frontiers; Ladybug, Honeybee, EnergyPlus, and Navisworks as named tools; "four to six years" for the PhD; a statement that only licensed architects may stamp drawings.
- Not researched: M.Arch funding norms, B.Arch admission norms, capstone and thesis norms, international licensure, and whether design faculty can enter without a doctorate.
