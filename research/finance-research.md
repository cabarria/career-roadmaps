## TL;DR

- Finance is typically a business-school major (BBA/BSBA, 120 to 124 credits) built on two pillars: investments/capital markets and corporate finance.
- The defining feature of the undergraduate years is recruiting, not coursework. Investment banking junior-summer applications start posting around September of sophomore year, surge January to March, and the junior summer internship is the main route to a full-time offer.
- No graduate degree is required to enter the field. The MS in Finance, MBA, MFE and CFA charter are accelerants. CFP is the standard credential for financial planning (voluntary; the CFP Board lists its requirements). Securities licensing (FINRA) is general background that this pass did not verify.
- A finance PhD takes about five years, is funded at research programs, and trains academic researchers in financial economics. It is not a higher-level MBA. Finance is the second-highest-paid business-school discipline (AACSB 2025-26: mean $190,553).
- AI is automating junior-level modeling, pitchbook and reporting work. Sources disagree sharply on how many jobs this removes. Practitioners agree on one point: learn the skill by hand first, then automate it.
- Seven career destinations were researched. Pay is bonus-heavy in the front office (investment banking, private equity, quant), so BLS base-wage data understates it. The best-documented growth is in financial examiners (+19%) and personal financial advisors (+10%).

## Key Findings

- Recruiting timeline: Adventis counted 15 firms posting summer 2027 internships between August and November 2025, 27 in December and 46 in January. Some private equity and advisory firms posted before the traditional banking window.
- Conversion: summer analyst programs convert to full-time offers at roughly 70 to 80% in strong markets, versus 30 to 50% for off-cycle and 10 to 20% for semester internships (Prospect Rock).
- Entry pay in investment banking: about $170K to $195K all-in for first-year analysts at large banks and elite boutiques (Mergers & Inquisitions, Wall Street Careers). Hours are routinely 60 to 80 per week.
- BLS (May 2024): financial and investment analysts $101,350 median, +6% growth; personal financial advisors $102,140 median, +10%; financial examiners $90,400 median, +19%; financial risk specialists $106,000 median.
- Faculty pay (AACSB, 2025-26, Americas): finance faculty average $190,553 with a $170,000 median, second only to real estate; finance assistant professors average about $180K. Roger Martin attributes high business-school pay to a structural shortage of business PhDs (2020 opinion).
- CFA pass rates (May 2026): Level 1 39%, Level 2 43%, Level 3 50%. Only about 10 to 20% of candidates who start finish all three levels.
- AI exposure is concentrated at the junior level. Reported projections (Bloomberg Intelligence via Forbes, Citigroup via a secondary source) are large but come second-hand, and practitioners on forums are skeptical of front-office headcount cuts.

## Undergraduate (Years 1 to 4)

Degree structure:

- Business core plus finance major. Examples: Illinois requires 124 hours and the Gies business core including ACCY 201 and 202; Quinnipiac requires 122 credits; UT Dallas requires 120; Georgetown requires 15 credits of finance on top of the business core.
- NYU Stern splits finance into investments/capital markets (pricing and valuation, investment strategies, workings of capital markets and regulation) and corporate finance (financial decision-making, governance, markets for corporate control). Its concentration is Foundations of Finance, Corporate Finance, and three advanced electives.
- Babson's concentration requires at least four 3000-level finance courses (16 credits). Babson notes that students most successful at landing finance offers have usually taken at least one accounting course, most commonly Intermediate Accounting for Finance (ACC3502).
- Illinois recommends additional accounting and states that many employers look favorably on it. It also requires a computer science course in the first year.
- University of Florida's foundation courses are economics, mathematics, computing skills and accounting. UT Austin offers a Financial Analyst Program (one year, competitively selected).
- UT Dallas offers career-focused concentrations (its page says both "six" and "five" tracks, so the count is unclear).

Weeder courses: not researched. No source in this pass identifies which courses are notoriously hard. The first draft of the artifact called accounting "the classic weeder sequence", which is unsupported.

Skills and tools:

- QuintEdge lists what banks prioritize: modeling (DCF, LBO, merger models), accounting and the three statements, valuation, attention to detail, communication, stamina.
- Brookings (Oct 2025): "Excel is no longer a differentiator" and "Python is fast becoming the new Excel."
- Market data platforms named in the sources: Bloomberg and Capital IQ (Mergers & Inquisitions, in passing). Bloomberg Market Concepts, FactSet and the SIE exam are not covered by any source in this pass.

Recruiting (the centerpiece of this stage):

- Applications for junior summer internships begin appearing in the fall of sophomore year, more than 18 months ahead; January to March is the heaviest period; interviews are rolling (Adventis, Dartmouth).
- Early insights programs start the summer before sophomore year and feed interview pipelines. Many are diversity-focused, some are open to all (Adventis).
- Referrals matter; selective finance clubs and strong alumni networks are an advantage; students at schools without banking pipelines still break in each cycle (Adventis, Leland, Priority Candidates).
- GPA: above roughly 3.5 to 3.7 keeps most doors open (Prospect Rock).
- Timeliness caveat: the Wall Street Playbook (updated 15 June 2026) says the summer 2027 cycle was already in triage by mid-June 2026. As of late November 2026, the summer 2028 cycle (class of 2029) is the live one. These dates shift every year and should be rechecked.

## Master's (Years about 5 to 7)

Degree types:

- MS in Finance (MSF/MF/MiF): one to two years, focused on financial theory, markets, risk, investments (National University). Cheaper than an MBA on average.
- MBA with finance concentration: about two years, broad (leadership, marketing, strategy). Practitioner forums suggest taking it three to five years into a career. Mergers & Inquisitions notes that entering private equity directly out of an MBA is nearly impossible without prior banking or PE experience.
- MFE/quant master's: stochastic calculus, derivative pricing, programming. Stratus Admissions notes programming ability is a differentiator and some MFE programs require it. Berkeley's MFE (2019 data): 67 students received 129 offers in 2018, average first-year pay over $159K. Carnegie Mellon's MS in Computational Finance reported 93% employed within three months.
- STEM designation: Stevens states its applicable MBA concentrations hold STEM designation, which gives international students a 24-month OPT extension. Iowa's finance PhD is also STEM-designated.

CFA charter:

- Pass rates May 2026: L1 39%, L2 43%, L3 50% (Schweser, 300Hours). Historic averages: about 40, 45 and 50%.
- Roughly 300 hours of study per level (Schweser). Only 10 to 15% of Level 1 candidates eventually earn the charter (300Hours); Katalyst says about 20% of enrolled candidates complete all three levels.
- Cost: $1,140 early or $1,490 standard registration per level; realistic total $5,500 to $6,000+ with retakes; the $350 enrollment fee was eliminated in February 2026 (Test Prep Insight).
- Charter requires passing all three levels plus approved work experience (300Hours). UK NARIC benchmarks the levels from diploma (L1) to bachelor's (L2) to master's (L3).
- Who it serves: investment management, equity research, portfolio strategy (Test Prep Insight, WSO, Katalyst). Practitioner forums say MFin and MBA have broader exits.
- FP&A salary guides list CFP, CMA and CFA as top pay-boosting certifications (Datarails).

Other credentials:

- CFP (CFP Board, fetched 2026-11-22): a bachelor's degree in any discipline plus CFP Board registered coursework; a 170-question exam in two three-hour sessions; 6,000 hours of experience (or 4,000 apprenticeship hours); ethics declaration and background check. SmartAsset says credentials like CFP and CFA may raise advisor pay by 30% or more. BLS says certification may improve chances for advancement.
- FRM (GARP): two parts, long-run pass rates about 46% and 55% (QuintEdge).
- FINRA licenses (SIE, Series 7, 63, 79): general background knowledge only, not verified in this pass.
- CPA: Finance Alliance says most FP&A managers hold a CPA or MBA. Mergers & Inquisitions says controllership is more useful for the CFO path at smaller firms and FP&A at larger firms.

## PhD / Research (Years about 8+)

Structure:

- About five years, funded at research programs (Iowa: 72 semester hours plus dissertation, five years typical; Oregon: tuition waiver, stipend, health plan). Ohio State: fifth year is the job market and defense.
- Years one and two: PhD courses in asset pricing, corporate finance, economics and econometrics; qualifying or comprehensive exam at the end of year two (Oregon, Miami, Memphis). Passing the comp exam gives ABD status (Memphis).
- Years three to five: research papers, dissertation proposal, a job market paper (Miami, Oregon: proposal ideally defended by end of year three).
- Admission: Memphis says students should arrive with extensive quantitative background and data-processing skills; Oregon runs a short math-tools course before the first term. Applications typically open October and close December or January (MyStudyFuture).
- Stanford GSB: a finance PhD "is not a higher-level MBA, but an advanced, academically oriented degree in financial economics."

Research frontiers named in the sources:

- Climate and ESG finance, household finance, financial intermediation, entrepreneurial finance/venture/PE, FinTech, behavioral finance (Duke Fuqua research areas).
- Private credit: a 2026 arXiv survey says climate/ESG integration and systemic-risk measurement in opaque private credit markets lack systematic academic study.
- Machine learning: an arXiv systematic review of 22 studies (2024 to 2026) finds hybrid ML/econometric frameworks improving prediction in asset pricing, volatility, credit risk and macro policy analysis.
- FinTech credit scoring from digital footprints is the subject of a published paper by Berg, Burg and coauthors (title confirmed; findings not read).

Placements:

- Columbia: graduates "take jobs primarily in academic or research institutions, while some opt to work in industry."
- Iowa lists tenure-track placements and non-academic employers; USC lists IMF, US Treasury's Office of Financial Research and Cornerstone Research among recent outcomes; MyStudyFuture names the Federal Reserve, SEC, IMF and World Bank.
- Faculty pay (AACSB 2026 Trend Watch, published 30 June 2026, 2025-26 survey; the regional figures cover the Americas macroregion of 386 institutions and 27,105 faculty, which is mostly US but not US-only): finance faculty (including banking) average $190,553 with a median of $170,000 (N=4,248), the second-highest discipline after real estate ($192,795). By rank, finance assistant professors average about $180K, associate professors $193K and full professors $239K (rounded chart values). For comparison, the Americas median for all-discipline assistant professors is $146,900.
- Supply: Roger Martin (former Rotman dean, Poets&Quants, June 2020) describes a structural shortage of business PhDs (about 1.7 graduating PhDs per thousand student-years versus 17.5 for higher education overall) and says new finance, accounting and strategy PhDs commanded more than $150,000 when he was dean (1998 to 2013). That is an opinion piece with a dated figure; the AACSB report itself makes no finance-specific supply claim.
- Context: NEA data reported by Poets&Quants (May 2025) puts average business faculty pay at $117,000 at four-year institutions in 2023-24, third behind health professions and engineering/architecture. That covers all business faculty at all kinds of institutions, so it sits well below the AACSB survey figures.

## Tools and AI

- Forbes (June 2026): AI is automating routine tasks and hitting entry-level and junior roles hardest. It cites Bloomberg Intelligence projecting up to 200,000 Wall Street job cuts over the next few years. It also lists new hybrid roles (AI automation engineers, AI governance and compliance managers) at $130K to $180K mid-level baseline.
- A lower-quality source (Whitehat SEO) cites a Citigroup report that 54% of financial jobs have high automation potential and reports Goldman Sachs layoffs of 1,000+ linked to AI. Treat as unverified.
- Deloitte (via Cyndx): top 14 global banks could raise front-office productivity 27% to 35% with generative AI.
- Brookings (Oct 2025): hybrid skills win; Morgan Stanley advisors work with GPT-4-based copilots; tacit knowledge limits displacement. McKinsey 2025: only 1% of organizations call their generative AI deployments mature, while 92% plan to increase spending.
- Mergers & Inquisitions (Mar 2026): doubts that banks will stop hiring juniors because AI generates pitchbooks and models; names AI products (Hebbia, ProSights, Shortcut AI, Claude for Financial Services).
- Wall Street Oasis practitioners (Feb 2026): "be the smartest raw analyst you can be," learn techniques by hand first; large banks use AI for back-office more than front-office; headcount likely grows more slowly rather than falling.
- QuintEdge (May 2026): as of 2026, AI has not reduced investment banking headcount or depressed salaries.

## Career Paths

All figures US unless noted. "Industry" means a practitioner or recruiter source, not BLS.

### 1. Investment Banking

- Titles: Summer Analyst, Analyst, Associate, VP, Director, Managing Director.
- Reality: highly competitive and front-loaded (see recruiting above). Analysts work 60 to 80 hours a week, 100+ in deal peaks (Glassdoor via Wall Street Careers); the two-year program is treated as a credential and launchpad. Regional middle-market banks pay less base ($80K to $100K) with better hours.
- Salary: first-year analyst $170K to $195K all-in (Wall Street Careers, Mergers & Inquisitions, 2026); analyst bonuses 50 to 100% of base; associates $300K to $450K at bulge brackets; elite boutiques pay associates $50K to $100K+ more (Mergers & Inquisitions). Indeed's average posting salary is $133,762 (a broader, lower-comp sample).
- Sources: Wall Street Careers; Mergers & Inquisitions; Indeed.

### 2. Private Equity and Buy-Side

- Titles: Analyst, Associate, Senior Associate, VP, Principal, Partner.
- Reality: mega-fund associates are overwhelmingly former bulge-bracket or elite-boutique analysts; on-cycle recruiting can run from first contact to offer in one evening (Wall Street Careers). Direct-from-undergrad PE analyst seats exist and are growing, but total comp is typically below an IB analyst's (Mergers & Inquisitions). Partners at top firms can reach $5M to $10M, but carry may take 10+ years.
- Salary: associates $250K to $330K+ (MnA Community); $250K to $400K total (Qubit Capital); PE analyst average $178,595 on Glassdoor (25th to 75th percentile $140K to $232K).
- Sources: Mergers & Inquisitions; MnA Community; Glassdoor; Qubit Capital.

### 3. Quantitative Finance

- Titles: Quant Researcher, Quant Trader, Quant Developer, Strat, Quant PM.
- Reality: math, statistics and programming matter more than the finance degree. New PhDs at Citadel or Jane Street start at the top of the range; some prop firms and mid-tier funds hire strong master's graduates (Quantt). Hedge fund pay depends on firm tier and bonus.
- Salary: junior researcher base $150K to $250K, bonuses 2 to 5 times base at top firms (Myntbit); junior total comp $150K to $320K US or GBP 85K to 180K in London (Quantt); Jane Street trader $400K to $700K total on a job posting cited by a Substack analysis; ZipRecruiter average for hedge fund quant analyst $133,877 (25th to 75th percentile $111.5K to $145.5K).
- Sources: Myntbit; Quantt; Navnoor Bawa Substack; ZipRecruiter.

### 4. Asset and Wealth Management

- Titles: Financial Advisor, Wealth Advisor, Research Analyst, Portfolio Manager.
- Reality: BLS projects +10% growth (2024 to 2034) with about 24,100 openings a year; a bachelor's degree is typical entry. Pay is highly skewed (10th percentile under $49,990, 90th over $239,200) and depends on client base. Robo-advisors may affect clients wanting low-cost planning, but BLS and SmartAsset expect complex planning to keep demanding human advisors.
- Salary: median $102,140 (BLS, May 2024); securities and investments industry median $109,390 (SmartAsset citing BLS).
- Sources: BLS Personal Financial Advisors; SmartAsset; CFP Board.

### 5. Corporate Finance and FP&A

- Titles: FP&A Analyst, Senior Analyst, Manager, Director, VP, Controller, Treasurer, CFO.
- Reality: broad entry (a solid-but-not-top-tier school with decent grades and good accounting knowledge, per Mergers & Inquisitions); slower promotion (10 to 15 years to director); most managers hold a CPA or MBA.
- Salary: FP&A analyst $71,250 to $88,000 (Robert Half 2026); BLS financial analyst median $101,350; manager $105K to $150K plus bonus (Workday via A-State); director/VP total $150K to $210K+; public-company CFO average base about $362K, total about $506K (Analytics Insight).
- Conflicting figures: Datarails quotes entry FP&A at $47K to $67K, Abacum quotes an implausible $138K "national average" for entry level. Neither was used.
- Sources: Mergers & Inquisitions; Robert Half via A-State; Workday via A-State; Finance Alliance; Datarails; BLS.

### 6. Risk, Regulation and Policy

- Titles: Risk Analyst, Financial Examiner, Compliance Officer, Model Validator, Fed/SEC Economist.
- Reality: BLS projects financial examiners +19% (2024 to 2034), about 5,700 openings a year. Separately, CFI and Forbes describe AI governance, compliance and model validation as emerging roles. FRM is the standard risk credential.
- Salary: examiners $90,400 median; financial risk specialists $106,000 median, 10th to 90th percentile $62,270 to $182,310 (BLS, May 2024).
- Sources: BLS Financial Examiners; BLS Financial Analysts; CFI; Forbes.

### 7. Fintech and Entrepreneurship

- Titles: Fintech Financial Analyst, Product Manager, Compliance/RegTech Specialist, Data Scientist, BizOps/Strategy, Founder.
- Reality: demand centers on hybrid profiles that pair finance knowledge with data and AI skills (Forbes, IntelligentHQ, Brookings). One practical guide (Fintek Cafe) describes entering through adjacent roles, for example a bank credit analyst moving to a credit product analyst role at a lender and later into product management, and recommends the CAMS certification for compliance careers. Whitehat SEO suggests bankers with AI experience are valued in fintech (low-quality source). Neither salary guide covers the founder path, so founder pay and odds are not established.
- Salary (secondary sources, treat as indicative): Stealth Agents, citing BLS, Robert Half 2026 and Levels.fyi, lists a fintech financial analyst median base of $99,890 (typical total $90K to $130K), product manager median base $131,000 (total $145K to $230K), compliance officer median base $115,000, RegTech specialist $128,000 and data scientist $108,020. Fintek Cafe, citing Glassdoor and Levels.fyi, gives base ranges of $130K to $200K for product managers and $80K to $140K for compliance and risk analysts. Levels.fyi (2026) shows a $160,000 median total compensation for fintech software engineers. Forbes cites $130K to $180K for mid-level AI-automation finance roles.
- Caveats: both guides are commercial content with internal inconsistencies (Stealth Agents lists a compliance total below its base; Fintek Cafe's two salary tables disagree). Product-manager figures describe experienced hires, and no source in this pass gives true entry-level fintech pay. The fintech analyst base is close to the BLS national median for financial analysts ($101,350), so it does not show a clear fintech premium.
- Sources: Stealth Agents; Fintek Cafe; Levels.fyi; Forbes.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- Everything is US-centric. Non-US figures appear only for London (quant, GBP) and an Asia-Pacific JPMorgan posting. Other regions were not researched.
- Front-office pay comes from practitioner sites, not audited data. Bonuses vary by year; only first-year analyst and associate ranges are fairly consistent across sources.
- The IB recruiting cycle dates shift annually; figures here reflect the summer 2027 cycle as reported in mid-2026.
- CFA pass rates differ slightly between sources (Level 1 May 2026: 39% in Schweser and 300Hours; UWorld says 45% for 2026; February 2026 was 45%).
- Several sources are old forum threads (2007 to 2010) used only for qualitative practitioner opinion: GMAT Club, QuantNet, Wall Street Oasis.
- Claims still unverified and kept out of the artifact: "weeder" accounting sequence; Bloomberg Market Concepts and FactSet; SIE and Series 79 details; "~80% of VPs hold an MBA"; WRDS, CRSP and Compustat; LLMs as standard research instruments; founder path details; "top-5 MFE" tiering.
- Resolved in version 2 of this file: finance-faculty pay and supply (now sourced to AACSB and Poets&Quants) and fintech compensation (now sourced, but only to commercial guides).
- The AACSB figures cover institutions that participate in the AACSB survey in the Americas macroregion, not all US schools. Martin's supply argument is a 2020 opinion piece.
- CFP is a voluntary certification, not a legal gate to giving planning advice. The first draft implied it was a gate.
