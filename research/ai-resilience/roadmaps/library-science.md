# Library Science: AI resilience

Evidence cutoff **2026-11-25** · United States · scenario horizon **2029–31**.

## Featured occupation

**Librarians and media collections specialists**; stable career ID `librarians-and-media-collections-specialists`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `25-4022`.

Librarians and media collections specialists in all settings combined; library technicians (25-4031), clerical library assistants (43-4121), archivists, curators and postsecondary library science teachers are separate occupations with their own task mixes.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 25-4022.00 Librarians and Media Collections Specialists.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 142,300 jobs in 2025; 2.6% projected change, 2025–35; 12,600 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 25-4022](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

Librarianship mixes text-heavy tasks that generative tools can already assist with and relationship, teaching and stewardship tasks that stay with people. Surveyed librarians already use the tools: 66.5% of 272 respondents used generative AI at work and rated it 6.76 out of 10, while naming inconsistent accuracy, privacy and prompting skill as the main concerns. Among 483 academic library leaders surveyed in late 2025, 40% used AI in discovery and access, 29% in research support and 26% in internal data analysis, 24% used none, and 83% expected more demand for AI-literacy instruction. Cataloging is where the evidence is most specific: the Library of Congress found title and author fields reached up to 99% accuracy but subject and genre fell below 50%, short of the 95% bar for full automation. Employment signals point mostly elsewhere: BLS projects 2.6% growth for librarians but declines of 6.4% for both library technicians and clerical library assistants, and 2025 federal grant terminations at IMLS (reinstated in December 2025) show how funding can matter more than technology. The Anthropic (20.32%) and Microsoft (25.43 points) indicators put librarians near the middle of the featured occupations; they track text-and-chat tool use and are not a job-loss estimate.

**Independent sources:** [Library of Congress cataloging experiments](https://repository.ifla.org/handle/20.500.14598/6861), [Ithaka S+R US Library Survey 2025](https://sr.ithaka.org/publications/ithaka-sr-us-library-survey-2025/), [SJSU survey of 272 librarians](https://ischool.sjsu.edu/ciri-blog/how-librarians-are-using-generative-ai-insights-nationwide-survey), [Library Journal Placements and Salaries Survey 2024](https://libraryjournal.com/story/challenges-opportunities-placements-and-salaries-2024), [NPR on IMLS grants](https://npr.org/2025/12/04/nx-s1-5633347/libraries-museums-federal-funding-imls-trump-cuts).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

By 2029–31, if discovery systems add AI answer tools, vendors offer AI-assisted cataloging and models improve at subject analysis, routine cataloging, copy-editing of records and first-draft reference answers could take less staff time, and library technician and clerical roles, already projected to shrink, could contract faster. Teaching AI literacy, verifying sources, selecting collections for a community, running programs and managing budgets depend on trust and local judgment, and could grow in importance as AI-generated content spreads. Whether libraries use time saved to expand services or to cut staff depends on funding more than on technology.

This scenario assumes AI tools in library systems reach production quality and are priced for small libraries. Slow adoption, accuracy problems like the Library of Congress's subject-field results, or continued budget cuts would keep today's pattern. None of this establishes employment probabilities.

## Student preparation

- Work in a library early, in any role, and keep a record of tasks you can show: reference questions answered, programs run, collections organized, metadata edited.
- Learn to verify AI output against sources, and practise teaching others to do it; AI-literacy instruction is among the most commonly expected new duties.
- Add one technical or data skill that complements library judgment, such as metadata and linked data, SQL or Python for data cleanup, or research data management.

## Limits and scoring

BLS groups all librarians in one occupation and publishes no separate wage for school library media specialists, so the anchor is a broad average of very different settings. The Ithaka S+R survey covers academic libraries only, the 272-librarian survey is a convenience sample, the Library of Congress result is an abstract about one set of eBook records, and the Library Journal survey comes from graduates of participating schools. No causal study of AI and librarian employment was found, and the Occupational Outlook Handbook's Job Outlook text for librarians does not mention AI.

No comparable occupation-level validated resilience score; missing representative task-time, adoption and demand-response evidence. Published exposure indicators track text-chat tool use; no occupation-level causal hiring study found.
