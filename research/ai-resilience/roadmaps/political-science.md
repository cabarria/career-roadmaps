## Current evidence

Gilardi et al. show strong performance on bounded text annotation; a later US news tweet study finds variability and conventional supervised classifier superiority on some tasks. Document summaries and coding can be cheap, but political interpretation, source verification, sampling and fieldwork still matter. Text-classification scores are not evidence of political job loss.

**Independent sources:** [ChatGPT outperforms crowd workers for text-annotation tasks](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305016120), [Are chatbots reliable text annotators? Sometimes](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf069), [Experimental evidence on productivity effects of generative AI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586), [The AI Jobs Transition Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/the-ai-jobs-transition-framework_report.pdf).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Agents may accelerate document review, polling summaries and draft briefs. Government and campaign adoption depends on public records policies, evidence standards, procurement, confidentiality and stakeholder accountability. Junior research assistants may lose some routine coding tasks; fieldwork and validated methods gain importance.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.
