**Writers and authors** — stable career ID `writers-and-authors`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `27-3043`.

Writers and authors; includes creative-writing subtype, not separately weighted. Editors, technical writers and teachers are separate.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 27-3043.00 Writers and Authors; 27-3043.05 Poets, Lyricists and Creative Writers.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 140,300 jobs in 2025; -0.3% projected change, 2025–35; 11,900 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 27-3043](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

Noy and Zhang's randomized study shows productivity gains on bounded professional writing; freelance data show exposed platform categories lost work and earnings after major model releases. Neither measures the whole writer occupation. Drafts and editing are exposed; source discovery, original reporting, distinctive voice, copyright and accountable factual verification remain valuable.

**Independent sources:** [Experimental evidence on productivity effects of generative AI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586), [Short-Term Effects of Generative AI on Employment](https://ssrn.com/abstract=4544582), [Copyright and AI, Part 2: Copyrightability](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf), [Factors affecting occupational utilization](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/factors-affecting-occupational-utilization.htm).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Agents could deliver full standardized copy workflows, compressing junior editing and freelance assignments. More cheaply produced content may raise volume while reducing rates; trust and curation may become scarcer. Investigative work and education depend on settings and access, not just text quality.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.
