## Current evidence

AMA's 2026 physician survey reports 81% professional AI use, much of it summarization and documentation across specialties. Five-system longitudinal scribe evidence finds modest EHR time savings rather than demonstrated physician substitution. FDA-authorized diagnostic systems include non-generative ML. Examination, procedures, diagnosis, consent and treatment responsibility remain central. The five-system study's 13.4-minute EHR reduction and 16-minute documentation reduction are per eight scheduled patient hours, not per encounter, and the observed visit increase was modest.

**Independent sources:** [2026 Physician Survey on Augmented Intelligence](https://www.ama-assn.org/sites/ama-assn.org/files/2026-03/physician-ai-sentiment-report_0.pdf), [Changes in Clinician Time Expenditure and Visit Quantity With Adoption of AI-Powered Scribes](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2847319), [Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Medical Devices](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Validated EHR agents could support note preparation and routing, increasing clinician capacity or shifting staffing. Payment incentives, privacy, liability, error monitoring and patient acceptance determine adoption. Trainees should not bypass independent clinical reasoning; specialties with procedures differ from document-heavy roles.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.

## Student preparation

- Learn clinical interviewing, physical examination and differential reasoning through accredited training.
- Practice checking draft notes against source encounters in approved simulations.
- Study informed consent, privacy and clinical evidence assessment..
