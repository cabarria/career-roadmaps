## Current evidence

Writing and tutoring experiments demonstrate bounded content assistance, not philosophical research or classroom replacement. Generative tools can suggest objections, draft lecture materials and summarize literature; accurate citations and original arguments require scrutiny. Research accountability and confidentiality are illustrated by NIH rules but faculty practice varies.

**Independent sources:** [Experimental evidence on productivity effects of generative AI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586), [Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1054), [Accelerating Mathematical and Scientific Discovery with Gemini Deep Think](https://deepmind.google/blog/accelerating-mathematical-and-scientific-discovery-with-gemini-deep-think/), [Guidelines for the Conduct of Research, ninth edition](https://oir.nih.gov/system/files/media/file/2026-09/guidelines-conduct_research.pdf).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Agents may make routine lecture prep cheaper and require redesigned assessments to preserve reasoning. University budgets and tenure scarcity are independent constraints. Ethics and governance jobs may expand with adoption, but policy, consulting and law have separate occupation demands and credentials.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.

## Student preparation

- Practice reconstructing arguments and answering objections without AI.
- Use primary texts and verify sources, then compare alternative arguments.
- Build experience in discussion facilitation, applied ethics or policy evaluation..
