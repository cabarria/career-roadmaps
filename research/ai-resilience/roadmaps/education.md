**High school teachers** — stable career ID `high-school-teachers`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `25-2031`.

Secondary teachers except special and career/technical education; not administrators, all grade levels, or instructional designers.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 25-2031.00 Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Career/Technical Education.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 1,087,500 jobs in 2025; -0.2% projected change, 2025–35; 63,500 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 25-2031](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

RAND documents self-reported K–12 instructional AI use. Tutor CoPilot's randomized field study improved topic mastery by four percentage points when tutors used AI guidance, not when teachers were replaced. Draft lesson plans and feedback can be assisted, but supervision, classroom order, relationships, accessibility and assessment integrity are continuing duties. RAND's 2024 survey of 8,814 teachers found 25% reported AI instructional planning or teaching use, with secondary teachers at 34%; the survey predates the scenario horizon.

**Independent sources:** [AI Use Among Teachers and Principals in the 2023–2024 School Year](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA100/RRA134-25/RAND_RRA134-25.pdf), [Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1054), [The AI Jobs Transition Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/the-ai-jobs-transition-framework_report.pdf).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Approved curriculum-aligned tutors could individualize exercises and reduce preparation; some staffing models may change, especially virtual instruction. Child privacy, budgets, pedagogical quality and equitable access limit adoption. Demand depends on enrollment, funding and staffing policies, not exposure alone.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.

## Student preparation

- Observe classrooms and develop a lesson both with and without AI.
- Design oral, process-based and in-class assessments and audit generated materials.
- Practice safeguarding, accessibility and privacy-compliant tool use during placements..
