# Economics: AI resilience

Evidence cutoff **2026-11-25** · United States · scenario horizon **2029–31**.

## Featured occupation

**Economists** — stable career ID `economists`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `19-3011`.

Economists by occupational title; environmental economists are a subtype; other economics graduate roles not covered.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 19-3011.00 Economists; 19-3011.01 Environmental Economists.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 18,600 jobs in 2025; +4.7% projected change, 2025–35; 1,100 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 19-3011](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

Korinek documents LLM assistance for search, code, derivations, writing and communication, not measured occupational employment effects. Data collection, identification assumptions, institutional knowledge and interpretation still require economist judgment. Consultancy experiment demonstrates bounded analytic gains, not an economist-specific estimate.

**Independent sources:** [Generative AI for Economic Research: LLMs Learn to Collaborate and Reason](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33198), [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/), [Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about Recent Employment Effects of AI](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf), [AI-exposed jobs deteriorated before ChatGPT](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/ai-exposed-jobs-deteriorated-before-chatgpt/).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Research agents could perform more literature and replication work. Predoctoral jobs that teach coding and data preparation may change; student researchers must still understand identification and provenance. Demand could rise with cheaper applied analysis, but academic hiring and agency budgets remain separate drivers.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.

Published 2023-09-21; observation 2023 experiment; BCG consultants; multinational. randomized field experiment. Locator: Key findings. Limits: 758 consultants; 25%+ speed and 40%+ quality gains on in-frontier tasks; tasks outside frontier differ; no employment result.

Published 2026-08; observation 2021–2026 payroll panel; United States. observed employment. Locator: Figure C.11 and discussion. Limits: ADP payroll panel, occupation exposure and age; relative trends, not randomized treatment.
