# The Physics Undergraduate-to-Career Roadmap (USA, 2026 Edition)

## TL;DR
- **Physics is a "high-skill, narrow-funnel" field**: undergraduate and PhD training are rigorous and (at the PhD level) fully funded, but only roughly 10-20% of physics PhDs ever land tenure-track faculty jobs (one peer-reviewed estimate puts physics at 21% holding a tenure-track position 5-6 years post-graduation, vs. 14% in biology and 23% in chemistry). The economic payoff for most physicists now comes from leaving academia — for quantitative finance ($300k-$700k+ at top funds), tech/AI/data science ($130k-$350k+), national labs ($135k-$260k staff scientist), quantum computing ($130k-$400k), and the unusually stable, licensure-gated field of medical physics (~$150k-$250k+).
- **The single most important strategic fact**: a physics PhD is now mostly a launchpad into industry, not academia. Per AIP's *Physics PhDs Initial Employment* data (classes of 2020-21 and 2021-22), new PhDs split 46% academic / 34% private sector / 16% government / 4% other, with roughly half taking postdocs and half "potentially permanent" jobs — and ~73% of those permanent positions are in the private sector, which pays the most. The BLS median wage for physicists is $166,290 (May 2024), but that masks enormous spread by sector.
- **Hot fields and surprises to act on**: condensed matter (~20% of PhDs) remains the largest subfield and the frontier (moiré/twisted bilayer graphene, topological materials); the muon g-2 anomaly was resolved in 2025 (theory shifted to agree with experiment, not new physics); the quantum-computing job market is real but maturing off its hype peak; and finance + medical physics remain the two standout "value" destinations for physics talent.

## Key Findings

1. **Undergraduate core is highly standardized.** Across US BS programs (UChicago, Princeton, Yale, UCLA, Cornell), the required spine is: introductory mechanics → E&M → modern physics, then upper-division classical mechanics (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian), two semesters of E&M, two-to-three quarters of quantum mechanics, statistical/thermal physics, mathematical methods, and advanced/experimental lab. The "weeder" reputation attaches to upper-division E&M (Griffiths/Jackson), quantum mechanics, and classical mechanics.

2. **The PhD is fully funded; the master's usually is not.** PhD admission comes with a stipend (~$28k-$45k depending on institution/cost of living) plus full tuition waiver, financed by TA/RA appointments (Stony Brook's current 9-month TA stipend is $28,000 with PhD tuition fully covered). Standalone/terminal physics master's programs are typically NOT funded — students pay tuition. This is the single biggest financial-planning fact at the master's stage.

3. **Subfield distribution (AIP "Trends in Physics PhDs"):** condensed matter ~20% (largest, classes of 2017-2018); astronomy/astrophysics/cosmology ~13.5%; biophysics ~7.8% (notably more women); particles & fields (HEP) ~10%; AMO ~10-12%; nuclear ~6-8%; plus applied/optics/materials/computational.

4. **Career reality is bimodal.** Academia is a long tournament (2-3 postdocs, geographic immobility, ~10-21% tenure-track hit rate). Industry — finance, tech/AI, quantum, defense/aerospace, medical physics — absorbs the majority and frequently pays multiples of academic salaries.

5. **AI/ML is genuinely transforming parts of physics** (LHC data analysis, telescope-survey processing, ML-accelerated DFT, AlphaFold-adjacent structural biology) while being overhyped in others (it does not "discover new physics" autonomously; physics-informed neural networks and surrogate models are tools, not replacements for theory or experiment).

## Details

### 1. Undergraduate Foundation (Years 1-4)

**Core required courses (typical US BS).** Drawing on UChicago, Princeton, Yale, UCLA, and Cornell catalogs, the canonical sequence is:
- **Introductory sequence:** calculus-based mechanics; electricity & magnetism; waves/optics/thermal; "modern physics" (special relativity, intro quantum, Bohr model).
- **Upper-division core:** Classical/analytical mechanics (Lagrangian & Hamiltonian, central forces, oscillations — e.g., Thornton & Marion); Electromagnetism I & II (electrostatics through Maxwell's equations and radiation — Griffiths); Quantum Mechanics I-II(-III) (Griffiths, then applications — hydrogen atom, angular momentum, spin, perturbation theory); Statistical Mechanics & Thermodynamics (Schroeder); Mathematical Methods for Physicists (vector calculus, PDEs, special functions, linear algebra).
- **Lab spine:** introductory labs, electronics, and an advanced/experimental physics lab; many programs require a senior thesis or independent research.

**Weeder courses.** Upper-division E&M (the Griffiths → Jackson transition is the classic "wall"), Quantum Mechanics, and Classical Mechanics are the courses students are told to brace for; mathematical methods acts as a gatekeeper because it underpins all three.

**Lab & computational skills developed:** error/uncertainty analysis and statistical treatment of data; Python (NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Pandas) increasingly the default; MATLAB; Mathematica; LaTeX for writing; Jupyter notebooks; some C/C++ or Fortran in computational-physics courses (finite-element/PDE solvers). Over half of physics PhDs report computer simulation/modeling as a major dissertation tool (AIP).

**Foundational instruments:** oscilloscopes, function generators, lock-in amplifiers, vacuum systems, optics benches and lasers, spectroscopy, and basic electronics (diodes, op-amps, FETs, logic gates, ADC/DAC).

**High-value extracurriculars:**
- **NSF REU programs** — competitive paid summer research, typically ~$7,000 stipend for 10 weeks plus housing/travel (e.g., Cornell's astrophysics REU pays $7,000 + $1,250 housing/meals + up to $800 travel).
- **DOE SULI** (Science Undergraduate Laboratory Internships) — paid 10-week summer or 16-week semester internships at 17 national labs (Fermilab, SLAC, ORNL, PNNL, Argonne, PPPL, etc.); ~$650/week stipend; requires US citizenship/permanent residency and ≥3.0 GPA.
- **SPS** (Society of Physics Students), undergraduate research with a faculty member (the single highest-signal activity for PhD admissions), Physics GRE prep, and physics-olympiad/competition history.

### 2. Master's Stage (Years ~5-7)

**Program types:** (a) **thesis-based MS** (~2 years; coursework + research thesis; best for strengthening a PhD application via publications); (b) **course-based/non-thesis MS** (30-36 credits, ~1 year, comprehensive exam or capstone; career-oriented); (c) **Professional Science Master's (PSM)/MPS** (career-focused, internship/capstone, ties science to business/communication); (d) **MS "en route"** to a PhD (picked up after PhD coursework, usually no separate thesis); and (e) **terminal MS**.

**Funding norm — usually NOT funded.** Unlike fully funded PhD admission, terminal/standalone physics master's students typically pay tuition and are not guaranteed stipends. Stony Brook's department states master's students "are not guaranteed any financial support" and "do pay tuition," while "all students admitted to our Ph.D. program will receive financial support."\[1\] NYU similarly states it is "the responsibility of the Master student to arrange the financing of their studies."\[2\] The Council of Graduate Schools' GradSense notes assistantships "are more common for PhD study."\[3\] Course-based master's programs have a community reputation as departmental "cash cows."

**When a master's is worth it:** (1) to repair a weak/non-traditional undergrad record before reapplying to PhD programs (use a thesis program for research output); (2) to pivot from an adjacent field (e.g., engineering → physics); (3) as the **terminal professional credential** for industry tracks — medical physics, quantum, applied/computational physics. For strong, PhD-bound US students, the standard path is to skip the standalone master's and enter a funded PhD directly.

**Named professional master's programs worth knowing:**
- **Medical physics MS (CAMPEP-accredited)** — required for the radiation-oncology career track and American Board of Radiology eligibility. Named examples: Duke, Columbia (which explicitly states it offers no financial support), Washington University in St. Louis, Johns Hopkins, Vanderbilt.
- **Quantum / quantum-engineering / QISE master's** — a fast-growing category: USC Viterbi (MS in Quantum Information Science; 2025 reported alumni average salary ~$124,800), Columbia (MS Quantum Science & Technology), University of Arizona (MS Optical Sciences, QISE emphasis, tied to the NSF Center for Quantum Networks), UCLA (Master of Quantum Science & Technology), Stevens Institute (MS Quantum Engineering, graduates hired by IBM/Google/Amazon), University of Delaware (MS Quantum Science & Engineering).
- **Applied physics MS and computational physics MS** — offered broadly, often as concentrations within physics or applied-physics/applied-math departments (e.g., Columbia's APAM).

### 3. PhD / Advanced Research Stage (Years ~8+)

**What training looks like:** Unlike biology, physics PhDs rarely do formal lab rotations — students usually join a group directly. The structure is: 1-2 years of graduate core coursework (classical mechanics, E&M, quantum, statistical mechanics) → a **qualifying/comprehensive exam** (often completed by end of year 1-2; Pittsburgh requires passing by end of year 2) → dissertation research under an advisor and **thesis committee** → defense. **Time to degree** averages ~6 years (Pittsburgh cites 6.1 years; some finish in 4-5). Funding is the **fully funded model**: stipend + tuition waiver via TA/RA.

**Initial employment (AIP, classes of 2020-21 and 2021-22):** ~46% academic sector, ~34% private sector, ~16% government, ~4% other; roughly half take postdocs and half take "potentially permanent" positions, with ~14% leaving the US. About 72% of postdocs are in academia, while ~73% of "potentially permanent" positions are in the private sector — which pays the most (median ~$105k for earlier cohorts, very wide range); postdocs and academia pay least initially.

**Current research frontiers by subfield (2024-2025):**
- **Condensed matter (largest subfield):** moiré superlattices and magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (correlated insulators, unconventional superconductivity); twisted transition-metal-dichalcogenide bilayers (e.g., twisted MoTe₂) hosting the **fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect** and fractional Chern insulators (multiple 2024 *Nature* papers); topological materials (Weyl/Dirac semimetals, topological insulators); quantum spin liquids; high-Tc and unconventional superconductivity.
- **Astrophysics/cosmology:** JWST's revelation of surprisingly bright, massive galaxies at redshift z>10 (universe <400 Myr old), straining galaxy-formation models (JADES survey); the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA O4 run — LVK logged its **200th gravitational-wave candidate on March 19, 2025** ("in the first 23 months of O4, 200 GW candidates have been logged"), with O4 concluding Nov 18, 2025 at ~250 real-time candidates (cumulative advanced-era total ~390 confirmed events), including "mass-gap" objects; the pulsar-timing-array detection of the nanohertz gravitational-wave background; dark-matter direct detection; CMB-S4 (next-generation ground-based CMB survey, in development).
- **AMO:** optical atomic clocks at the **10⁻¹⁹** level — JILA/Jun Ye's strontium lattice clock reached a total systematic uncertainty of **8.1×10⁻¹⁹** (Aeppli, Kim, Warfield, Safronova & Ye, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 133, 023401, July 10, 2024 — "the lowest uncertainty of any clock to date"), and NIST's aluminum-ion clock reached the 10⁻¹⁹ level (2025), driving talk of redefining the SI second;\[4\] neutral-atom quantum computing (Atom Computing's >1,000-atom array; QuEra's 48-logical-qubit demo;\[5\] Pasqal ~1,000 trapped atoms);\[6\] NV-diamond and atom-interferometry quantum sensing.
- **HEP/particle:** the post-Higgs "precision and frontier" era — the **Muon g-2** experiment's final result (released June 3, 2025: aμ = 0.001165920705(114) at 127 ppb precision, surpassing the 140 ppb design goal) "agrees closely with a significantly revised Standard Model prediction" (per *CERN Courier*), so the long-standing anomaly "is therefore severely undermined" — the resolution came from improved theory (lattice QCD), not new physics; dark-matter searches (LUX-ZEPLIN/LZ set the world's best WIMP limits in Dec 2025, finding no WIMPs between 3 and 9 GeV/c²; XENONnT); the DUNE long-baseline neutrino experiment (Fermilab-hosted, science era ~2030+); discussion of a future muon collider.
- **Biophysics:** cryo-EM as the dominant structural method (PDB cryo-EM depositions rose from 67 in 2012 to 5,788 in 2024),\[7\] now coupled to AI (AlphaFold3, *Nature* 2024; ModelAngelo);\[8\] single-molecule techniques including high-speed AFM; active matter / non-equilibrium living-systems physics; mechanobiology.
- **Nuclear:** neutron-star/dense-matter equation of state via NASA's NICER X-ray pulse-profiling (PSR J0437−4715, mass 1.44 ± 0.07 M☉, 2024) combined with gravitational-wave tidal-deformability constraints; FRIB (Facility for Rare Isotope Beams, Michigan State) — the world's most powerful heavy-ion linac,\[9\] which discovered five new isotopes in 2024\[10\]\[11\] (thulium-182/-183, ytterbium-186/-187, lutetium-190; *PRL* 132, 072501) and reached 20 kW beam power in March 2025;\[12\] precision nuclear structure (shell-closure breakdown near N=20) and nuclear astrophysics.

**AI/ML reshaping physics — real vs. hype:** Genuinely transformative uses: deep learning for LHC event classification, jet tagging, anomaly detection, and detector simulation (the HEP-ML "Living Review," maintained by Feickert & Nachman, catalogs hundreds of papers); convolutional/graph neural networks for telescope-survey image processing; ML-accelerated density functional theory and interatomic potentials in materials/condensed matter; AlphaFold-class models in structural biophysics. The 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics (Hopfield & Hinton) recognized neural-network foundations. Overhyped/limited: AI does not autonomously "discover physics"; physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and surrogate models are useful accelerators but face accuracy and generalization limits and do not replace first-principles theory or experiment.

### 4. Tools, Software, and AI Adoption

**Standard tools by level:**
- **Undergraduate:** Python (NumPy/SciPy/Matplotlib/Pandas), MATLAB, Mathematica, LaTeX, Jupyter; oscilloscopes and lab electronics.
- **Graduate/research, by subfield:** **ROOT** and **GEANT4** (particle/nuclear physics data analysis and detector simulation); **VASP / Quantum ESPRESSO** (condensed-matter DFT); **LabVIEW** (experimental instrument control); molecular-dynamics (LAMMPS/GROMACS-type), Monte Carlo, and finite-element packages.
- **AI/ML stack:** **PyTorch / TensorFlow** for deep learning, **scikit-learn** for classical ML, plus **physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)**, **graph neural networks** (materials and particle reconstruction), **neural ODEs**, normalizing flows, and transformers/diffusion models increasingly appearing in physics papers.

**Honest practitioner view:** ML is now a standard analysis tool, not a novelty. It excels where there is abundant labeled/simulated data and a well-posed classification/regression/generation task (collider data, survey images, surrogate models for expensive simulations). It is weakest — and most over-sold — when promoted as a route to autonomous theory discovery or as a substitute for understanding; interpretability and out-of-distribution reliability remain open problems.

### 5. Career Paths and Salaries

**Anchor data point:** BLS median annual wage for **physicists** was **$166,290** (May 2024); 10th percentile ~$80,020, 90th percentile >$239,200. Astronomers' median was $132,170. Projected employment growth ~4% (2024-2034), ~1,800 openings/year for physicists and astronomers combined. These figures cover people formally classified as physicists/astronomers and understate the much larger flow of physics PhDs into finance, tech, and engineering job titles.

**Path 1 — Academic research (tenure-track professor).** Junior → senior: postdoc → assistant professor → associate (tenured) → full professor. **Reality: extremely competitive.** Historical IOP/NSF analyses put the share of physics PhDs reaching permanent academic research jobs at 10-20%; one peer-reviewed estimate finds 21% in a tenure-track position 5-6 years post-PhD. Two to three postdocs (each ~$55k-$70k) are now typical before a tenure-track offer, with severe geographic immobility. Per AIP's *State of the Academic Workforce* (Spring 2024 survey), nontenured positions held steady at 21% of physics-department faculty (and rose to 33% in astronomy departments, 2022-2024); per AAUP, only ~31.8% of all US faculty are now tenured/tenure-track. **Salary (AAUP 2024-25):** assistant professor commonly ~$80k-$110k; full professor averages ~$150k-$170k (men ~$168,927, women ~$147,375 at full rank), with substantial spread — public regional schools lower, elite private research universities higher ($200k+).

**Path 2 — National lab / government research (DOE labs, NIST, NASA).** Junior → senior: postdoc → staff scientist/research physicist → senior/distinguished scientist or group leader. **Reality: highly competitive but more stable than academia**; staff-scientist positions are coveted and a postdoc is still the standard entry. Funding is grant/appropriation-dependent (employee reviews flag funding volatility). **Salary (Glassdoor estimates):** Argonne "Scientist" averages ~$199,500 (range ~$155k-$261k); SLAC associate staff scientist averages ~$170k (range ~$136k-$215k); research physicists at the larger labs broadly ~$120k-$220k+.

**Path 3 — Quantum computing & technology.** Companies: IBM Quantum, Google Quantum AI, IonQ, Rigetti, PsiQuantum, Quantinuum, Microsoft, Atom Computing, QuEra, Pasqal. Roles: quantum hardware scientist, quantum software/algorithms engineer, error-correction researcher, quantum engineer. **Salary (2025-26):** broad $110k-$240k+ base; tech-giant total comp higher — Google Quantum AI ~$140k-$400k, IBM Quantum ~$130k-$350k, Microsoft Quantum ~$135k-$380k (base+equity); new PhD grads often start ~$140k-$180k; field median ~$165k. **Surprise/caveat:** despite headline claims of "180% job growth 2020-2024" (LinkedIn), the market is maturing and consolidating off its hype peak — many roles now demand specific skills (Qiskit/Cirq, cryogenics, QEC) and a real PhD; vendor "10,000-qubit by 2026"\[13\] claims are projections, not achievements.

**Path 4 — Finance & quantitative analysis ("Wall Street physics").** Heavily recruits physics PhDs. Firms: Citadel/Citadel Securities, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Renaissance, Jane Street, Hudson River Trading, Point72, Five Rings. Roles: quantitative researcher, quant developer, quant trader, risk analyst. **Salary (2025):** H1B data put average quant-researcher **base** ~$190k (NY ~$198k);\[14\] top shops pay flat $300k base (Five Rings, Jane Street).\[14\] Total comp with bonus routinely $300k-$700k+; Citadel QR total comp on Levels.fyi ranges ~$336k (L1) to ~$642k+ (L3), median ~$396k.\[15\] Fresh PhDs are commonly quoted $300k-$450k packages. **This is the single highest-paying mainstream destination for physics PhDs.**

**Path 5 — Technology / data science / AI / software.** Roles: data scientist, ML engineer, research scientist, software engineer. **Salary:** data scientist median ~$112,590 (BLS); ML engineer base commonly ~$128k-$186k, senior total comp regularly >$350k with equity at top firms; AI/ML research scientist roles at top labs among the highest paid. Data-scientist roles projected +34% (2024-2034, BLS). Physics PhDs are valued for modeling, statistics, and coding; a PhD is preferred for research-scientist roles but not required for applied ML/data science.

**Path 6 — Medical physics / radiation oncology.** Requires an MS or PhD in medical physics (CAMPEP-accredited), a 2-year CAMPEP residency, and American Board of Radiology certification. Junior → senior: resident → clinical/radiation-oncology medical physicist → chief/lead physicist. **Salary:** residents earn ~$60k-$72k (PGY-level; e.g., UF 2025-26 PGY1 $67,177 / PGY2 $69,444); board-certified clinical medical physicists earn well — job postings cite a median ~$252,700 for experienced therapeutic physicists, with the broad band ~$150k-$250k+. **Surprises:** (1) unusual career stability and high pay once licensed; (2) a severe **residency bottleneck** — CAMPEP graduate-program output has historically outpaced accredited residency slots roughly 3:1,\[16\] making residency the true competitive chokepoint, administered via the MedPhys Match.

**Path 7 — Defense, aerospace & national security.** Employers: Lockheed Martin, Raytheon (RTX), Northrop Grumman, Boeing, General Dynamics; DoD labs (DARPA, ARL, NRL); NNSA labs (Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, Sandia). Roles: systems engineer, research scientist, optical engineer, RF engineer. Usually requires US citizenship and often a security clearance (a durable career moat). **Salary (Levels.fyi):** Raytheon aerospace engineer ~$90k (P1) to ~$200k (P5); Lockheed aerospace engineer ~$99k (E1) to ~$154k (E4); senior technical/principal and clearance-gated roles commonly exceed $200k total comp. Stable, government-funded demand (hypersonics, stealth, space, directed energy).

**Path 8 — Science policy, education & communication.** Routes: AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships and partner-society Congressional fellowships (APS, AGU, etc.), OSTP, NSF/DOE program-officer roles, science journalism, museum/planetarium education, and high-school physics teaching. **Salary:** AAAS STPF stipends ~$75k-$100k (AAAS notes some PhD-experience tracks run ~$101,401-$131,826); other Congressional fellowships ~$78k-$87k; permanent policy/program-officer roles ~$70k-$120k+; K-12 teaching varies widely by state and district.

## Recommendations

**Undergraduate (Years 1-4):**
1. Lock in the core sequence early and treat E&M, quantum, and classical mechanics as the make-or-break courses; get tutoring/study groups before, not after, you struggle.
2. **Do research as early as possible.** By sophomore summer, apply to NSF REUs and DOE SULI (US citizens/permanent residents). One strong faculty research relationship + a poster/publication is worth more for PhD admission than a high GPA alone.
3. Build a real computational toolkit (Python scientific stack + Git + LaTeX). This is the most transferable skill regardless of which path you take.
4. **Decision threshold:** if by junior year you are excited by research and competitive for REUs, aim for a funded PhD. If not, plan toward an industry-facing master's or a BS-level technical role.

**Master's decision:**
5. Do **not** pay for a generic terminal physics MS if your goal is a research PhD — enter a funded PhD directly. Use a master's only to (a) repair your record (choose thesis-based), (b) pivot fields, or (c) earn a professional credential (medical physics, quantum, applied). For medical physics, confirm CAMPEP accreditation before enrolling.

**PhD and beyond — choose your subfield with the exit in mind:**
6. If you want the broadest, best-paying exits, favor subfields with strong industry pull: condensed matter, AMO/quantum, computational, and anything heavy in ML/data and instrumentation. HEP and astrophysics are intellectually thrilling but have the thinnest permanent-academic markets — plan explicitly for an industry exit.
7. **Pursue at most one postdoc on the academic track.** If you do not have a competitive tenure-track shortlist by the end of a second postdoc, pivot deliberately to national labs, finance, tech, or industry rather than drifting through a third.
8. **Match destination to temperament and constraints:** finance (highest pay, high intensity, no clearance needed), tech/AI (high pay, best work-life balance, abundant roles), medical physics (high stability + pay but residency-gated and licensure-bound — apply broadly in the MedPhys Match), defense/aerospace (stable, clearance is a moat, requires US citizenship), national labs (closest to academic research with more stability), policy/education (mission-driven, lower pay).

**Benchmarks that should change your plan:**
- If quantum-computing hiring continues to consolidate (watch QED-C workforce reports and startup funding), treat quantum roles as a subset of the broader tech/AI market rather than a guaranteed boom.
- If you can secure a security clearance and are a US citizen, defense/aerospace materially widens your stable-job options.
- For finance, target a top-tier PhD with strong coding and a publication record; the bar is rising as firms increasingly prefer "homegrown" talent over lateral hires.

## Caveats
- **Salary figures mix sources of differing rigor.** BLS (median wages) and AAUP (faculty) are authoritative; Glassdoor/Levels.fyi/Indeed/ZipRecruiter are self-reported and noisy (note the wildly divergent "Argonne average" figures across aggregators — from ~$52k to ~$200k depending on which roles are sampled). Quant-finance totals come partly from H1B filings (base only) and community/recruiter sources; treat top-end packages as attainable-but-not-typical.
- **Vendor and market-research claims about quantum computing** (qubit counts, "100,000 jobs by 2030," "10,000 qubits by 2026") are largely projections or marketing; achieved, peer-reviewed milestones are more modest. The same caution applies to AI-in-physics hype.
- **The ~10-21% tenure-track figure** is a range drawn from IOP/NSF/AIP-adjacent analyses and one peer-reviewed estimate, not a single official statistic; the precise number varies by cohort and definition. The directional conclusion (academia is a narrow funnel) is robust.
- **Subfield percentages** are AIP two-year averages (drawn largely from the 2017-2020 PhD classes) and shift modestly between survey classes; treat them as approximate.
- Figures for active-matter biophysics specifics were the thinnest-sourced element of this report and should be verified against a current review before heavy reliance.
