# Medicine (Pre-Med) Roadmap: Research

Date accessed for all sources: 2026-11-23. US pathway only (MD and DO).

## Provenance note

The roadmap JSX (`artifacts/medicine-premed-roadmap.jsx`) was built before this file existed, which broke the builder's rule that the JSX be populated only from a research file. This file was reconstructed afterwards from the same session's searches and fetches. Every JSX claim was then audited against it (see "Audit of the JSX"). Claims that no retained source supported were corrected, softened, or labelled. Where a figure came only from a search snippet, or from a commercial or advocacy site, the Sources table says so.

## TL;DR

- Medicine is a gated, credentialed ladder: 4 years college, 4 years medical school, 3 to 7 years residency, then optional fellowship. Arithmetic from the stage lengths gives 11 to 15 years before fellowship.
- Roughly 45% of MD applicants and about 63% of DO applicants are accepted somewhere (2025 cycle, per-applicant, not per-school).
- Only about 16.5% of students who intend pre-med finish college with the prerequisites done (one 102-school study).
- Step 1 is pass/fail, so Step 2 CK and research now carry the weight in the Match.
- The 2026 Match was the largest ever (44,344 positions offered, 93.5% filled), yet 2,862 positions went unfilled on Match Day.
- Federal borrowing for professional students is capped from July 1, 2026 ($50,000 per year, $200,000 total per the AMA), below published cost of attendance, so financing is now a gating factor.
- Pay is high but spread widely: Medscape 2026 average $386K; primary care $298K; orthopedics $611K. Doximity's 2026 data show 16 specialties above $500K.
- Most non-clinical salary figures (pharma, informatics, consulting, public health) rest on commercial or self-reported sites. Treat them as low confidence.

## Key Findings

- Funnel: about 52,400 MD applicants and 23,400 matriculants in 2025 (just under 45%). DO acceptance about 63%. Applicants applied to about 19.5 schools on average in 2025-26.
- Stats: 2025 MD matriculants averaged GPA 3.81 and MCAT 512.1; applicants averaged 3.67 and 506.3. One secondary source gives 3.79 for matriculant GPA (conflict noted below).
- Cost: class of 2026 median four-year cost of attendance about $297,745 (public) and $408,150 (private). Median debt for graduates who borrow is about $215K to $223K depending on source.
- Residency stipends (AAMC 2025 survey): PGY-1 $68,166; PGY-3 $73,301; PGY-8 $94,215.
- Workforce: AAMC projects a 2036 shortage of 13,500 to 86,000 physicians, but it is an advocacy body for more funded training slots. AAMC also models possible surpluses in some specialties (about 3,700 to 4,300), including emergency medicine and PM&R.
- AI: 81% of US physicians reported professional AI use in the AMA 2026 survey (38% in 2023). Ambient scribes reduce documentation time by about 16 minutes per encounter in one JAMA study, while a review notes high omission rates.

## Undergraduate (Pre-Med)

Prerequisite core (varies by school): one year each of general biology, general chemistry, organic chemistry, and physics, all with lab; plus biochemistry, statistics, and behavioral science (psychology/sociology) and English. Sources are mostly advising sites (S36 context, commercial); confirm each target school's list.

- Weeder reality: the 16.5% completion figure is from a 102-school study of 15,442 students, summarized by AdmissionsRx (S36). We did not verify where in the sequence attrition peaks.
- GPA mechanics: AMCAS computes cumulative and BCPM GPAs and includes all attempts of every course, so a retake adds a second grade instead of replacing the first (S1).
- Clinical hours and shadowing: commercial advising sites cite roughly 150 hours as a floor and 200 to 300+ as competitive, and 50 to 100 shadowing hours (S32, residencyadvisor, medicalaid). Low confidence, not an AAMC requirement.
- Gap years: about 27% of matriculants go straight through and the average matriculant age is about 24 (secondary, residencyadvisor and Shemmassian-type sources). Low confidence.
- Letters: typically 3 to 5 including science faculty, and a committee letter at schools that offer one (AAMC letter pages seen as search results only).
- MCAT: scored 472 to 528, four sections, about 7.5 hours seated, $355 for 2026 dates (S32). Physics is tested in the Chemical and Physical Foundations section, not as "a quarter of the exam". Behavioral science is one of four sections.
- Application: AMCAS (MD), AACOMAS (DO), TMDSAS (Texas). AMCAS fees $180 plus $48 per additional school for the 2027 cycle (S2, S32). Verification takes time and schools review on a rolling basis, so advisors recommend submitting early. The specific "June beats September" claim was removed (S2 gives timelines but no such comparison).
- Secondaries, situational-judgment tests (CASPer, AAMC PREview), and MMI interviews are used by many schools (S13, S14).
- MD-PhD/MSTP: 2,040 applicants against 54,699 MD applicants in 2025-26, which is about 27 times fewer (Shemmassian says "nearly 30"; 54,699 / 2,040 = 26.8). Programs run 7 to 8 years and are typically funded.

## Master's stage, reframed as Medical School (Years 5-8)

- Structure: MS1-MS2 preclinical, Step 1 (pass/fail since January 2022), MS3 core clerkships with NBME shelf exams, MS4 sub-internships, away rotations, and the Match.
- Step 2 CK is the main objective metric now. It is usually taken after the core clerkships (background knowledge, not separately sourced). The earlier JSX statement that it is "taken during clerkships" was corrected.
- DO students take COMLEX Levels 1 to 3, and many also take USMLE (S3). DO curricula add osteopathic manipulative medicine; the "about 200 hours" figure was not verified and was removed.
- Study tools: Anki, UWorld, and third-party resources. One survey study found 87.8% believed Anki significantly contributed to module success (S16). The claim that shared decks are "how most of a class learns" was not supported and was removed.
- POCUS and cadaver trends: seen in curriculum literature (S7); no specific percentage is carried into the JSX.
- Match 2026 (S12): 44,344 positions offered, 93.5% filled, more than 38,000 matched, 2,862 unfilled (389 more than 2025). NRMP reports 2,581 positions offered through SOAP (up 330). Lecturio gave 2,851 (S45), which conflicts; NRMP is primary, so 2,581 is used. Family medicine offered 5,491 positions, internal medicine 11,632, pediatrics 3,185 (94.4% filled).
- IMGs (S12): foreign-born IMGs needing visa sponsorship matched at 54.4% (five-year low); those not needing sponsorship at 67.9% (five-year high). The report states the figures, not the cause.
- Program signals (S4) are in the ERAS process.
- Financing: federal loan caps began July 1, 2026 (S19). AMA reports $50,000 per year and a $200,000 aggregate for professional students, a phase-out of Grad PLUS, and a transition period for students who borrowed before July 1, 2026. The AAMC counterpart page failed to load (S20), so the lifetime cap across all degrees ($257,500 in widely reported summaries) is not verified here.

## PhD / Research stage, reframed as Residency & Fellowship (Years 9-15+)

- Lengths (S44 and AMA): 3 years for family medicine, internal medicine, pediatrics; 3 to 4 for emergency medicine; 4 for anesthesiology, psychiatry, OB/GYN, neurology, pathology; PM&R listed as 3 to 4 depending on whether the preliminary year counts; 5 for general surgery, radiology, urology, orthopedics; 7 for neurosurgery.
- Fellowship typically adds 1 to 3 years, some longer. Ages: starting medical school at 24, graduation is about 28, residency completion about 31 to 35, fellowship completion about 32 to 38. This is arithmetic from the stage lengths, not a surveyed figure.
- Duty hours (S42): 80 per week averaged over four weeks, 24 hours plus up to 4 for transitions of care.
- Licensure: Step 3 is usually taken during intern year (S5) and is required for an unrestricted license. Interns practice on a training license. Licensure is per state; the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact eases but does not remove this (S18). Board certification is voluntary legally but effectively required by employers (S17).
- GME cap: Medicare-funded slots capped by the 1997 Balanced Budget Act; Congress added 1,200 in December 2020 (S24); AAMC advocates adding 14,000 over seven years (S23). This is AAMC's advocacy position, so it is attributed rather than stated as settled fact.
- Fellowship Match (NRMP SMS, S25): 15,846 applicants for 15,358 positions across 6,606 programs and 81 subspecialties for the 2026 appointment year, the largest in its 42-year history. Eight subspecialties filled under 50%.
- Physician-scientist route: NIH K awards (S6). About 91% of the physician-scientist workforce holds no PhD (S35, secondary citation). MD-PhD training time rose from about 6.7 to over 8 years (S34). In one national cohort (2000-2005 graduates), 52.4% held full-time faculty appointments; Shemmassian says "around 75%", which conflicts and is commercial.
- CDC EIS is a two-year fellowship (S47).
- Clinical informatics: ABMS subspecialty since 2011; from 2025 board eligibility requires an ACGME-accredited fellowship (S48, snippet).

## Tools and AI

- EHR burden: a 2016 Annals time-motion study found about two hours of EHR and desk work per hour of patient care (secondary mention only).
- Ambient scribes: 70% of UCSF physicians use them as of 2026 (S26, trade press). A 2026 JAMA study reports about 16 fewer documentation minutes per encounter (S27). A narrative review reports high omission rates and intermittent factual errors (S28).
- AI adoption: 81% of physicians reported professional AI use in the AMA 2026 survey (S9).
- Imaging AI: the FDA list of AI-enabled devices passed about 950 by 2024. The earlier JSX said "in 2024 alone", which is wrong because the figure is cumulative; the source is a vendor guide (S43).

## Career Paths

Salary figures are as reported by each source. Confidence is stated per path.

### 1. Clinical Practice

- Titles: Attending Physician, Hospitalist, Medical Director, Section Chief, Partner.
- Reality: once board-certified, employment is near-certain; the gate is training. Burnout has declined each year since its pandemic peak but remains about 10 points above pre-pandemic levels of about 40% (S37, blog-level source). The "49-hour average week" and "four straight years" claims were not supported and were removed. "Pay varies more within a specialty than between specialties" is an observation by a physician-finance writer quoted in a snippet, not a measured result.
- Salary: Medscape 2026 average $386K; primary care $298K; specialists $417K; orthopedics $611K; eight specialties above $500K (S8, S29).
- Confidence: high for Medscape figures (via Becker's fetch); medium for the burnout framing.

### 2. Surgical & Procedural

- Titles: Resident, Fellow, Attending Surgeon, Division Chief.
- Reality: competitive fields (dermatology, plastics, orthopedics, ENT, neurosurgery). Mean Step 2 CK for matched US MD seniors exceeded 250 in dermatology, plastics, orthopedics, and ENT (snippet, source not retained); neurosurgery was not in that list, so the JSX no longer attributes the 250 figure to it. The claim "none of these expanded in 2026" was not verified and was removed.
- Salary: orthopedics $611K (Medscape 2026). Doximity 2026 data (source URL not retained) list neurosurgery at $829,161 and 16 specialties above $500K. Methodologies differ, so Medscape's "eight above $500K" and Doximity's "16" are both reported.
- Confidence: medium (two commercial surveys, different methods).

### 3. Primary Care & Underserved

- Titles: Family Physician, General Internist, Pediatrician, Community Health CMO.
- Reality: widest door (family medicine 5,491 positions, internal medicine 11,632 in 2026). AAMC projects a primary care shortage of 20,200 to 40,400 by 2036 (advocacy source). NHSC loan repayment exists (S11). The claim that burnout in these fields is "top tier" and that pay is the "least of the clinical paths" was not verified; rewritten as "toward the low end".
- Salary: family medicine $288K; primary care average $298K (Medscape 2026). The "pediatrics and general IM in the high-$200Ks" claim was removed as unverified.
- Confidence: high for the two Medscape numbers.

### 4. Academic Medicine & Research

- Titles: Instructor, Assistant Professor, Physician-Scientist, Division Chief, Dean.
- Reality: MD-PhD is 7 to 8 years and typically funded. 52.4% of one 2000-2005 cohort held full-time faculty appointments (a conflicting commercial figure is "around 75%"). The JSX claim about a JCI Insight "lifetime earnings gap" was not found in retained sources and was removed.
- Salary: no verified average was retained. The JSX figure "~$208K" appeared only in the JSX and was removed.
- Confidence: low for salary; medium for pathway facts.

### 5. Pharma, Biotech & Medical Affairs

- Titles: Medical Science Liaison, Medical Advisor, Medical Director, VP Medical Affairs, CMO.
- Reality: MSL roles are open to PharmD and PhD holders as well as physicians (background knowledge, not fetched), so they are not a physician-only track. One non-clinical careers guide cites entry-level medical directors at $250K to $300K and advancement in 18 months to two years (S38). Layoff and pipeline risk is a general qualitative point.
- Salary: Glassdoor MSL average $210,090; GSK median MSL postings $248K (snippet); pharma medical director average $232,369 per ZipRecruiter (S39). All are commercial or self-reported. The "$750K+ with equity" claim was removed.
- Confidence: low to medium.

### 6. Clinical Informatics & Health Tech

- Titles: Physician Informaticist, Medical Director of Informatics, Associate CMIO, CMIO.
- Reality: ABMS subspecialty open to any primary specialty. Since 2025, board eligibility requires an ACGME-accredited fellowship (S48).
- Salary: physician informaticist average $217,445, top earners $300K+ (S40, a single secondary source). The "informatics fellows $132K to $238K" and "CMIO roles higher" claims were not supported and were removed.
- Confidence: low.

### 7. Public Health, Policy & Government

- Titles: EIS Officer, Medical Officer, Epidemiologist, Health Commissioner, FDA/NIH Leadership.
- Reality: EIS is a two-year CDC fellowship (S47); the class of 2024 selected 50 officers from 316 applicants. Typically paired with an MPH or preventive medicine residency (qualitative).
- Salary: no verified figures were retained. The JSX ranges ($150K to $250K; CDC "up to ~$300K") were not found in the sources and were removed. Federal pay depends on agency and grade.
- Confidence: low for salary.

### 8. Consulting & Health Ventures

- Titles: Associate, Engagement Manager, VP Strategy, Startup CMO, Partner.
- Reality: Glassdoor self-reports for McKinsey list associate median total pay of $243,387 in one snippet and $259,464 in another (S41), so they conflict. The JSX claim that compensation "starts near a specialist's" was wrong (the average specialist earns $417K); starting pay is closer to primary care. The claims that MBB firms run dedicated physician tracks and that most consultants leave within two to four years were not supported and were removed.
- Salary: McKinsey associate $243K to $259K (Glassdoor, self-reported). No verified figure for digital-health CMO roles.
- Confidence: low.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- US only. DO and MD pathways differ at the exam level (COMLEX vs USMLE).
- Conflicting figures: matriculant GPA 3.81 vs 3.79; median debt $215K vs $223,130; SOAP positions 2,581 (NRMP) vs 2,851 (Lecturio); MD-PhD faculty outcome 52.4% vs "around 75%"; Medscape 8 vs Doximity 16 specialties above $500K.
- Loan caps: AMA fetch confirms $50,000 per year and $200,000 aggregate. The lifetime cap and exact transition rules are not verified because the AAMC page did not load.
- Salary data come from self-selected surveys (Medscape, Doximity, Glassdoor); regional, employer, and practice-setting variation is large.
- State licensure, state loan repayment, and Match competitiveness shift by year.
- The AAMC shortage projection and GME-cap framing come from an advocacy organization.
- Step 2 CK timing, MSL composition, and several "background knowledge" statements were not separately sourced.
