# The Psychology Career Roadmap: Education, Frontiers, AI Risk, and Eight Career Destinations (2025–2026)

## TL;DR
- **Psychology is a long, competitive, but durable path:** the discipline is built on a quantitative scientific core (statistics, research methods, biopsychology) that "weeds out" students who expect a soft social science; licensure as a *psychologist* requires a doctorate (PhD/PsyD) in nearly every US state, while master's-level counseling licensure (LPC/LCSW/LMFT) is a faster, separate route.
- **AI is reshaping tasks, not eliminating the profession:** routine scoring, intake screening, scheduling, documentation, and manualized chatbot-CBT are automating fast (Woebot shut down its consumer app June 30, 2025), but complex assessment, trauma therapy, crisis work, supervision, and research leadership remain resilient — clinical/counseling psychologists are rated "Minimal Risk (10% probability of automation)" by WillRobotsTakeMyJob.com, versus 26% for school psychologists and 0% for clinical neuropsychologists.
- **Pay varies enormously by subfield and setting:** BLS May 2024 median for psychologists overall is $94,310; I-O is the highest-paid specialty, school psychology the lowest among the main tracks; UX research and I-O consulting offer the highest non-clinical ceilings, while academia is the most oversupplied and competitive destination.

## Key Findings

1. **The undergraduate degree is a foundation, not a destination.** A bachelor's in psychology qualifies graduates for entry-level roles (case manager, behavioral technician, research assistant, HR/market-research roles) but not for clinical practice. The conceptual backbone is set by APA-defined knowledge domains, and the hardest courses are quantitative.
2. **The master's stage forks sharply by subfield.** In clinical/counseling psychology a master's is usually a *bridge or stepping stone* (or a separate licensure path via counseling/social work); in I-O, school, and applied fields it is frequently *terminal and fully employable*; funding is the norm in PhD programs and rare in master's and PsyD programs.
3. **Doctoral training is a 5–7+ year, funded-but-grueling apprenticeship** culminating in the APPIC internship match — a known bottleneck. The PhD/PsyD split is research vs. practice, with meaningful differences in funding, match rates, and academic access.
4. **Research frontiers are increasingly computational:** computational psychiatry, network analysis of psychopathology, digital phenotyping, precision mental health, and (with a major regulatory setback) psychedelic-assisted therapy.
5. **AI's real disruption is administrative and adjunctive,** with hype outpacing clinical validation; AI-resilience comes from assessment expertise, complex/relational care, and computational literacy.

## Details

### 1. Undergraduate Foundation (Years 1–4)

**Core required courses / conceptual backbone.** US psychology majors are organized around the APA-defined knowledge domains, and the near-universal required sequence is: **Introduction to General Psychology** (the prerequisite gateway course), **Statistics / Quantitative Methods**, **Research Methods / Experimental Psychology**, and distribution requirements across the discipline's pillars — typically **Biopsychology/Behavioral Neuroscience, Cognitive Psychology, Developmental Psychology, Social Psychology, Personality, and Abnormal Psychology (Psychopathology)**. Programs (e.g., Rutgers, Ohio State, Columbia, Pitt, NYU) require a C or better in intro and statistics to declare or progress, and most cap the major with a senior seminar, capstone, or honors thesis. A typical major is ~10–12 courses / 33–40 credits.

**"Weeder" / notoriously hard courses.** The consensus hardest courses are the quantitative and biological backbone: **Statistics, Research Methods/Experimental Psychology, Biopsychology/Neuroscience, and Cognitive Psychology**, with Abnormal Psychology challenging for its volume of diagnostic detail. Statistics and research methods are repeatedly cited by students as the courses that "nearly killed" them, precisely because many enter expecting a non-mathematical major. Honors students who take graduate-level (500-level) statistics and advanced research methods rate those as the hardest of all.\[1\]

**Hands-on lab, clinical observation, research skills.** Undergraduates develop skills in experimental design, hypothesis testing, data collection and cleaning, APA-format scientific writing, and interpreting peer-reviewed literature. Lab courses (cognitive psych lab, social psych lab) and practicum/field placements (community psychology practicum, supervised field placement) build applied experience; clinical *observation* (not independent practice) may occur through volunteer placements at clinics, crisis lines, or human-services agencies.

**Foundational tools, software, methods.** The standard toolkit is **SPSS** (still dominant in undergraduate teaching), increasingly **R** (and RStudio) for reproducible analysis, plus **Excel**, **Qualtrics** for survey building, and exposure to Python in computationally oriented programs. Methods training spans descriptive and inferential statistics, correlational vs. experimental vs. quasi-experimental design, psychometrics, and research ethics/IRB procedures.

**High-value extracurriculars.** The single highest-leverage activity for graduate-school-bound students is **faculty-mentored research** (working in a lab, presenting at conferences, and ideally co-authoring publications) — clinical PhD programs weight research experience above clinical exposure. Other high-value items:
- **Psi Chi**, the International Honor Society in Psychology (founded 1929,\[2\] an APA/APS affiliate, 890+ chapters), offering ~$400,000+ annually in awards/grants,\[3\] a peer-reviewed journal, and a one-time lifetime membership fee (~$65–75). Eligibility typically requires a GPA in roughly the top 35% of class and ~3.0+ in psychology coursework.\[4\]
- **APA** (student affiliate via APAGS) and **APS** memberships; specialty societies such as **SIOP** (I-O) and **NASP** (school).
- Honors thesis, supervised research credit, conference poster presentations (regional/national), and relevant volunteering (crisis lines, teen drop-in centers, hospitals) — especially work with a target clinical population.

### 2. Master's Stage (Years ~5–7)

**Main program types and who each is for:**
- **Clinical/Counseling Psychology MA/MS:** Often a research-and-academic preparation degree or a *bridge* to doctoral study; generally does **not** by itself confer licensure as a psychologist.
- **Clinical Mental Health Counseling / Counseling (CACREP- or MPCAC-accredited):** A *terminal, licensure-track* degree leading to **LPC/LMHC**; this is the standard master's route to becoming a practicing therapist. (Note the CACREP vs. MPCAC accreditation split,\[5\] and that NBCC restricts the NCE exam to CACREP graduates.)
- **MSW (Social Work):** The largest mental-health field by headcount; leads to **LCSW**; more systems/social-justice oriented than empirical.
- **Marriage & Family Therapy (MFT):** Systems/family focused, leads to LMFT.
- **I-O Psychology MA/MS:** A strongly **terminal, employable** applied degree for talent, selection, org-development, and people-analytics roles.
- **School Psychology EdS/Specialist (60+ credits, 1,200 internship hours):** The **standard entry credential** — per NASP's 2020 Membership Survey (Goforth et al., 2021), "Approximately 74% of school psychologists have a specialist degree or certificate of advanced graduate degree; 9% a master's degree; and 17% a doctoral degree."
- **Generalist/Experimental/Developmental MA:** Often a research bridge to PhD.

**Funding norms.** Funded PhD programs (tuition remission + assistantship stipend) are the norm at not-for-profit research universities. **Master's programs typically expect students to pay tuition**,\[6\] and **PsyD programs rarely offer full funding** (aid is mostly federal loans, with assistantships covering only ~20–30% of students per one analysis).\[7\] For-profit and some online programs can be very costly and should be scrutinized for outcomes (internship match, licensure rates).

**Advanced skills introduced.** Psychometric assessment administration/scoring, structured/semi-structured diagnostic interviewing, evidence-based therapeutic modalities (CBT, DBT, ACT, exposure, IPT, motivational interviewing), treatment planning, multicultural competence, and supervised practicum hours. Counseling master's programs are practicum-heavy; research master's programs emphasize advanced statistics and a thesis.

**Terminal vs. bridge vs. skipped — by subfield:**
- **Clinical/counseling *psychology*:** master's is usually a **bridge** or is **skipped** entirely (many students go BA → funded PhD directly); to *practice as a therapist* at master's level, students choose the **counseling/MSW** route instead.
- **I-O:** master's is frequently **terminal** and highly employable.
- **School:** the EdS **specialist** degree is effectively the terminal entry credential.
- **Academic/research:** master's is often **bridged or skipped** (US PhD programs typically embed a master's en route).

### 3. PhD / Advanced Research Stage (Years ~8+)

**What doctoral training looks like.** APA-accredited doctoral programs require **at least 2,000 hours of supervised clinical experience**, including a full-time, year-long **predoctoral internship**. The arc: coursework (assessment, intervention, ethics, statistics) → practicum placements with supervised client contact (often starting Year 1–2) → comprehensive/qualifying exams → dissertation (original empirical research for PhD; a more applied doctoral project for many PsyD programs) → the **APPIC Match** for internship → licensure (EPPP exam + supervised hours + sometimes a jurisprudence exam) → optional postdoctoral fellowship and board certification (ABPP).

**The APPIC match bottleneck.** Internship is required to graduate, and historically a meaningful share of applicants go unmatched in a given year; a small number of programs (disproportionately some PsyD/for-profit programs) produce most unmatched applicants. PhD applicants have historically matched at higher rates than PsyD applicants. Geographically constrained students fare worse.

**Typical duration and funding.** PhD: ~5–7 years (some 4, some 8+); PsyD: ~4–6 years. PhD programs are commonly funded; PsyD programs commonly are not.

**PsyD vs. PhD distinction.** PhD = research-intensive ("scientist-practitioner" or "clinical science" model), full funding more common, lower acceptance rates, dissertation, best path to tenure-track academia. PsyD = practice-focused ("practitioner-scholar"/Vail model), more and earlier clinical hours, larger cohorts/higher acceptance rates, less funding, applied doctoral project. Both qualify for licensure as a psychologist.

**Admissions competitiveness.** Clinical psychology PhD admission is among the most competitive in all of graduate education. Acceptance rates for clinical programs commonly run in the single digits to low teens (some elite programs ~5%); ratios like 300 applicants for ~8 slots are typical. Successful applicants increasingly arrive with 2–3 years of research experience and peer-reviewed publications/presentations. (Master's programs and non-clinical PhDs are less selective.)

**Current cutting-edge research frontiers:**
- **Computational psychiatry:** uses data-driven/AI models (including reinforcement-learning and Bayesian models of cognition) and integrates neural data (fMRI) to understand mechanisms of disorders\[8\] and improve diagnosis/treatment.
- **Network analysis of psychopathology:** models disorders as networks of interacting symptoms (vector-autoregressive/idiographic models on time-series data)\[9\] rather than latent diseases — associated with Borsboom, Epskamp, and colleagues.
- **Digital phenotyping:** uses passive smartphone/wearable sensor data (GPS, accelerometry, typing, sleep) plus machine learning to predict symptom severity (e.g., depression, anxiety, schizophrenia relapse); active research platforms are in use by many research teams worldwide.
- **Precision / personalized mental health:** n-of-1 and predictive-model approaches to match patients to treatments.
- **Psychedelic-assisted therapy:** an active but **regulatorily stalled** frontier — the FDA **rejected** Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA-assisted-therapy application for PTSD in August 2024 (Complete Response Letter requesting an additional Phase 3 trial), prompting a ~75% workforce cut at Lykos; psilocybin (Compass Pathways' COMP360) research continues.

**How AI is reshaping research and practice.** On the research side, machine-learning classifiers (random forests, LSTMs), large-scale passive sensing, and LLM-based text analysis are now standard tools. On the clinical side, **AI ambient documentation scribes** (e.g., Nabla, DAX/Microsoft, plus behavioral-health-specific tools like Mentalyc, Blueprint, JotPsych, TherapyNotes' TherapyFuel) are the fastest-spreading real-world use, though a 2024–2025 multicenter RCT found only modest time savings (~16 minutes per 8 hours of care; ~9.5% note-time reduction for the Nabla tool) and high non-adoption.

### 4. AI and Job-Obsolescence Risk (Special Emphasis)

**Most vulnerable roles/tasks:**
- **Routine psychological test scoring and preliminary interpretation** (automated software).
- **Intake screening / initial history-taking** (chatbots, digital forms, triage algorithms).
- **Administrative tasks** — scheduling, billing, documentation/progress notes.
- **Manualized, low-acuity CBT delivery** via chatbots/apps for mild-to-moderate symptoms.
Estimates cited in the trade/education literature suggest roughly 30–40% of psychology-adjacent *tasks* could be automated by ~2030 — task-level, not job-level.

**Most resilient roles/tasks:**
- **Complex trauma therapy and high-acuity/relational care** (therapeutic alliance).
- **Neuropsychological assessment** (integrating history, behavior, and nuanced test interpretation) — rated 0% automation probability by WillRobotsTakeMyJob.com.
- **Crisis intervention and risk management.**
- **Clinical supervision, research leadership, and program design.**
- **Ethical judgment, cultural sensitivity, and adaptive case formulation.**
The healthcare sector is widely characterized as among the most AI-resilient because of the "empathy factor," and clinical/counseling psychologists are rated "Minimal Risk (10% probability of automation)."

**Current state of AI in mental health.**
- **Woebot** (rules-based CBT chatbot) **shut down its consumer app on June 30, 2025**. Per STAT News (July 2, 2025), "Roughly 1.5 million people used Woebot"; founder/CEO Alison Darcy said the shutdown was "largely attributable to the cost and challenge of fulfilling the Food and Drug Administration's requirements for marketing authorization," made more pressing by the rise of LLMs the FDA has not yet figured out how to regulate. Woebot raised $124M total (including a $90M Series B/C in 2021) and earned FDA Breakthrough Device Designation in 2021 for its postpartum-depression tool WB001; it has pivoted to a B2B/enterprise model.
- **Wysa** (CBT/DBT/mindfulness; FDA Breakthrough Device designation; NHS partnership) pivoted to a **hybrid "Copilot" model** integrating licensed clinicians.
- **Therabot (Dartmouth)** RCT in *NEJM AI* (Heinz, Mackin et al., March 27, 2025; N=210, 106 Therabot vs. 104 waitlist control) reported "a 51% reduction in major depression symptoms, a 31% reduction in generalised anxiety symptoms, and a 19% reduction in eating-disorder risk" at 8-week follow-up — but it was an expert-fine-tuned research model with clinical oversight, not a consumer product.
- Overall, systematic reviews find **small-to-moderate effect sizes** (≈0.2–0.6) for chatbot interventions vs. ≈0.8–1.0 for in-person CBT, with short trials and company-funded research limiting confidence.

**AI-resilience skills/specializations.** Assessment and neuropsychology expertise; complex/specialized clinical work (trauma, personality, serious mental illness, child); supervision and leadership; **computational/data-science literacy** (R/Python, ML, psychometrics) to *build and validate* tools rather than be displaced by them; and AI governance/ethics expertise.

**Honest practitioner perspective — hype vs. reality.** The dominant expert consensus (APA, World Economic Forum 2025 Future of Jobs, practitioner literature) is that **AI will not replace licensed psychologists** and that care-economy jobs will grow, but that it *will* change workflows and that uncritical reliance carries real risks. A 2026 *Frontiers in Psychology* analysis warns of **automation bias, cognitive offloading, "moral deskilling," and erosion of clinical judgment** when practitioners over-trust AI — even experienced clinicians are vulnerable under high cognitive load. The realistic picture is augmentation and task-shifting, with genuine displacement risk concentrated in low-acuity self-help and administrative niches.

### 5. Career Paths — Eight Destinations

> **Licensure note (applies throughout):** Independent practice as a "psychologist" is a legally protected title requiring a **doctorate** (PhD/PsyD), supervised hours, and the **EPPP** in nearly all US states. Master's-level clinical practice uses separate licenses — **LPC/LMHC, LCSW, LMFT** — with their own exams and supervised-hour requirements. Salary figures are 2024–2025, primarily BLS OEWS May 2024 (psychologists overall median **$94,310**; mean ~$106,850; range ~$54,860 at the 10th percentile to ~$157,330 at the 90th).

**(a) Clinical / Counseling Psychology (private practice, hospitals, CMHCs).**
- *Titles:* (junior) postdoctoral fellow, staff psychologist → (senior) licensed clinical psychologist, clinical director, practice owner.
- *Licensing:* doctorate + EPPP + supervised hours; ABPP optional.
- *Competitiveness:* doctoral admission very competitive; strong job demand thereafter (BLS projects ~11% growth for clinical/counseling psychologists 2024–2034).
- *Salary:* BLS clinical/counseling median ~$96,100 (top 10% over ~$144,260). Private practice with a full caseload typically grosses ~$150,000–$200,000 and nets ~$100,000–$160,000 after overhead, with session rates ~$150–$300; building a full caseload takes 1–3 years.

**(b) Academic Research / Tenure-Track Professor.**
- *Titles:* (junior) postdoc, assistant professor → associate professor (tenured) → full professor; department chair.
- *Licensing:* none for pure research; PhD required; clinical faculty may also be licensed.
- *Competitiveness:* **the most oversupplied destination.** Per Rose (1972), cited in Nature's Social Sciences Community, "In 1970, there were around 2-3 psychology PhD graduates per assistant professor of psychology vacancy…yet in 2017 that number is around 8." Roughly **1 in 3 applicants land a tenure-track job on their first market attempt**, rising to ~50% by the third attempt (SPSP technical report). Publications and mentorship/"fit" are decisive; 20–30% of faculty come from the top 10 programs. The market has shifted heavily toward contingent/adjunct positions.
- *Salary:* BLS median for postsecondary psychology teachers (SOC 25-1066) is **$80,330** (May 2024); per AAUP's 2024–25 survey, full-time faculty salaries rose 3.8% nominally but real salaries remain ~6.2% below fall 2019.\[10\]\[11\] Assistant-professor starting salaries at research universities commonly run ~$80,000–$90,000, full professors well into six figures at R1s (e.g., ~$119,000–$128,000 in AAUP institutional examples); adjuncts are paid roughly $3,000–$6,000 per course section.

**(c) Industrial-Organizational (I-O) Psychology.**
- *Titles:* (junior) people-analytics analyst, talent-management/assessment consultant → (senior) director of org development, head of people analytics, principal consultant, independent consultant.
- *Licensing:* generally **none** (I-O is non-clinical); master's is employable, PhD raises the ceiling.
- *Competitiveness:* small field, so competition for the best jobs is keen, but demand is solid; quantitative/data-science skills are the differentiator.
- *Salary:* the **highest-paid psychology specialty.** BLS figures are cited inconsistently across the May 2023 and May 2024 surveys — the widely repeated **$139,280 median\[12\] appears to be May 2023, while the May 2024 OEWS median is ~$109,840**\[13\] (25th percentile ~$80,790; 75th ~$198,170; top 10% over ~$210,830 in the 2023 data). SIOP's 2022 Income & Employment survey (2021 data, >1,200 respondents) shows practitioners out-earning academics by ~19.5%, doctoral independent consultants with the highest median (~$350,000), and master's-level tech-industry practitioners around $121,729; doctoral-level I-O psychologists earn roughly $40,000–$70,000 more on average than master's-level peers.

**(d) School Psychology.**
- *Titles:* (junior) school psychologist (EdS) → (senior) lead/supervising school psychologist, district coordinator, NCSP-credentialed specialist.
- *Licensing:* state department-of-education credential; **EdS/specialist (60+ credits, 1,200 internship hours)** is the standard entry degree; the optional **NCSP** (NASP) credential is recognized in many states and can add $2,000–$5,000 stipends.
- *Competitiveness:* **a candidate's market** — per the NASP State Shortages Dashboard, "The national ratio for the 2024-2025 school year is 1071 students to 1 school psychologist," versus NASP's recommended 500:1, producing signing bonuses and loan-forgiveness opportunities.
- *Salary:* BLS median **$87,550** mean / ~$84,940–$87,550 median (May 2024); top 10% over ~$131,470; high-cost states pay most (California state median ~$118,310, Oregon mean ~$113,180).\[14\] Most work 10-month contracts with summers available for additional assessment income.

**(e) Neuropsychology / Health Psychology.**
- *Titles:* (junior) postdoctoral neuropsychology fellow → (senior) clinical neuropsychologist, director of neuropsychology, forensic neuropsychology consultant.
- *Licensing:* doctorate + **2-year postdoctoral fellowship** (Houston Conference guidelines) + EPPP; board certification (ABPP-CN/ABCN) increasingly expected and worth ~10–25% higher pay.\[15\]
- *Competitiveness:* long training pipeline (often 10+ years post-bachelor's) but strong demand in hospitals, rehab, and aging care.
- *Salary:* neuropsychologists fall under BLS "Psychologists, all other" (median ~$117,580); specialty sources estimate medians ~$120,000–$130,000, with private practice $150,000–$250,000+\[15\] and forensic/expert-witness work adding $50,000–$150,000.\[16\]

**(f) Forensic Psychology.**
- *Titles:* (junior) forensic evaluator, correctional psychologist → (senior) expert witness, forensic consultant, court/agency director.
- *Licensing:* doctorate + licensure; ABPP-Forensic optional but valuable in court (requires ~100 forensic cases / 1,000 hours post-licensure).
- *Competitiveness:* niche; federal (FBI, BOP) and consulting roles are competitive; expert-witness work is lucrative but reputation-dependent.
- *Salary:* BLS "Psychologists, all other" median **$117,580** (2024); specialty estimates ~$101,000–$115,000 base, with expert testimony\[17\] at $150–$450/hour adding $50,000–$150,000/year.

**(g) UX Research / Behavioral Science in Tech.**
- *Titles:* (junior) associate/junior UX researcher → (mid) UX researcher → (senior) senior/lead/principal UX researcher, research manager, head of research; also "behavioral scientist," "people analytics," "quantitative researcher."
- *Licensing:* none; bachelor's/master's sufficient; PhDs (psychology, HCI, cognitive science) are common and can raise pay 10–25%.
- *Competitiveness:* attractive pay but **volatile** — ~21% of UX researchers reported layoffs in 2025, and AI tools now assist ~80% of researchers, raising the bar.
- *Salary:* roughly $93,000–$196,000 depending on level/location (Glassdoor median total ~$119,000); juniors ~$100,000–$140,000 and seniors ~$140,000–$180,000+, with top firms (Meta median ~$212,000, Google ~$202,000) far higher; major tech hubs pay large premiums.

**(h) Government / Policy / Public Health / Consulting.**
- *Titles:* (junior) staff psychologist (VA/DoD), research psychologist, program analyst → (senior) chief of mental health, program director, policy advisor, GS-13/14/15 psychologist.
- *Licensing:* clinical roles require doctorate + licensure; policy/research roles vary.
- *Competitiveness:* stable, strong benefits, loan-repayment programs; the **VA is the largest single employer of psychologists.**
- *Salary:* federal psychologists span roughly **GS-11 to GS-15**; VA clinical psychologist averages ~$127,000 (Glassdoor estimate), commonly ranging ~$103,000–$167,000 base\[18\] plus locality pay (33%+ in the DC area),\[19\] and reaching ~$200,000+ at senior levels. GS-13–15 generally falls ~$96,000–$172,000 before locality adjustments.

## Recommendations

**For prospective/early undergraduates (Years 1–2):**
- Treat **statistics and research methods as the make-or-break courses** — front-load them, and don't expect a math-free major. Aim for a B+ or better; these grades and skills gate both graduate admission and AI-resilient careers.
- **Get into a research lab by sophomore year.** Research experience (ideally a poster or co-authored paper) is the strongest predictor of funded-PhD admission and is more valued than clinical hours even for clinical programs.
- Join **Psi Chi** and a relevant specialty society (SIOP, NASP, APAGS) once eligible.

**For students choosing a graduate path (Years 3–5):**
- **Decide your end-state first**, because it dictates the degree:
  - Want to *do therapy soon* and minimize debt → **CACREP counseling master's (LPC) or MSW (LCSW)**, ~2–3 years.
  - Want the *psychologist title, assessment authority, and highest clinical ceiling* → **funded clinical/counseling PhD** (or PsyD if you can manage funding and prioritize practice).
  - Want *corporate/non-clinical high pay* → **I-O master's or PhD**, or pivot toward **UX/behavioral science**.
  - Want *stable demand and good work-life balance* → **school psychology EdS** (currently a candidate's market).
- **Prioritize funded, APA-accredited programs**; scrutinize PsyD/for-profit programs on internship-match and licensure rates before enrolling. Avoid large debt for low-funding programs.

**For doctoral students and early-career psychologists:**
- **Build AI-resilient specialization:** neuropsychological/complex assessment, trauma and high-acuity care, supervision, or research leadership — plus **computational literacy (R/Python, ML, psychometrics)** so you can build and validate AI tools rather than be displaced.
- **Adopt AI as augmentation, not authority:** use ambient-scribe documentation tools to cut administrative load, but guard against automation bias and maintain independent clinical judgment and proper consent/HIPAA practices.
- Pursue **board certification (ABPP)** in assessment-heavy specialties (neuropsychology, forensic) where it materially raises pay and credibility.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that should change the plan:**
- If you **dislike research**, do not pursue a PhD — choose a PsyD or master's licensure route.
- If you **cannot relocate**, weigh the APPIC-match and academic-job geographic risk heavily, and favor school psychology or master's-level licensure.
- If **psychedelic-assisted therapy** gains FDA approval (watch the required additional MDMA Phase 3 trial and Compass's psilocybin program), a new credentialed niche may open — but do not bet a career on it before approval.
- If a target subfield's **AI-automatable task share** (intake, scoring, low-acuity CBT) is most of the job, treat it as a transitional role and build toward complex/relational or computational work.

## Caveats
- **Salary data conflicts and lag.** The I-O figure is genuinely inconsistent across sources: **$139,280 is most likely the BLS May 2023 median, while May 2024 is ~$109,840** — this small-sample occupation (≈5,600 workers) produces volatile estimates; verify the live BLS figure before relying on it. SIOP's most recent public survey is the 2022 report (2021 data). School-psychology and "Psychologists, all other" medians are reported in slightly different forms ($84,940 vs. $87,550; $117,580) across BLS vintages and aggregators.
- **Geographic variation is large** — California, New Jersey, Colorado, Oregon, Maryland, and DC pay well above national medians, but cost-of-living offsets much of the premium; federal locality pay adds 18–33%+ in high-cost metros. Telehealth is modestly compressing geographic pay gaps.
- **Forensic and neuropsychology salaries are estimates,** because BLS lumps them into "Psychologists, all other"; private-practice/expert-witness income varies widely and is reputation-dependent.
- **Aggregator sites (PayScale, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, and education-marketing sites)** rely on self-reported or modeled data and should be treated as lower-confidence than BLS, AAUP, NASP, and SIOP primary sources.
- **AI claims are fast-moving.** Effect sizes, product availability (e.g., Woebot's pivot, Wysa's hybrid model), and regulatory status can change within months; the figures here reflect 2024–early 2026 reporting.
- **Licensure rules vary by state** and change; always verify current requirements with the specific state board and accreditation body (APA, CACREP, MPCAC, NASP) before committing.
