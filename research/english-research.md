# English (Literature, Rhetoric, and Writing): Research Notes for the Field Roadmap

**Field:** English, the US bachelor's in English (literature, rhetoric and writing, creative writing) and the careers it opens (US-centered)
**Compiled:** 2026-11-23
**Companion artifact:** `artifacts/english-roadmap.jsx` (corrected on 2026-11-23 to match this file)

## Provenance note

This file was written after the JSX, not before it. The artifact was built earlier in the same working session from a first pass of seven searches (curricula, MA versus MFA, the PhD job market, AI and writing jobs, BLS salaries, UX writing pay, and English and law school). The builder instructions require a research file before any code, and that file was skipped. The JSX also carried claims none of those searches supported. On 2026-11-23 the research was extended with 27 further calls (19 page fetches and 8 searches), and every claim in the artifact was checked against the combined sources. The section "Audit of the JSX" lists each claim that was contradicted, unsupported, or out of date, and what was changed.

Source codes (S1, S2, ...) point to the Sources table at the end, which gives the URL, the date on the source where one is shown, and the date accessed (all 2026-11-23). "Seen as" says whether a page was fetched or only read as a search excerpt. A fetch returns a model-extracted summary of the page, so figures from fetches should be rechecked on the page before they are quoted anywhere important. Snippet-only figures deserve the same second look. Sources of low reliability (anonymous advice, aggregators, university program marketing) are marked "low". The roadmap uses them only for descriptive statements, never for figures.

## TL;DR

- The English major centers on close reading, writing, a required methods or theory course, and distribution across historical periods. Requirements differ by school (S1 to S8).
- Graduate routes split by goal: the literature MA (a PhD bridge), the MFA (terminal, craft-focused, some fully funded), and writing and editing programs. No earnings or placement data for standalone MAs turned up (S9 to S11, S41, S42).
- The tenure-track market is small and uneven. In 2022 there were about 1,144 new English PhDs and 469 advertised assistant-professorships (S12). The MLA counted 911 English ads in 2022-23, 61.8% of them tenure-track (S20).
- Subfield matters more than almost anything else: at Ohio State, 77% of Writing, Rhetoric, and Literacy PhDs (17 of 22) got tenure-track jobs in 2018-22, against 17% of literature PhDs (10 of 58) (S13). One program, small samples.
- BLS May 2025 medians: writers and authors $76,910; technical writers $90,390; editors $77,920; public relations specialists $74,750; English postsecondary teachers $78,760; high school teachers $72,040 (all subjects); lawyers $159,670 (S22 to S28).
- Projected 2025-35 growth is flat or negative for most writing occupations (writers and authors 0%, editors -1%, technical writers 1%), flat for English postsecondary and high school teaching (0%), 2% for public relations, and 5% for lawyers (S22 to S28).
- AI: practitioners report AI drafting by default in technical writing, with writers shifting to review (S35). Teaching guidance says AI detectors are unreliable (S43). The most dramatic job-loss statistics found came from an aggregator with no primary sourcing and are not used (S36).
- The claim that English majors dominate the LSAT holds for one law school in one year (Kentucky, fall 2014, average 162). LSAC warns against reading major averages as proof that a major prepares students better (S38, S39).

- **The BLS pages changed between data years.** First-pass excerpts (May 2024) gave writers and authors $72,270 with 4% growth, technical writers $91,670 with about 56,400 jobs, editors $75,260 with 1% growth and about 115,800 jobs, public relations specialists $69,780 with 5% growth, and postsecondary teachers $83,980. The 2025 pages show lower growth projections and lower employment counts for technical writers and editors. The pages read do not explain the shifts, so no cause is claimed.
- **PhD market, volume.** For 2022 there were about 1,144 new English PhDs and 921 advertised English jobs, 469 of them assistant-professorships (S12). The MLA counted 911 English ads in 2022-23, down 1.1% from 921, with the tenure-track share up from 52.2% to 61.8% (S20). A 2015 study found that over the prior 35 years fewer than half of English PhDs landed tenure-track jobs on graduation (S18).
- **PhD market, subfield (Ohio State).** From 2018 to 2022, 77.3% of Writing, Rhetoric, and Literacy PhDs (17 of 22) and 17.2% of literature PhDs (10 of 58) reached tenure-track jobs; folklore was 0 of 7. From 2001 to 2022 the rates were 79.7% (55 of 69) and 53.4% (111 of 208). From 2009 to 2017 they were 76.0% (19 of 25) and about 62% (S13). The page's chart text says 78% for 2001-22 but its notes compute 79.7%, so the notes figure is used.
- **PhD market, other programs.** Ohio State's 2018-22 overall tenure-track rate was 31%, against 30% at UT Austin, 28% at Michigan, and 32% at Maryland (S13). Duke's three-year tenure-track placement was about 80% for 2000-2015, 44.4% (4 of 9) for the 2021-22 cohort, and about 26% (5 of 19) for graduates from fall 2021 to summer 2024 (S14). Penn reports a six-year placement rate of 64% (44 of 69 for 2010-2016 graduates), counting only first-time tenure-track jobs (S16).
- **Prestige gates placement.** A 2015 study found top-10 programs gave students a reasonable chance at tenure-track jobs at research universities and top liberal-arts colleges, while chances from lower-ranked programs were close to nil. It also noted that published data do not capture attrition (S18).
- **Contingent pay.** Adjuncts are generally paid $3,000 to $7,000 per class and rarely get health care (S17). A 2019 AFT survey found over 30% of non-tenure-track educators in higher education earned under $25,000 a year (S17).
- **Time to degree.** Median time to degree for humanities and arts PhDs was 6.8 years in 2020 and has been 6.8 years or longer every graduation year from 2003 to 2020. The broader "Letters" grouping (English and similar fields) was 6.7 years in 2007 (S21). No English-only figure was reported.
- **MFA funding.** Michigan lists a full tuition waiver, a $23,000 stipend, $5,000 of summer funding, and health care. UT Austin's Michener Center lists a $29,500 fellowship plus tuition (S41). Arizona gives all accepted MFA students three years of full funding through a teaching assistantship with a stipend of about $22,500 (2025-26) and three courses taught per year (S42). Iowa lists financial assistance for all students but gives no figure (S41). No acceptance rates or unfunded-program tuition figures were found.
- **AI and writing work.** A 2026 practitioner write-up says AI now creates first drafts by default in technical writing and humans review and correct them, and that senior writers act as editors and curators (S35). A 2025 UX content survey found 58% of respondents said AI had improved their work (S29). A site tracking technical-writing roles forecasts a 2028 "AI editor-in-chief" role and lists a March 2025 Canva layoff of 10 of 12 technical writers, but it is an aggregator and the layoff was not confirmed at a primary source, so it is not used (S34).
- **LSAT.** At the University of Kentucky College of Law, English majors in the fall 2014 entering class averaged 162, the highest among arts and sciences majors, and above that law school's 75th percentile (S38). LSAC's applicants-by-major page states that its data do not show any major better prepares students for the LSAT or law school. The retrievable part of that table stopped at "Art History", so no English row was seen (S39).

## Undergraduate

### Curriculum

- **Methods gateway.** Columbia requires "Approaches to Literary Study" for every major and urges taking it in the first or second year, though it is not a prerequisite for most courses (S8). UW requires English 202 and then 302 "Critical Practice" (S3). Stanford requires coursework in methodology (S1).
- **Surveys and distribution.** Stanford's core is team-taught and runs from the Middle Ages to the present (S1). Columbia requires two courses on literature before 1700, with only one on Shakespeare allowed to count (S8). NYU's literature track requires one course each in Critical Theory, Genre, Difference and Representation, pre-1640, 1640 to 1900, and post-1900 literature (S6). USC requires two courses before 1800 and two from 1800 on, plus two interpretive-lens courses (race, gender, and sexuality; genre and media) (S7). UW requires 15 credits in each of three areas (S3). UNC has a three-course core and seven concentrations including creative writing and film (S4).
- **Capstone and honors.** UW, NYU, and USC end the major with a capstone or senior seminar (S3, S6, S7). UMD has an honors program built around a sustained research or creative project (S5).
- **Tracks.** UMD offers literary and cultural studies, creative writing, media studies, and language, writing, and rhetoric tracks (S5). Stanford offers a philosophy-and-literature track and a data-science track (S1).
- **Foreign language.** Cornell's guide says most PhD programs require reading knowledge of at least one foreign language, usually French or German, sometimes two or more, and some require Latin (S2).

### Skills

- UMD lists close reading, writing across literary, rhetorical, linguistic, and media genres, selecting and crediting print and digital sources, and situating texts in their time and tradition as learning outcomes (S5).
- Kentucky alumni quoted by the department say close reading carried over to the LSAT and to law school (S38).

### Tools and extracurriculars

- The Iowa English research guide names JSTOR, Project MUSE, the MLA International Bibliography, and ABELL as core databases, and lists digital humanities journals (DHQ, Journal of Digital Humanities, Digital Studies). It names no software tools (S45).
- Cornell notes that many majors write for campus literary magazines and attend department lectures and readings (S2). UMD allows up to six credits of internship (ENGL 388) toward the major (S5). USC majors can study Anglophone literature abroad, for example at Queen Mary University of London, and many double major (S7). UMD offers an English and Secondary Education double major of 125 credits leading to a grades 7 to 12 teaching certificate (S5).
- BLS says experience from school newspapers, internships, and writing such as blogging helps writers and authors (S22, first-pass excerpt).

### Not found

- Which English courses are hardest. The first JSX named critical theory, poetry scansion, and Old and Middle English without a source, so those claims were removed.

## Master's

- **MA in literature.** Described as coursework in literary analysis, theory, and linguistics (S10), and as the stronger credential for PhD admission and literature teaching in an anonymous advice source (S9, low). BLS says a master's can be enough for some community-college teaching jobs (S26).
- **MFA.** Terminal, craft-focused, workshop-based, with a creative thesis (S10, S11). Funding examples are in Key Findings (S41, S42). Arizona's assistants complete a ten-day training and a first-semester preceptorship before teaching (S42).
- **Writing and editing programs.** Master's programs in writing often add editing, publishing, and pedagogy electives (S10, S11). USC offers a progressive degree in Literary Editing and Publishing that earns a BA and MA in five years (S7). BLS says technical writers may benefit from a background in engineering or science (S23, first-pass excerpt).
- **Rhetoric and composition.** The strong placement figures found belong to the PhD track at one program (S13). No master's-level outcome data was found.
- **Not found.** Earnings or placement data for standalone MAs, MFA acceptance rates, and unfunded tuition figures.

## PhD / Research

- **Structure.** Brown requires 13 courses (six in year one, five in year two, two in year three), a language requirement, and a qualifying exam by April 15 of year three that combines a field essay of up to 2,500 words with a two-hour oral exam before three faculty. The dissertation starts after a proposal and first chapter are approved and ends with a defense before three faculty (S46). Notre Dame requires 14 courses within a roughly five-year plan, proficiency in two languages other than English (or fluency in one), exams before Thanksgiving of year three, and a prospectus defense in spring of year three, and it runs a job-search practicum in year five (S47).
- **Funding and teaching.** Brown guarantees six years: a fellowship in year one, teaching assistantships in years two, three, five, and six, and a dissertation fellowship in year four (S46). Notre Dame assigns service in years two to four, none in year five, and offers an optional sixth year (S47).
- **Duration.** See Key Findings (S21).
- **Job market and alternatives.** See Key Findings (S12 to S20). Duke runs an annual colloquium called "Translating the PhD", and its recent graduates include secondary teachers, university staff, and nonprofit and private-industry workers (S14). NYU's placement list includes a senior policy analyst at a think tank and an English teacher at a New York school (S15).
- **Research areas.** Bristol's English department lists Race and Ethnicity, Gender and Sexuality, Literatures of the Global South, Material and Digital Texts, Literature and the Environment, Spatial Humanities, Poetry and Poetics, and Literature, Science and Medical Humanities (S44, a UK department). NYU's list includes a medical-humanities postdoc and a Mellon postdoc in climate and inequality (S15). Stanford has a data-science track (S1). This is a small sample of department pages, not a ranked map of the field, and no source described specific computational methods or debates.

## Tools and AI

- **Databases.** JSTOR, Project MUSE, the MLA International Bibliography, and ABELL (S45).
- **Digital humanities.** Library guides list journals and companion volumes (S45). No source named a standard software stack, so none is listed in the roadmap.
- **AI detection.** The University of Minnesota's teaching guidance says detector scores are far from conclusive, can wrongly accuse students, and are not recommended. It suggests syllabus statements on ethical use, grading that rewards originality and analysis, drafts and outlines and in-class writing submitted with final work, and a conversation with the student (S43).
- **AI in writing careers.** See Key Findings (S29, S34, S35). Industry write-ups say routine copy and template-driven documentation are the most automatable work (S34, S37, both low).
- **Not used.** An aggregator's figures that entry-level writing roles fell 27% and freelance gigs 35% since 2023 cite no primary source (S36, low).

## Career Paths

### 1. Academia

- **Titles:** Adjunct or lecturer, visiting assistant professor, assistant professor (tenure-track), associate professor, full professor or chair.
- **Reality:** The dream that draws most English PhDs and the hardest to reach: about 1,144 PhDs against 469 assistant-professorships in 2022 (S12). Placement splits sharply by subfield (S13), and many PhDs spend years in contingent roles (S17). BLS projects no growth in English postsecondary jobs through 2035 (S26).
- **Salary:** English postsecondary teachers median $78,760 (BLS, May 2025); adjunct pay often $3,000 to $7,000 per course, rarely with health care. No tenure-track English salary range was found.
- **Sources:** S12, S13, S17, S26.

### 2. Publishing and editorial

- **Titles:** Editorial assistant, copyeditor, associate editor, senior editor, editorial director.
- **Reality:** Editors plan, review, and revise content for publication. BLS projects a 1% decline in editor employment from 2025 to 2035 (about 105,000 jobs). Pay spreads widely. Campus literary magazines and credit-bearing internships are available on-ramps (S2, S5). Beginning-level pay and the geography of publishing jobs were not found.
- **Salary:** Editors median $77,920 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $41,250; top 10% over $153,700.
- **Sources:** S24, S2, S5.

### 3. Content design and UX writing

- **Titles:** Content designer, UX writer, content strategist, senior content designer, content design lead.
- **Reality:** A 2025 survey reported a global median near $110,000, and 58% of respondents said AI had improved their work. The survey also found layoffs in the prior 24 months, mostly in the US, and over 80% of those laid off said they had found new roles (excerpt wording is ambiguous on the base). A hiring site lists a strong portfolio of clear, effective UX writing as expected and English among useful degrees.
- **Salary:** About $110,000 global median (2025 survey, quoted by a secondary site); US UX content strategists: middle half $72,000 to $110,000, top 10% about $136,000 (ZipRecruiter, November 2025). PayScale's average for content strategists with UX skills is $86,644 (July 2025).
- **Sources:** S29, S30, S31, S32, S33.

### 4. Technical and professional writing

- **Titles:** Junior technical writer, technical writer, senior technical writer, documentation lead, docs or content manager.
- **Reality:** BLS projects 1% growth from 2025 to 2035 on 46,400 jobs. Practitioners report AI now produces first drafts by default and writers spend more time reviewing and correcting, with junior roles under the most pressure and developer and compliance documentation seen as safer niches. The strongest displacement claims are forecasts, not measurements.
- **Salary:** Median $90,390 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $57,440; top 10% over $145,270.
- **Sources:** S23, S34, S35.

### 5. Secondary and K-12 teaching

- **Titles:** Student teacher, English teacher (grades 7 to 12), department head, curriculum specialist, instructional coach.
- **Reality:** Public school teachers need a state-issued license or certification, and a bachelor's is the typical entry degree. BLS projects flat employment from 2025 to 2035 with about 63,500 openings a year, mostly replacements, and says falling school-age enrollment constrains demand. Its page does not mention teacher shortages. UMD's English and Secondary Education double major builds certification into the degree.
- **Salary:** High school teachers (all subjects) median $72,040 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $48,780; top 10% over $107,600.
- **Sources:** S28, S5.

### 6. Law and legal

- **Titles:** Paralegal, law student (JD), associate attorney, litigator or counsel, partner or general counsel.
- **Reality:** A bachelor's in any major, then a three-year JD and a state bar exam; BLS says the path usually takes seven years of study after high school. English is a strong feeder at one documented school (Kentucky, fall 2014 class, average LSAT 162, highest among arts and sciences majors), but LSAC cautions against inferring that any major prepares students better. Pay is high and widely spread.
- **Salary:** Lawyers median $159,670 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $78,360; top 10% over $351,600. New graduates: NALP Class of 2025 median $100,000 (see `law-research.md`).
- **Sources:** S27, S38, S39, S49.

### 7. Communications, marketing, and public relations

- **Titles:** Communications coordinator, copywriter, PR specialist, content marketing manager, director of communications.
- **Reality:** BLS counts about 314,500 public relations specialists and projects 2% growth from 2025 to 2035. Industry write-ups say routine copy is the most automatable part of the work, which pushes value toward editing and strategy. No source compared English graduates with communications graduates.
- **Salary:** PR specialists median $74,750 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $44,110; top 10% over $135,150.
- **Sources:** S25, S37.

### 8. Writing, journalism, and media

- **Titles:** Staff writer, reporter or journalist, freelance writer, author or novelist, editor-in-chief.
- **Reality:** Salaried writing jobs generally ask for a bachelor's in English, communications, or journalism, and experience from school papers, internships, or blogging helps (S22, first-pass excerpt). BLS projects no growth for writers and authors from 2025 to 2035. Freelancers juggle several projects and keep looking for work. Reporters and journalists are a separate BLS occupation and are not priced here (S48).
- **Salary:** Writers and authors median $76,910 (BLS, May 2025); bottom 10% under $44,310; top 10% over $139,870.
- **Sources:** S22, S48.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **US only.** Everything here is US-centered. Degree structures differ elsewhere (Bristol, S44, is a UK department), and fields such as English language teaching abroad are not covered.
- **Fetch summaries.** Nineteen figures and statements came from model-extracted page summaries (marked "fetch"). Recheck before quoting.
- **BLS shifts.** Growth projections and employment counts for several occupations differ between the May 2024 and May 2025 pages, and the pages read do not say why.
- **BLS scope.** High school teacher figures cover all subjects, and postsecondary figures for all fields are shown beside the English-specific median. Reporters and journalists are a separate occupation.
- **Placement data is small-sample and self-reported.** Ohio State's WRL group is 22 graduates for 2018-22. The Ohio State page's category counts sum to more than its stated total of 293, and its chart and notes disagree on one rate (78% versus 79.7%). Programs define "placement" differently (Penn counts only first-time tenure-track jobs, Duke uses three years).
- **Year basis.** Goldstone's "2022" job counts match the MLA's 2021-22 ad total of 921, while the MLA's 2022-23 total is 911.
- **Salary sources mix.** BLS figures cover all workers in an occupation. The $110,000 UX figure is a global median quoted by a secondary site, and ZipRecruiter and PayScale figures are self-reported or scraped.
- **Not found.** Hardest courses, a standard software stack beyond databases, tenure-track English pay, early-career pay in publishing, MFA acceptance rates and unfunded tuition, master's-level outcome data, an English row in the LSAC major table, and any evidence of an English-teacher shortage.
- **Research frontiers are low confidence.** They rest on one UK department's page, one US library guide, one Stanford track, and a few placement lists.
