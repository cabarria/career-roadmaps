# Nursing: Research Notes for the Roadmap

Prepared for the Nursing career-roadmap artifact. Every figure below traces to a source listed at the end. Where the first draft of the artifact said something the sources do not support, that is recorded under Uncertainties.

Version 1. The first draft of the roadmap was built from eight search passes and no saved research file. This version adds a second pass (hardest courses, student activities, research priorities, PhD funding, NP workforce projections, manager pay, the nursing faculty shortage, and the claim that AI is cutting nursing jobs) and replaces May 2024 BLS figures with May 2025 figures and 2025 to 2035 projections.

Access dates: every page was read on 2026-11-22. Pages read in full are marked "(full page)" in Sources. The rest were read only as search-result excerpts, so details beyond the excerpt were not checked.

## TL;DR

- Nursing is licensed at the first degree. An ADN (about 2 years) or a BSN (about 4 years) leads to the same NCLEX-RN and the same RN license. The BSN is the hiring and graduate-school gateway: BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education, and one survey of 643 nursing schools found 25% of employers require a bachelor's and 69.8% strongly prefer one.
- The pay ladder is steep. May 2025 BLS medians: RN $97,550; nurse practitioner $132,300; nurse midwife $134,040; nurse anesthetist (CRNA) $236,590. BLS projects 36% growth for the combined NP, CRNA and midwife group from 2025 to 2035 and 6% for RNs.
- Graduate nursing splits into clinical advanced practice (NP, CRNA, nurse-midwife, clinical nurse specialist) and non-clinical tracks (leadership, education, informatics). Professional master's and doctoral tracks carry real tuition (NP master's about $35,000 to $65,000), and many students keep working as RNs; no source gave a funded-versus-self-pay norm for NP programs. Two PhD programs checked (Duke, Johns Hopkins) fund students through assistantships; funding varies by program.
- Two doctorates point different ways. The DNP is a practice doctorate (project, not dissertation). The PhD is a research doctorate. Only nurse anesthesia has a doctoral entry mandate (2025). BLS still lists a master's as typical entry for NPs.
- HRSA's 2025 workforce report projects a national surplus of primary care NPs (72,910 FTEs in 2038) alongside a shortage of primary care physicians (70,610 FTEs). A national surplus coexists with local shortages, especially in rural areas. No source found splits supply by NP specialty.
- AI exposure for bedside nursing is modeled as low, and nursing ranked #2 on a 2025 Forbes list of AI-safe jobs. The desk-based and documentation-heavy parts of nursing are where AI is arriving first. The one concrete layoff case found (12 utilization-review nurses at Montefiore, July 2026) is disputed by the hospital.
- Eight career destinations were researched: bedside RN, nurse practitioner, nurse anesthetist, nurse-midwife, leadership and administration, informatics, education and research, and travel nursing.

## Key Findings

- Same license, different doors: ADN and BSN graduates passed the NCLEX-RN at 86% and 87% in calendar 2025 (Post University citing NCLEX data). Pay differences are small at the start (about 3 to 5%) and widen to roughly $10K to $15K a year after 5 to 10 years, per one industry estimate (registerednursing.org). Other sources give larger gaps (Payscale: $96,000 BSN versus $79,000 ADN, via Nurse.org), so the size of the gap is uncertain.
- Pay premiums over RN (BLS May 2025 medians): NP about $34,750 more ($132,300 versus $97,550); nurse-midwife about $36,500 more; CRNA about $139,000 more. Combined APRN median is $134,920 with a 10th to 90th percentile spread of under $102,190 to over $224,490.
- NP market, two true statements: BLS projects 36% growth for the combined APRN group with about 32,200 openings a year, and HRSA projects a national primary care NP surplus. They measure different things (BLS counts jobs and replacement openings across all settings; HRSA models primary care NP FTEs against modeled demand). RN.org reports that about 90% of NPs train in primary care but only 31% to 45% practice there ("specialty drift").
- Nursing faculty are a bottleneck: US nursing schools turned away 92,672 qualified applications in 2025, citing faculty shortages first (AACN, fact sheet updated October 2026). Faculty vacancy rate was 7.2% (AACN, October 2025). Master's-prepared nursing professors average $82,762 against a $134,920 median for APRNs (AACN, May 2026). This compares roles, not degrees: no source here compares PhD and DNP holders doing the same job.
- AI in nursing: modeled estimates put RN task exposure at 26% and automation at 12% in 2025, rising to 38% and 18% by 2028 (aichanging.work, built on the Anthropic Economic Index, BLS and O*NET; the page also states 8% in one place, so treat as rough). The strongest labor signal is not job loss but dispute: about two in three nurses whose employers use AI acuity scores say those scores do not match their own assessment (MedCity News via allnurses.com), and a 100,000-member National Nurses United march in 2025 included protections against AI.
- CRNA is the one role with a hard doctoral entry rule. From 2025 all new candidates must come from an accredited doctoral program (DNP or DNAP). Programs run about three years after one or more years of critical care nursing; the average admitted class has about 2.5 years of ICU experience (Nurse.org). Total path from high school is typically 7 to 10 years (NurseJournal).
- Travel nursing pay has normalized: average weekly travel RN pay fell 15.1% in 2022, 11.3% in 2023 and 3.8% in 2024, to $2,294 a week in December 2024, below the inflation-adjusted January 2020 level of $2,319 (Vivian Health).

## Undergraduate (Years 1 to 4)

Degree routes:

- ADN: typically about 2 years, community-college based; accelerated ADN as little as 18 months (Denver College of Nursing).
- BSN: typically about 4 years; accelerated BSN for second-degree students as little as 21 months; RN-to-BSN as little as 15 months, or 1 to 2 years part-time (Denver College of Nursing; registerednursing.org).
- RN diploma: hospital-based, no degree awarded, credits may not transfer, less common today (NurseJournal).
- BLS (May 2025) lists a bachelor's as typical entry education for RNs and notes that employers, particularly hospitals, may require one. Graduates of any path qualify for entry-level staff nurse jobs (BLS).
- Direct-entry MSN programs take non-nursing graduates toward advanced practice and fold in RN licensure (registerednursing.org).

Core curriculum:

- Shared core: adult health, maternal-newborn, pediatrics, and often psychiatric, community health and gerontological nursing (Kaplan, a 2018 page).
- BSN adds research, leadership, public health and evidence-based practice; BSN clinicals tend to include more varied settings and leadership practicums (UWA, NurseJournal).
- Clinical experience is a crucial part of both ADN and BSN programs (Jersey College, Herzing).

Hardest courses (NurseJournal, updated 2024, interviews with nurses; no attrition or pass-rate data on the page):

- Pathophysiology.
- Pharmacology, described as hard for the depth and amount of knowledge needed.
- Medical-Surgical 1 (Adult Health 1), often the first course where students combine earlier material.
- Evidence-Based Practice (heavy reading and concise scientific writing).
- Anatomy and Physiology I and II and organic chemistry are named as prerequisites that predict success in the harder courses.

Skills described by sources:

- Core entry-level skills include clinical and technical proficiency, communication and critical thinking (Denver College of Nursing).
- ADN-level duties: recording symptoms and medical history, patient education, working with physicians, using basic medical equipment (Nightingale College). BSN-prepared nurses can manage other nursing staff (Nightingale College).
- Documentation in electronic health records (Epic and Oracle Health are named) is a growing AI target (allnurses.com).

Licensure:

- NCLEX-RN is required for both ADN and BSN graduates. Calendar 2025 pass rates: BSN 87%, ADN 86%; in 2020 they were about 90% and 82% (Post University). Another source gives 85% to 90% and notes program quality matters more than degree type (registerednursing.org).

Beyond the classroom (thin evidence; see Uncertainties):

- National Student Nurses' Association (NSNA) chapters: state and national conventions, leadership and networking benefits claimed by one 2018 school profile (Purdue).
- Externships: structured summer programs on one hospital unit with a preceptor; at one New York school applications open in late fall or early spring of junior year and strong performers can receive return offers. Named hospitals are New York City systems (NYU student account, July 2026). Treat as one school's experience.
- Career fairs, "Meet the Manager" events and alumni specialty nights run by schools (NYU). Asking a clinical unit manager for contact information was suggested as a hiring route (NYU).
- Undergraduate research opportunities and named nursing REU-style programs were not researched.

Tools:

- Electronic health records (Epic, Oracle Health), now adding ambient AI note-writing. Early adopters report saving 45 minutes to 2 hours per shift on documentation (American Nurse Journal via allnurses.com, second-hand).
- Remote patient monitoring. UCHealth's virtual sepsis program is reported to monitor about 2,000 patients a day and detect sepsis 2 to 4 hours earlier (Memrizz, a secondary summary).

## Master's (Years about 5 to 7)

Program types:

- NP tracks. Examples listed by programs: Family (FNP), Psychiatric-Mental Health (PMHNP), Adult-Gerontology Primary Care and Acute Care, Pediatric Primary Care, Women's Health (Azusa Pacific, St. John Fisher, University of Cincinnati).
- Other APRN roles: clinical nurse specialist (DNP tracks exist, for example Point Loma), nurse-midwife and nurse anesthetist. CNS scope, pay and demand were not researched.
- Non-clinical MSN paths: Clinical Nurse Leader, nurse educator, nurse executive (Sacred Heart University, a marketing page that quotes $90,000 to $132,000+ across these paths).
- Post-master's certificates add a second population focus in about 20 to 26 credits and roughly a year (graduatecertificates.com).

Admission and clinical requirements:

- University of Cincinnati FNP: active RN license, BSN from an accredited program, recommended 3.0 GPA, statistics, and at least one year of US RN experience.
- Programs must hold CCNE or ACEN accreditation for graduates to sit national certification (Sacred Heart University; ANCC).
- ANCC PMHNP certification requires at least 500 faculty-supervised clinical hours; WGU's PMHNP program requires 650; Kansas required 750 for students starting on or after 2025-03-01 (ANCC; WGU).
- All APRN tracks include graduate courses in advanced pathophysiology, advanced pharmacology and advanced health assessment (Franklin University; ANCC).

Certification and licensure:

- FNP exams: ANCC FNP-BC (more policy and theory content) or AANP FNP-C (clinical focus); 150 to 175 questions; fees $240 to $395 (Franklin University).
- PMHNP-BC (ANCC): 175 questions (150 scored) in 3.5 hours; 82% pass rate in ANCC's 2025 data.
- State APRN licensure is required in addition to national certification (BLS).
- Full practice authority (FPA): about 30 states plus DC as of mid-2026, up from 22 in 2020 (CMF Group). Another tracker counts 29 states plus DC and two territories (NursePractitionerOnline). Recent changes: New Jersey (signed March 2026), Oklahoma (effective November 2025). Transition-to-practice requirements range from about 1,000 to 9,000 supervised hours (NursePractitionerOnline).

Cost and funding:

- NP master's tuition typically $35,000 to $65,000 (Walsh University). Midwifery programs about $75,000 to $150,000 (Nurse.org). CRNA programs $50,000 to over $200,000 (Nurse.org); average in-state tuition about $117,749 across 147 programs in one school database (The CRNA Club).
- For comparison, the class of 2025 paid an average of $228,959 for a four-year medical degree (Education Data Initiative via Walsh University).
- Many NP programs are online and part-time so students can keep working (npschools.com). Some employers offer tuition assistance for bridge programs (registerednursing.org, Kaplan). Whether employer help extends to graduate NP tracks was not verified.

Is the master's terminal? BLS lists a master's in an advanced practice nursing field as typical entry education for NPs, nurse-midwives and nurse anesthetists. For nurse anesthetists that line conflicts with the doctoral mandate in effect since 2025 (see PhD / Research), so BLS appears to lag on that one role. For NPs, some programs and advocates favor the DNP (npschools.com), but the master's remains the BLS entry norm.

## PhD / Research (Years about 8+)

Two doctorates (UCSF, Duke, Point Loma, Research.com):

- DNP: practice doctorate for advanced practice, clinical leadership and quality improvement; typically 3 to 4 years; ends in a practice-focused project, not a dissertation; average tuition about $40,000 (Research.com). In 2020, 66% of DNP programs were primarily online (AACN via St. Thomas University).
- PhD in Nursing: research doctorate; typically 4 to 6 years; dissertation of original research; no clinical hours required; BSN-to-PhD tracks can run about 4.5 years (UCF, Research.com).
- Growth: from 2010 to 2019 DNP graduates grew more than 500% to 7,944 a year, while PhD graduates grew 50% to 804 (National Academy of Medicine data via St. Thomas University). Physician-advocacy critics call some programs "diploma mills" (Physicians for Patient Protection, 2019); that is an advocacy position, not a measured finding.
- A Point Loma alumna summarizes the split: DNP-prepared nurses translate evidence, PhD-prepared nurses create it.

PhD funding (varies by program):

- Duke: tuition fully funded for up to five years; 12-month stipend (amount not stated on page); research or teaching assistantship about 8 hours a week starting in the second semester of year 1; students are expected to seek external fellowships.
- Johns Hopkins: most full-time students are fully funded with a stipend for the first three years; assistantship about 20 hours a week with a tuition waiver. The $22,920 stipend on the page is dated 2015-16, so it is not used as a current figure.

Nurse anesthesia doctorate:

- As of 2025 all new CRNA candidates must graduate from an accredited doctoral program (DNP or DNAP) approved by the Council on Accreditation (NurseJournal; Research.com; Nurse.org). Programs take about 3 years (DNP 3 to 4 years) after at least one year of critical care nursing (BLS); admitted students average about 2.5 years of ICU experience (Nurse.org).

Research priorities and frontiers:

- NINR 2022 to 2026 Strategic Plan research lenses: Health Equity; Social Determinants of Health; Population and Community Health; Prevention and Health Promotion; Systems and Models of Care. The one-page summary does not list symptom science or data science. The plan period ends in 2026; the successor plan was not checked.
- Workforce and access: HRSA's 2025 report projects a primary care NP surplus (72,910 FTEs) and a primary care physician shortage (70,610 FTEs) in 2038; 63.1% of primary care Health Professional Shortage Areas are rural (RN.org summary of HRSA and PubMed Central studies). How to place NPs where need is highest is an open question in those sources.
- AI and the workforce: a 2024 commentary notes that peer-reviewed literature on AI replacing nurses is limited (ScienceDirect commentary, 2024). Evidence on acuity tools, ambient documentation and monitoring is mostly practitioner surveys and news.
- Faculty roles: 79.8% of nursing faculty vacancies required or preferred a doctoral degree (AACN 2023 survey of 922 schools, via Nurse.org).

## Tools and AI

- Standard tools: EHR platforms (Epic, Oracle Health), bedside monitoring and remote patient monitoring, and increasingly ambient documentation AI.
- Time savings are reported but second-hand. Wolters Kluwer's 2025 survey found 72% of nurses name administrative burden as a major driver of burnout and 45% believe generative AI can reduce it (via allnurses.com). An AMIA 2025 study found nurses spent about 10% of a 12-hour shift on non-nursing tasks (via University of Cincinnati).
- Ranking: nursing was #2 on Forbes' 2025 list of AI-safe jobs, behind phlebotomists (via University of Cincinnati).
- Modeled risk: RN exposure 18% in 2023, 26% in 2025, 38% projected for 2028; automation risk 8%, 12%, 18% (aichanging.work). The page is inconsistent (it also states "8% automation in patient care") and is a modeled estimate, not measured displacement.
- Staffing tools: MedCity News (February 2026, via allnurses.com) reports that about two in three nurses at employers using AI-generated acuity measures say the scores do not reflect their own assessment.
- Labor action: several 2025 nursing strikes centered on AI concerns, including a National Nurses United march of 100,000 members (HealthLeaders, September 2025).
- Contested layoff case: Montefiore (Bronx) laid off 12 utilization-review nurses in early July 2026 after a strike; the nurses and the New York State Nurses Association say the AI platform Datavant replaced them. The hospital denies it, calls the claim inaccurate and says Datavant is a nonclinical paperwork tool. The article does not independently establish cause (Prism Reports, 2026-08-03).
- A figure that insurers cut utilization-review nurse headcount 20% to 40% since 2022 appears in an allnurses.com article with no source. It was not verified and is not used.
- Clinical decision support and AI diagnostics for NPs: no practitioner-level source on utility versus hype was found in this pass.

## Career Paths

All figures US unless noted. BLS figures are May 2025 data with 2025 to 2035 projections unless noted. "OEWS mean" figures come from BLS OEWS as reported by Nurse.org.

### 1. Bedside and Staff RN

- Titles: New-Grad RN, Staff RN, Charge Nurse, ICU, ER or OR RN.
- Reality: the most accessible destination. BLS projects 6% growth (2025 to 2035), about 180,800 openings a year and 3,465,400 jobs in 2025. Hospitals employ 59% of RNs. A bachelor's is the typical entry education and hospitals may require one; 25% of employers require a BSN and 69.8% strongly prefer it (Herzing, 643-school study).
- Salary: median $97,550; 10th to 90th percentile under $68,940 to over $137,470 (BLS). OEWS mean $101,420. By setting (median): government $110,780; hospitals $100,220; ambulatory $91,230; nursing and residential care $84,300 (BLS).
- AI exposure: modeled as low; see Tools and AI.
- Sources: BLS Registered Nurses; Herzing; Nurse.org (May 2025 OEWS).

### 2. Nurse Practitioner

- Titles: FNP, PMHNP, AGACNP, other NP specialties; independent practice in FPA states.
- Reality: master's is the BLS typical entry; national certification plus state APRN license required. BLS employment 336,300 (2025). Group growth 36% (2025 to 2035), about 32,200 openings a year across NPs, CRNAs and midwives. HRSA projects a primary care NP surplus of 72,910 FTEs by 2038 with a physician shortage of 70,610 FTEs; local shortages persist, 63.1% of primary care shortage areas are rural, and only 31% to 45% of NPs practice in primary care. No source quantified supply or demand by specialty. A large mental-health need exists (KFF figure of 122 million people living in mental-health shortage areas, as quoted by nursinglicensemap.com), but that is not a PMHNP-versus-FNP job-market comparison.
- Salary: median $132,300 (BLS, May 2025); OEWS mean $137,300. Combined APRN median $134,920.
- AI exposure: no NP-specific estimate found.
- Sources: BLS APRN page; HRSA State of the Primary Care Workforce 2025; RN.org; CMF Group; NursePractitionerOnline; Nurse.org.

### 3. Nurse Anesthetist (CRNA)

- Titles: ICU RN (prerequisite), CRNA, lead CRNA, locums or independent CRNA.
- Reality: highest-paid nursing role. Doctoral program required since 2025; BLS says at least one year of critical care RN experience, and admitted classes average about 2.5 years. BLS employment 54,500 (2025). AANA projects a shortage of about 12,500 anesthesia providers by 2033 (via The CRNA Club). About 140 accredited programs. Total path 7 to 10 years from the start of the BSN.
- Salary: median $236,590 (BLS OOH, May 2025); OEWS mean $248,320 with 10th to 90th percentile $155,250 to $339,500. Outpatient care centers average $274,350 (Research.com). Travel CRNA average $259,707 (ZipRecruiter via Nurse.org). A blog reports new graduates start around $180,000 (The CRNA Club, anecdotal).
- AI exposure: no estimate found; low exposure is an inference from the real-time hands-on nature of the work.
- Sources: BLS APRN page; Nurse.org; NurseJournal; Research.com; The CRNA Club.

### 4. Certified Nurse-Midwife

- Titles: CNM, hospital midwife, birth-center midwife, women's health APRN.
- Reality: master's-level APRN. BLS employment 8,200 (2025), a small field. Midwifery programs cost about $75,000 to $150,000 (Nurse.org). Midwifery-specific growth projection was not isolated; the 36% figure is for the combined group.
- Salary: median $134,040 (BLS, May 2025); OEWS mean $136,980.
- AI exposure: no estimate found.
- Sources: BLS APRN page; Nurse.org CNM guide; Credenza.

### 5. Nursing Leadership and Administration

- Titles: Charge Nurse, Nurse Manager, Director of Nursing, Chief Nursing Officer (CNO).
- Reality: usually needs a second master's (nursing leadership or MHA) or an executive-track DNP; nurse executive certifications such as CENP and NEA-BC are cited as helpful (NP Hub, a blog). No source gave a reliable time-to-CNO. BLS's broader category, medical and health services managers, has a bachelor's as typical education, under 5 years of related experience, and 24% projected growth (2025 to 2035).
- Salary: medical and health services managers median $123,860 (BLS, May 2025); hospitals $133,580; nursing and residential care $100,520. CNO: Salary.com average $244,640 (June 2026), range $225,800 to $307,180, midpoint $264,150; Salary.com also shows a median of $207,925 (2025). Payscale reports $116,000 to $250,000. These conflict; ranges are shown rather than one number.
- AI exposure: not estimated. Nurse leaders are on the receiving end of staffing-AI disputes (HealthLeaders).
- Sources: BLS Medical and Health Services Managers; Salary.com; Payscale; NP Hub.

### 6. Nursing Informatics

- Titles: Informatics Nurse, Clinical Informatics Specialist, Director of Clinical Informatics, Chief Nursing Informatics Officer.
- Reality: bridges clinical work and health IT (EHR configuration, rollouts, analytics). About 80% of informatics nurses work remotely (IMIA 2025, via Research.com). Pay is lower at entry and higher at executive level.
- Salary: informatics nurse $85,161 and a field average of $99,161 in 2025 (Payscale via Research.com); Chief Nursing Informatics Officer $155,833 (ZipRecruiter via Research.com) versus $223,950 (Salary.com). These conflict; both are shown.
- AI exposure: Research.com ties pay growth to AI, predictive analytics and EMR expansion. That the work is growing because of AI is an inference.
- Sources: Research.com; Nurse.org informatics guide; Salary.com.

### 7. Nurse Educator and Scientist

- Titles: Clinical Instructor, Nursing Faculty, Nurse Scientist, Professor.
- Reality: a PhD is the research-university route; a DNP can teach at some smaller universities (Point Loma). 79.8% of vacancies required or preferred a doctorate (AACN 2023). Schools turned away 92,672 qualified applications in 2025, with faculty shortage the top reason; faculty vacancy rate 7.2%. AACN says higher pay in clinical and private-sector settings is pulling educators away.
- Salary: master's-prepared nursing professors average $82,762 (AACN, May 2026). Nursing faculty earned $8,677 less than all other nurses and $18,346 less than staff nurses (2022 National Sample Survey of Registered Nurses, via Nurse.org). Postsecondary nursing teachers $77,440 (BLS, as quoted by UCF); clinical faculty $87,090 (IntelyCare); nurse researcher median $81,500 (Payscale via UCF). None of these is PhD-specific.
- AI exposure: not estimated.
- Sources: AACN Faculty Shortage Fact Sheet; Nurse.org educator pay article; UCF; IntelyCare; Point Loma.

### 8. Travel Nursing

- Titles: Travel RN, crisis-response RN, travel specialty RN, travel NP or CRNA.
- Reality: short contracts (commonly 13 weeks) with stipends in place of benefits. Average weekly pay was $2,294 in December 2024 after declines of 15.1% (2022), 11.3% (2023) and 3.8% (2024); that is below the inflation-adjusted January 2020 level of $2,319 (Vivian Health). A 2026 guide gives blended weekly pay of $2,170 to $2,400 (TLC Nursing).
- Salary: average $101,132 a year (ZipRecruiter, January 2025); 25th percentile $73,600, 75th $120,400 (Nurse.org). Full-time travel (48 to 52 weeks) often $110,000 to $140,000 (TLC Nursing). Premium over staff pay: about 27% (Betternurse, using 2023 BLS staff pay) or $15,000 to $40,000 a year (TLC Nursing). Travel NP average weekly pay $3,101 as of September 2024 (Vivian via Betternurse).
- AI exposure: not estimated.
- Sources: Vivian Health; Nurse.org; TLC Nursing; Betternurse.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- Everything is US-centric. Licensure, scope of practice and degree norms differ by country. Nothing outside the US was researched.
- Practice authority, transition-to-practice hours, clinical-hour rules and APRN licensing vary by state, and counts of full-practice states differ by source (29 plus DC versus about 30 plus DC).
- BLS figures mix statistics. OOH medians (for example RN $97,550; CRNA $236,590) are not OEWS means (RN $101,420; CRNA $248,320). OOH employment (CRNA 54,500) differs from OEWS employment (51,840). The roadmap states which one each figure is.
- Salary-site figures conflict: CNIO $155,833 versus $223,950; CNO median $207,925 versus average $244,640 versus Payscale $116,000 to $250,000; ADN versus BSN pay gaps range from about 3 to 5% at the start to $18,000 a year (NurseJournal) or $17,000 (Payscale via Nurse.org).
- Pages read only as search-result excerpts (not opened in full): all ADN versus BSN pages, Franklin, University of Cincinnati MSN FNP, ANCC PMHNP, npschools, WGU, graduatecertificates.com, Azusa Pacific, Sacred Heart, Walsh, NursePractitionerOnline, CMF Group, Nurse.org CRNA and salary pages, NurseJournal CRNA, The CRNA Club, Research.com pages, UCSF, Duke DNP-PhD comparison, Point Loma, STU, UCF, IntelyCare, allnurses.com AI article, aichanging.work, University of Cincinnati AI article, HealthLeaders, ScienceDirect, Salary.com, Payscale, Nurse.org informatics, Vivian, TLC Nursing, Betternurse, Credenza.
- Several sources are school or vendor marketing pages (Nightingale, Jersey College, Denver College, Sacred Heart, WGU, Walsh, Azusa Pacific). They are used for program structure, not for outcome claims.
- Old or narrow sources: Kaplan (2018), Purdue (2018 school profile), NYU (one New York student's account). Used only for qualitative context.
- Not used as evidence: Student Doctor Network threads (2017 and 2020) and a 2019 Physicians for Patient Protection essay about NP oversaturation and "diploma mills". HRSA's 2025 report is the evidence for the NP surplus.
- NP supply by specialty (FNP versus PMHNP) is unquantified. The first draft's statement that new-graduate FNPs struggle to place while PMHNP demand stays strong rested on forum comments and is not in the artifact.
- The 35% (2024 to 2034) and 36% (2025 to 2035) APRN growth figures are for the combined NP, CRNA and midwife group, not NPs alone. One site quotes 40% for NPs alone (npschools.com); not verified.
- Montefiore is a single, disputed case. It shows a desk role being cut around the introduction of an AI tool, not that AI caused the cut.
- NINR's strategic plan covers 2022 to 2026; a successor plan was not checked.
- Not researched: undergraduate research or REU-style programs, honor societies, nurse residency program outcomes, clinical nurse specialist scope and pay, LPN and LVN routes, accreditation differences between CCNE and ACEN beyond eligibility, military and public-health nursing, and international nursing pathways.
- Corrections to the first-draft artifact (all fixed in the roadmap): RN "$93,600 median" was labeled May 2025 but is a May 2024 figure; APRN growth of 35% and RN growth of about 5% were 2024 to 2034 figures, now 36% and 6% (2025 to 2035); CRNA count of about 52,000 and midwife count of about 11,000 are now BLS 54,500 and 8,200; "HRSA projects oversupply in every state" came from a 2020 forum post, replaced by the 2025 HRSA national projection; "20% to 40% utilization-review cuts" was unsourced; "$3,000 a week pandemic peak" and "15% travel premium" were unsourced; "nurse manager $95,000 to $130,000" was unsourced; "symptom science is the NIH-backed core of nursing research" is not supported by NINR's page; "PhD faculty earn $77,000 to $90,000 versus DNP $130,000+" compared occupations, not degrees; the footer date said July 2026.
- Carried over unchanged from the first draft because they are descriptive and carry no figure or attribution (author synthesis, not independently sourced; treat as description, not findings): the Specialty Clinical Rotations and BSN-only coursework items; the dosage calculation, hands-on procedural skills and charting skill items; the Program Types items (MSN NP tracks including Neonatal, clinical nurse specialist, non-clinical MSNs, post-master's certificates); the certification exams, core APRN coursework and evidence-based practice items; the DNP "What it's for" and CRNA mandate items; the PhD "What it's for" item; the Implementation science item; and the day-to-day descriptions in the Bedside RN, Midwife, Informatics and Educator cards (demanding shift work, intensity of managing two lives, a landing spot for nurses who like systems, a calling more than a payday).
