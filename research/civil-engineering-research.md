# The Civil Engineering Career Roadmap (USA): Undergraduate to PhD, Stage 1 Research Synthesis

Prepared 2026-11-23. This file is the source of record for `artifacts/civil-engineering-roadmap.jsx`. It was written after the artifact was built (the artifact was produced before the research file existed), so it was reconstructed from the original search results and then extended with new fetches for figures that had no source. Where a figure remains weakly sourced, it is listed under Uncertainties.

## TL;DR

- **Civil engineering is a licensure-defined profession.** The bachelor's launches a career, and advancement runs through the FE exam, about four years of supervised experience, and the PE license rather than through a graduate degree. The BLS median is $99,590 (May 2024), with about 368,900 jobs in 2024, 5% projected growth for 2024 to 2034, and about 23,600 openings per year.[^1]
- **The PE premium is real but the size depends on the source.** ASCE's 2025 report states a PE adds $40,000 per year; Monograph cites $30,000 to $42,000; CareerBldr cites $15,000 to $20,000. One PE-prep publisher cautions that no general dollar premium is established because the BLS median does not separate PEs from non-PEs.[^2][^3][^4][^5]
- **Specialty and sector drive pay.** Structural engineering is consistently described as highest paid, followed by geotechnical. Only about 20% of civil PEs specialize in geotechnical work, which supports consulting rates of $150 to $300 per hour for licensed specialists. Public-sector base pay runs 10% to 20% below private, offset by pensions.[^4][^6][^7]
- **The PhD is a small, funded path.** Civil careers are defined by practice and licensure, so most engineers stop at the bachelor's or a coursework master's. A civil PhD typically takes four to six years full time.[^8]

## Key Findings

1. **Licensure ladder.** FE exam (usually senior year) leads to the Engineer-in-Training credential, then typically four years of qualifying experience under a licensed engineer (states vary), then the discipline-specific PE exam. Since April 2024 the NCEES civil PE exam is fully discipline-focused; first-time pass rates from January to June 2025 were: Water Resources and Environmental 68%, Geotechnical 61%, Structural 58%, Construction 56%, Transportation 55%.[^9][^10]
2. **Entry pay.** ASCE's 2025 report puts the median entry-level salary at $77,100 and the average base salary across respondents at $148,000 (up 6.4% from $139,000). ASCE's 2024 report, as cited by Monograph, gave a member median primary income of $130,000 and $135,000 including consulting.[^2][^4]
3. **Pay growth.** ASCE reports civil engineers saw 6% to 7% annual salary increases from 2022 to 2025, versus 3% to 5% for the overall US workforce (WorldatWork), and job changers received a median 20% increase.[^2]
4. **Public sector tradeoff.** Private base salaries are typically 10% to 20% higher; public pensions can represent $500,000 to $1,000,000+ in retirement value.[^6]
5. **Automation exposure is low, anchored by licensure.** A PE must take legal responsibility for stamped public-safety work. This is an argument from structure of the profession, not a measured displacement statistic.
6. **Funding.** The federal Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (about $1.2 trillion over its authorization period, a widely cited figure) is repeatedly named as the reason state DOTs are awarding more work than firms can staff. The $1.2T figure was not independently re-verified in this pass.[^11]

## Details

### 1. Undergraduate Foundation (Years 1 to 4)

**Conceptual backbone.** Curricula checked across ABET-style programs (North Dakota, CSUN, CCNY, Notre Dame, Rhode Island, Gonzaga, Marshall, Rutgers) share a core: statics, mechanics of materials, structural analysis, structural design in concrete and steel, soil mechanics and geotechnical engineering, fluid mechanics and hydraulics, hydrology and water resources, transportation engineering, environmental engineering, surveying and construction engineering, and a senior capstone design.[^12]

**Weeder courses.** Statics, mechanics of materials, and fluid mechanics are the courses most often described as gatekeepers. This is program-guide and student consensus rather than measured failure rates.

**Hands-on skills.** Surveying and site layout, soil and concrete and asphalt testing, structural analysis software, hydrologic and hydraulic modeling, plans, specifications and quantity take-offs.

**Foundational tools.** AutoCAD and Civil 3D, SAP2000, ETABS, RISA, or STAAD, HEC-RAS, HEC-HMS, StormCAD and EPA SWMM, Revit and BIM, ArcGIS or QGIS, and Python for automation.

**High-value extracurriculars.** Take the FE exam in the senior year; internships at consulting firms (AECOM, WSP, Stantec, Jacobs), contractors, and public agencies; ASCE student chapter; Concrete Canoe and Steel Bridge competitions.

### 2. Master's Stage (Years 5 to 6)

**Program types.** Thesis MS (funded by assistantship, PhD bridge); coursework or professional MS (MEng), usually self-funded or employer-sponsored; BS/MS 4+1 tracks and online programs.

**Is it necessary?** Not for most roles, but common for structural and geotechnical specialization. The artifact states that structural practice often expects an MS and that many states are moving toward requiring education beyond a bachelor's for licensure; neither claim was verified in this pass (see Uncertainties).

**Specializations.** Structural, geotechnical, transportation, water resources and hydraulics, environmental, construction engineering and management, coastal and climate resilience.

**Credentials.** FE then PE (discipline-specific, 8 hours); SE for advanced structural work in some states; PMP; LEED, ENV SP, and dam-safety specialty credentials.[^9][^13]

**Advanced tools.** ETABS, SAP2000, ABAQUS; PLAXIS and GeoStudio; HEC-RAS 2D and MIKE; Revit and Navisworks; ArcGIS Pro and LiDAR; Python.

### 3. PhD / Advanced Research Stage (Years 6 to 11)

**Structure.** Drexel's civil PhD states the program takes "four to six years" full time with at least 90 credits (45 beyond a prior MS), ending with an oral thesis defense. Most incoming students there receive research assistantships. Qualifying exams and candidacy are standard at most programs but were not stated on the Drexel page.[^8]

**Funding.** Research and teaching assistantships and fellowships cover tuition and a stipend at typical research universities. The artifact says advisor grants are "often NSF, DOT, or agency funded"; that specific attribution was not verified.[^8][^14]

**Research frontiers (2025 to 2026).** Resilient and climate-adaptive infrastructure; smart infrastructure and digital twins; low-carbon and sustainable materials (including mass timber); structural health monitoring and sensing; seismic and extreme-event engineering; construction robotics and automation; AI for design, planning, and asset management; water systems and urban sustainability.[^15]

**Digital-twin market size.** One vendor forecast (Knowledge Sourcing Intelligence, 2025) projects the digital twin in construction market reaching about $155 billion by 2030, a 19% CAGR from a roughly $64.9 billion 2025 base. This is a market-research forecast and should be read as indicative, not as measured spending.[^16]

### 4. Tools and AI

- **Software stack.** Civil 3D, Revit and BIM, SAP2000 and ETABS and RISA, HEC-RAS and SWMM, GIS, and Python are the practical defaults.
- **AI use cases.** Generative design, clash detection, construction-site computer vision, predictive maintenance of bridges and pavements, and surrogate models for expensive structural or hydraulic simulations. Review literature describes these as active research and early commercial adoption.[^15]
- **Practitioner caveat.** Code compliance, constructability, and the engineer's seal remain human responsibilities.

### 5. Career Paths

Salary ranges are those on the artifact cards, with basis stated.

1. **Structural Engineering.** Titles: Structural Engineer, Project Engineer, Sr. Structural Engineer, Engineer of Record (PE/SE), Principal. Reality: highest-paid, highest-liability civil track; master's often expected; PE and in some states SE license gate engineer-of-record status. Salary on card: $70K to $160K+. Basis: ASCE entry median $77,100 and average base $148,000; the $160K+ top end is indicative.[^2][^6]
2. **Geotechnical Engineering.** Titles: Geotechnical Engineer, Project Engineer, Sr. Geotechnical Engineer, Geotechnical PE, Principal. Reality: about 20% of civil PEs, consulting rates $150 to $300 per hour, registered geotechnical engineers report medians near $100K to $110K. Salary on card: $75K to $150K+. Basis: PEwise and Monograph.[^4][^7]
3. **Transportation Engineering.** Titles: Transportation Engineer, Traffic Engineer, Sr. Transportation Engineer, PE / Project Manager, Program Manager. Reality: largest subfield, tightened by federal infrastructure funding; the transportation PE has the lowest first-time pass rate (55%). Salary on card: $68K to $140K+. Basis: indicative, from aggregator guides.[^6][^10]
4. **Water Resources and Environmental.** Titles: Water Resources Engineer, Environmental Engineer, Hydraulic Engineer, Sr. WRE / PE, Practice Lead. Reality: climate resilience and aging water infrastructure expand demand; the WRE PE has the highest first-time pass rate (68%). Salary on card: $68K to $130K. Basis: indicative.[^10]
5. **Construction Management.** Titles: Field/Project Engineer, Construction Manager, Sr. Project Manager, Program Manager, Director of Construction. Reality: project managers with a PMP often earn $110K to $140K. Salary on card: $75K to $150K+. Basis: Monograph for the PMP range; the "$10K to $25K bonus" statement on the card is unsourced.[^4]
6. **Public Sector / Government.** Titles: Staff Engineer, Project Engineer (DOT/City), Sr. Engineer, Engineering Manager, City/County Engineer. Reality: private base pay is typically 10% to 20% higher; public pensions can represent $500K to $1M+ in retirement value. Salary on card: $70K to $130K plus pension value. Basis: CareerBldr.[^6]
7. **Land Development / Site Civil.** Titles: Site/Land Development Engineer, Civil Designer, Project Engineer, Sr. Site Engineer, Development PE. Reality: Civil 3D-heavy, permitting-intensive, cycle-sensitive. Salary on card: $68K to $125K. Basis: indicative; a JobDescription.org page gives $72K to $125K for civil engineers generally.[^17]
8. **Academia and Research.** Titles: PhD Researcher, Postdoc, Research Engineer, Assistant to Full Professor, National Lab Scientist. Reality: PhD-gated; many faculty also hold the PE. Salary on card (before this update): $60K postdoc to $150K+ tenured, with faculty "starting ~$90K to $115K (9-month)". Sourced correction: NRSA postdoc Level 0 is $63,480 (FY2026), and BLS reports postsecondary engineering teachers (all engineering fields, all ranks) at a median of $106,910 with a 90th percentile of $200,030 (May 2023).[^18][^19]

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **Provenance.** Most figures in the first pass came from search-result excerpts, not full-page reads. Figures verified from full pages on 2026-11-23: the ASCE 2025 report, the Drexel PhD page, the Knowledge Sourcing page, NIH NRSA stipends, and BLS engineering-teacher wages.
- **PE premium range.** $15K to $20K (CareerBldr), $30K to $42K (Monograph), $40K (ASCE 2025), versus PEwise's caution that no general premium is established. The artifact's "$15K to $40K" spans the sourced estimates; the true premium varies by specialty, employer, and location.
- **BLS median.** $99,590 is the May 2024 figure. A May 2023 figure of $95,890 appears in some sources. PEwise, a secondary source, cites a May 2025 BLS median of $100,840; this was not confirmed against BLS directly.[^5]
- **Salary ceilings.** The $130K to $160K+ structural ceiling, the $140K+ transportation ceiling, and the land-development range are indicative from aggregator guides, not primary surveys.
- **Unverified artifact claims.** (a) "Many states are moving toward requiring education beyond a bachelor's for licensure", (b) structural practice "often effectively requires" an MS, (c) advisor grants "often NSF, DOT, or agency funded", (d) construction bonuses of "$10K to $25K", (e) the IIJA $1.2 trillion figure.
- **Geography.** Pay and demand vary strongly by state and metro; public-sector salary scales and pension rules differ by state. All content is US-specific; licensure rules are set state by state.
