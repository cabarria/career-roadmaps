# The Environmental Engineering Career Roadmap (USA): Undergraduate to PhD, Stage 1 Research Synthesis

Prepared 2026-11-23. Provenance note: this file was written after the first version of the roadmap was built. It combines the seven searches run for that build with follow-up searches and page fetches run on 2026-11-23 to close gaps (BLS data year, PhD structure, EPA changes, PFAS rules, software, competitions, faculty pay). The roadmap was then revised so that its claims trace to this file. Claims that could not be sourced were removed or softened. Several figures come only from search-result excerpts, and page fetches were summarized by a fetch tool, so re-check key figures on the source page before relying on them. Definitional course content (for example, what a unit-process lab involves) is standard curriculum description and is not individually sourced.

## TL;DR

- This is a small occupation. BLS counts 39,100 environmental engineer jobs in 2025, a May 2025 median wage of $107,110 (10th percentile $69,990, 90th percentile $162,220), 6% projected growth from 2025 to 2035, and about 2,300 openings a year. [S1]
- A bachelor's degree is the entry credential, and BLS says licensure is not required for entry-level work. Civil, chemical, and general engineering degrees are listed as routes into the same occupation. [S1]
- Employment is concentrated in engineering services (24%), state government (20%), and consulting (19%). BLS industry medians run narrowly, from $101,840 (consulting) to $115,220 (manufacturing). Self-reported Glassdoor data claim larger premiums in energy and utilities. [S1, S36]
- Whether a master's is needed is unsettled. Old Dominion says it is most often required for higher-level positions, but BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education and no source quantified a pay premium. [S1, S13]
- The federal picture shifted in 2025 to 2026. EPA announced in July 2025 that it would eliminate its Office of Research and Development. In May 2026 it proposed extending the PFOA and PFOS compliance deadline to April 2031 and rescinding limits on four other PFAS. PFOA and PFOS remain CERCLA hazardous substances. [S28, S29]
- Active research areas include PFAS removal, ML-designed membranes, and digital twins (one review counts output rising from one paper in 2015 to 41 in 2024). Practitioner views on hype versus utility were not found. [S24 to S27]

## Key Findings

1. **BLS figures changed between data years.** Search excerpts quoted May 2024 numbers: median $104,170, 4% growth for 2024 to 2034, about 3,000 openings, 39,400 jobs, and 10th and 90th percentiles of $64,950 and $161,910. A page fetch on 2026-11-23 returned May 2025 numbers: median $107,110, 6% growth for 2025 to 2035, about 2,300 openings, 39,100 jobs, and percentiles of $69,990 and $162,220. The roadmap uses the newer set. [S1, S30, S31]
2. **Several degrees lead into the same occupation.** BLS lists a bachelor's in environmental engineering or a related field such as civil, chemical, or general engineering as typical entry education. Some employers prefer internship or co-op experience. [S1]
3. **The employer mix is consulting- and government-heavy.** Engineering services employ 24% of environmental engineers, state government 20%, management, scientific, and technical consulting 19%, local government 7%, and manufacturing 7%. [S1]
4. **ABET sets a common backbone.** The criteria require math through differential equations, probability and statistics, calculus-based physics, chemistry (stoichiometry, equilibrium, kinetics), earth science, biological science, and fluid mechanics, plus material and energy balances and fate and transport across air, water, and soil. [S5]
5. **Program structure differs by school.** Oklahoma's core resembles civil engineering for two years, then goes strictly environmental. Oregon State shares a core with chemical and bioengineering. Syracuse ESF splits two years of core from upper-level ecological, geospatial, and water resources work. [S4, S6, S8]
6. **Master's formats vary widely.** Brown's non-thesis MS is eight courses. Old Dominion offers thesis, project, and course options. UW requires 42 credits and Drexel 45 quarter credits. [S11, S13, S15, S17]
7. **PhD milestones are concrete and strict.** Berkeley runs an oral preliminary exam and a qualifying exam with a written prospectus, and typically takes four to five years after the MS. UW runs a week-long qualifying exam with one retake allowed. [S19, S20]
8. **Pay sources disagree.** BLS industry medians span about $102K to $115K. Glassdoor (self-reported) puts energy, mining, and utilities near $150.8K in median total pay. Payscale ranges differ sharply by filter (for example, $75,476 average base for AECOM environmental engineers with consulting skills versus $61,842 with Civil 3D skills). [S1, S35, S36]
9. **The federal and regulatory environment is in flux.** ORD elimination and PFAS rule proposals both landed in the last 18 months. [S28, S29]
10. **Computation is a growing research area.** A review of 147 water-sector digital twin studies and a new ASCE task committee both point to growth, but reviews also flag technical challenges and knowledge gaps. [S24, S25, S26]

## Undergraduate (Years 1 to 4)

**Program structure.**

- Oregon State's ABET-accredited program draws on civil and chemical engineering, water and wastewater treatment, hazardous substance management, air pollution, and environmental health. Design is introduced in year one and developed in years three and four. The school's core is shared with chemical and bioengineering: first-year engineering, materials and energy balances, thermodynamics, transport phenomena, and senior-year unit operations. Internships run through the school and the MECOP co-op program. [S4]
- Oklahoma's core is similar to civil engineering, and the last two years are strictly environmental: air pollution control, water and wastewater engineering, and solid and hazardous waste management, plus two professional electives and a two-semester capstone. [S6]
- Colorado State requires organic and environmental chemistry, microbiology, hydrology, statistics, environmental toxicology, and water treatment, with technical electives and a year-long, term-based capstone. [S7]
- Syracuse ESF's program is ABET accredited as environmental engineering. It runs two years of core science and engineering, then upper-level work in ecological, geospatial, water resources, and environmental resources engineering, with a design course in year one and three engineering electives. [S8]
- MIT's Course 1-ENG offers an Environment track combining science with engineering applications to improve land, water, air, and public health. [S9]
- UT Austin requires six credit hours of technical electives from one of four specialization areas plus an approved design elective. [S10]

**Core curriculum themes.** Mass and energy balances, fluid mechanics and hydraulics, environmental chemistry, fate and transport, water and wastewater treatment, hydrology, microbiology, thermodynamics, statistics, toxicology, air pollution control, and solid and hazardous waste. [S4 to S8]

**Weeder courses.** A search for notoriously hard environmental engineering courses returned only generic "hardest majors" content and low-reliability blog posts. No reliable source named weeder courses, so the roadmap names none.

**Hands-on skills.** Team design projects (Oregon State frames them as open-ended, realistic problems with economic, operational, and reliability constraints), capstones of one to two semesters, and fieldwork. BLS says environmental engineers work in offices and in the field. [S1, S4, S6, S7]

**Foundational tools.**

- EPANET: EPA's public-domain software for modeling drinking-water distribution systems (pipe networks, tanks, pumps, water quality). [S23]
- SWMM: EPA's free, open-source model for runoff and drainage, used for flood-control design, detention facilities, combined sewer overflow reduction, inflow and infiltration, and green infrastructure. EPA says it is FEMA-approved for National Flood Insurance Program studies. [S22]
- ArcGIS, AutoCAD, MODFLOW, R and Python, and EQuIS appear in an environmental-scientist software list (low-reliability source). Payscale tracks Civil 3D as a skill for AECOM environmental engineers. [S35, S37]

**Extracurriculars.**

- WEF Student Design Competition: individuals or teams design a solution to a water quality problem. It starts at the Member Association level and ends at WEFTEC with three categories (Wastewater Design, Water Environment Design, Circular Water Economy). [S21]
- Co-ops and internships: BLS says some employers prefer them. [S1]
- Named REU programs, Engineers Without Borders, and AWWA student chapters were not researched.

## Master's (Years ~5 to 6)

**Program types.**

- Brown's non-thesis MS requires eight courses, covering water and wastewater treatment, air pollution control, soil and groundwater remediation, site characterization and risk assessment, life cycle assessment, and climate change mitigation. [S11]
- Old Dominion offers a thesis option (24 credit hours of coursework plus a 6-credit thesis), a project option (27 plus 3), and a course option (30 credit hours with a comprehensive exam). It also offers a distance-learning MS. [S13]
- Kansas offers three options; Option A is 30 credit hours including a 6 to 10 credit thesis and a final oral exam, with a 3.0 GPA minimum for admission. [S18]
- UW requires 42 credits. Drexel requires 45 quarter credits and four courses in an approved specialization (treatment processes, human risks, water resources, environmental modeling, or air quality). [S15, S17]
- Columbia offers full-time and part-time MS study with three elective specializations (Sustainable Energy, Sustainable Mining and Materials, Water Resources and Climate Risks) and an MS/PhD track. [S14]
- Ohio State's environmental track is an official specialization on the MS and PhD transcript. Northeastern offers both an environmental MS and a civil MS with a water, environmental, and coastal concentration. [S12, S16]

**Specializations named in sources.** Water and wastewater treatment, hazardous and solid waste, water quality, hydrologic processes and water resources, environmental microbiology, air quality, biofuels, nutrient cycling, pollution prevention, remediation, and life cycle assessment. [S11, S13, S15]

**Funding.** The master's pages reviewed do not state assistantship or tuition norms. This was not found. The roadmap tells students to ask programs directly.

**Terminal or bridge.** Old Dominion says master's degrees are most often required to hold higher-level professional positions in industry and in federal, state, and municipal agencies. This is one program's statement. BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education, and no source quantified a pay premium. Drexel requires extra undergraduate coursework for entrants without an ABET-accredited engineering bachelor's. [S1, S13, S15]

**Advanced skills.** Beyond program topic lists, no source gave method or tool detail for master's study. Specific commercial process, air dispersion, and LCA packages were not sourced and were removed from the roadmap. Drexel lists molecular biology, analytical chemistry, and bioinformatic techniques among its research methods. [S15]

## PhD and Research (Years ~7+)

**Structure.**

- Berkeley: 30 units of coursework (at least 12 in the major, 6 in each of two minors, one outside the department). An oral preliminary exam given by three faculty in the first year after the MS. A qualifying exam near the end of the third year of graduate study, run by a four-member committee, with a roughly 10-page prospectus, a 15 to 20 minute presentation, and an oral exam. A failed preliminary exam can be retaken once. A dissertation committee of three (one from outside the major) must read and approve the dissertation. The page says the PhD typically takes an additional four to five years after the MS. [S19]
- UW: the qualifying exam is taken within one to two years of starting the PhD track. It has a 48-hour open-book written exam on three questions, a 48-hour critical review of an assigned journal article, and an oral exam. Passing requires each question at 3 or higher and an overall average of at least 4. Only one retake is allowed, and a failed retake ends PhD study. [S20]

**Funding.** Neither page describes PhD funding in detail (Berkeley names one outside funding source, Environmental Engineers of the Future). This was not confirmed. The roadmap tells students to get funding terms in writing.

**Active frontiers.**

- PFAS removal. A 2025 study by Arizona State and the University of Wisconsin-Madison used machine learning, trained on literature and lab data and validated with molecular simulation, to speed discovery of PFAS-filtering membranes. [S27]
- Digital twins for water treatment. A review of 147 studies (January 2015 to May 2025) found output rising from one paper in 2015 to 41 in 2024. [S25]
- Machine learning in water engineering. ASCE's Environmental and Water Resources Institute approved a Machine Learning and AI in Water Engineering task committee at the end of 2024. [S24]
- Resource recovery. Drexel lists microbial processes for energy and resource recovery from waste, and UW lists resource recovery, among program areas. [S15, S17]
- Water and climate. Columbia's Water Resources and Climate Risks specialization and Kansas's hydroclimatology area (drought mechanisms, climate uncertainty). [S14, S18]
- Energy. Ohio State lists energy production and use among its areas. [S12]

**Policy context for research careers.** EPA announced on July 18, 2025 that it would eliminate its Office of Research and Development (1,540 positions, as many as 1,155 potentially laid off) and replace it with an Office of Applied Science and Environmental Solutions. Total EPA staffing was to fall to 12,448, a cut of more than 3,700 employees or nearly 23% from January 2025. [S28]

## Tools and AI

- **Standard tools by level.** Undergraduate: EPANET, SWMM, GIS, CAD. Software lists in lower-reliability sources add MODFLOW, EQuIS, and R and Python. [S22, S23, S35, S37]
- **Where AI fits.** Research focuses on digital twins for treatment plants, ML-designed membranes, and ML-enabled process optimization. ASCE's journal collection invites work that merges mechanistic treatment modeling with data-driven tools. [S24 to S27]
- **Honest practitioner view.** Not found. Reviews describe growth alongside technical challenges and knowledge gaps, and one arXiv paper studies adversarial attacks on AI water forecasting. No practitioner survey of utility versus hype was located. [S25]
- **Effect on permitting and regulatory work.** Not assessed by any source reviewed.

## Career Paths

Titles below are common usage and are not drawn from a sourced ladder. Pay is shown as the nearest sourced figure. Where no path-specific pay was found, the BLS range for all environmental engineers is used and labeled.

**1. Environmental Consulting**

- Titles: Staff Engineer, Project Engineer, Project Manager, Principal.
- Reality: Engineering services (24% of jobs) and consulting firms (19%) together employ about 43% of environmental engineers. Entry is through a bachelor's; some employers prefer internship or co-op experience. A PE license lets engineers sign off on projects and oversee others' work. Payscale lists an average base near $75K at one large consulting employer (self-reported).
- Salary: BLS medians of $101,840 (consulting) and $109,040 (engineering services), May 2025.
- Sources: [S1, S35]

**2. Water and Wastewater Treatment**

- Titles: Process Engineer, Design Engineer, Plant Engineer, Utility Engineering Manager.
- Reality: UW says alumni manage drinking-water and wastewater plants, and treatment appears in nearly every master's program reviewed. No source gave pay specific to treatment roles.
- Salary: nearest BLS proxies are local government (7% of jobs, median $104,140) and engineering services ($109,040).
- Sources: [S1, S11, S13, S16, S17]

**3. Site Remediation and Geoenvironmental**

- Titles: Field Engineer, Remediation Engineer, Risk Assessor, Program Manager.
- Reality: Brown lists site characterization, risk assessment, and soil and groundwater remediation. PFOA and PFOS remain CERCLA hazardous substances (EPA kept the designation in September 2025, and a D.C. Circuit challenge had oral argument in January 2026). A May 2026 proposal would move the PFOA and PFOS drinking-water compliance deadline from April 2029 to April 2031 and rescind limits for PFHxS, PFNA, GenX, and a hazard index mixture. States are adding their own rules. No source gave pay specific to remediation.
- Salary: no path-specific data. BLS all environmental engineers: $69,990 to $162,220 (10th to 90th percentile).
- Sources: [S1, S11, S29]

**4. Corporate EHS and Sustainability**

- Titles: EHS Engineer, Compliance Engineer, Sustainability Engineer, EHS or ESG Manager.
- Reality: BLS shows manufacturing as the best-paid of the five largest industries (median $115,220, 7% of jobs). Glassdoor (self-reported) puts energy, mining, and utilities at $150,819 median total pay, information technology at $142,085, financial services at $120,425, manufacturing at $118,318, and aerospace and defense at $118,173. One low-reliability guide cites a $153,120 average for crude oil pipeline transportation. BLS data do not confirm a premium that large. Payscale's EHS engineer base pay spans $64K to $120K (10th to 90th percentile), with early-career base of $46K to $83K and experienced base of $76K to $130K.
- Salary: BLS manufacturing median $115,220; Payscale EHS base $64K to $120K.
- Sources: [S1, S32, S34, S36]

**5. Government and Regulatory**

- Titles: Staff Engineer, Permit Engineer, Program Engineer, Supervisory Engineer.
- Reality: State government employs 20% of environmental engineers (median $102,160) and local government 7% (median $104,140). The fetched BLS page shows no federal row; a secondary source puts the federal median near $120,830. Federal staffing is in flux after the 2025 EPA reorganization.
- Salary: BLS medians of $102,160 (state) and $104,140 (local); federal about $120,830 (secondary source).
- Sources: [S1, S28, S31]

**6. Water Resources and Climate Resilience**

- Titles: Water Resources Engineer, Hydrologist, Stormwater Engineer, Resilience Planner.
- Reality: Programs list hydrology, stormwater, river ecohydraulics, hydroclimatology, and climate-risk work. SWMM supports flood-control design, floodplain mapping, and combined sewer overflow reduction. No source gave pay specific to this path.
- Salary: no path-specific data. BLS all environmental engineers: $69,990 to $162,220.
- Sources: [S1, S14, S18, S22]

**7. Environmental Data and Modeling**

- Titles: Environmental Modeler, Data Scientist, GIS Analyst, Modeling Lead.
- Reality: This is a growing research area rather than a measured job category. Digital-twin papers in the water sector rose from one in 2015 to 41 in 2024, and ASCE formed an ML and AI task committee at the end of 2024. Whether this is a distinct hiring track, and whether it pays a premium, was not confirmed.
- Salary: no path-specific data. BLS all environmental engineers: $69,990 to $162,220.
- Sources: [S1, S24, S25, S37]

**8. Research and Academia**

- Titles: PhD Researcher, Postdoc, Research Scientist, Professor.
- Reality: Requires a PhD (four to five years after the MS at Berkeley). Civil and environmental engineering departments post faculty searches, but no source quantified tenure-track competitiveness or typical postdoc pay. The salary figures come from one CU Boulder posting for a geotechnical position, so they are indicative only.
- Salary: one CU Boulder posting lists assistant professor $110,000 to $125,000, associate $125,000 to $150,000, and professor $145,000 to $200,000 (academic year).
- Sources: [S19, S28, S38]

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **Data year.** BLS figures changed between May 2024 and May 2025 data. The roadmap uses May 2025 wages and 2025 to 2035 projections from a page fetch. One third-party salary guide still cites a 7% growth projection, which conflicts with BLS. [S1, S39]
- **Licensure details conflict across secondary sources.** One Research.com article says candidates need four years of work experience before sitting for the FE exam, which contradicts BLS and other guides (FE is often taken right after graduation; the experience requirement applies to the PE exam). It also says only 30% of graduates pursue a PE license, a figure with no traceable source, so the roadmap drops it. The PE exam is computer-based, 80 questions, in a nine-hour appointment window with a fee near $400 (one guide). Experience requirements vary by state. [S1, S31, S32, S33]
- **Industry pay.** BLS medians cluster tightly. Glassdoor and Payscale are self-reported, and Payscale pages filtered by skill or employer give inconsistent ranges. [S1, S34 to S36]
- **Path-specific pay.** No source gave pay for remediation, water resources, data and modeling, or treatment roles. The roadmap shows the nearest BLS figure and says when none exists.
- **Not found.** Weeder courses, master's and PhD funding norms for environmental programs, named REU programs, postdoc pay, practitioner views on AI hype, how AI affects permitting work, and which US employers hire environmental PhDs.
- **Faculty pay.** Based on one posting for a geotechnical search, not an environmental one. The posting's date field was blank; full consideration was by November 16, 2025. [S38]
- **Regional variation.** US-only. Licensure rules, state employment shares (state government employs 20% of environmental engineers nationally), and PFAS rules differ by state. UK, EU, and Latin American routes were not researched.
- **Regulatory flux.** PFAS rule changes were proposals as of a June 2026 legal alert. Check the current status before relying on them. A D.C. Circuit decision on the CERCLA designation was possible later in 2026. [S29]
