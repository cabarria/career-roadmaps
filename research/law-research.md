**Field:** Law, the US Juris Doctor path and the careers it opens (US-centered)
**Compiled:** 2026-11-23

## TL;DR

- In the US, law is a graduate degree. Students earn any bachelor's, then a three-year JD. There is no required pre-law major. Admission turns mostly on GPA and LSAT score.
- The 2026 admission cycle was the biggest in 15 years: 82,487 applicants (up 7.9%) and 579,839 applications (up 10.8%) (S1, S2). LSAC data show the fastest growth in the higher LSAT bands (S3).
- Entry pay is bimodal in practice. NALP's Class of 2025 median is $100,000, with a 92.8% employment rate (S4, S5). Firms of 500+ lawyers start at a median $225,000, firms of 1 to 10 lawyers at $84,000, government at $81,000, public interest at $75,000 (S5).
- Big Law's scale moved in June 2026: Milbank set $235,000 for first years and $455,000 for eighth years, and more than a dozen firms matched within two weeks (S6).
- Financing changed on July 1, 2026. Grad PLUS ended for new borrowers, and professional students (law is named in the statute's definition) can borrow $50,000 a year and $200,000 in total in federal loans (S7, S8). A court stayed the Education Department's narrower "professional degree" definition, but the loan caps themselves stand (S9).
- The bar exam is changing. The NextGen UBE debuted in ten jurisdictions in July 2026. Most others begin July 2028, and New York and New Jersey keep the legacy exam through February 2028 (S10, S11).
- Law-firm AI use is near universal but shallow. ILTA's 2026 survey found 94% of firms using or exploring generative AI, while only two products were fully deployed to lawyers at half or more of firms (S12, S13).
- The law-professor market is small. The final Lawsky 2026 report counts 146 tenure-track hires at 96 schools, and 66% of them had held a fellowship (S14).

## Key Findings

- **Applicant pool:** 82,487 applicants in 2026, up 7.9% from 2025 and 27.4% from 2024. Applications reached 579,839 (S1). An LSAC-based admissions blog reports the largest growth in the 165 to 180 LSAT range and substantial growth at 160 to 164 (S3).
- **Political science is the largest feeder major, but the best breakdown is old.** LSAC's 2015-16 data show 12,693 political science applicants, 18.1% of the total (S15). AccessLex put the 2014-15 share at 18.5% (S16). No newer LSAC major breakdown was found.
- **LSAT format:** two scored Logical Reasoning sections and one Reading Comprehension section, plus one unscored variable section (S17). Starting with the August 2026 test, nearly all test takers sit it in person at Prometric centers, with limited exceptions such as certain disability accommodations (S17, S18).
- **GRE:** ETS says 130+ ABA-approved law schools accept the GRE, including every T14 school. The ABA permitted it in 2021 (S19). ETS is an interested party for that count.
- **Experiential credits:** ABA Standard 303 requires six. A May 2025 proposal to double it to twelve was pulled back by the council on August 22, 2025, after deans objected. The revised version had delayed the start to 2032 (S20). The ABA's 2026 dashboard and August 2026 recap list no new vote on it (S21, S22).
- **Cost:** average total cost of a law degree is about $217,480 including living expenses, and average tuition is about $46,000 a year (S23). Public in-state tuition averaged $31,430 in fall 2024 (S24). The 2024 1L class expected to graduate with $76,300 of debt on average, and 17% expected $150,000 or more (S25).
- **Loans from July 1, 2026:** professional students get $50,000 a year and $200,000 aggregate in Direct Unsubsidized loans. The aggregate does not include undergraduate loans (S7, S26). All borrowers face a $257,500 lifetime cap, with narrow exceptions (S8).
- **Repayment and PSLF:** the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) launched July 1, 2026. Only on-time RAP payments qualify for PSLF on new loans (S27). From July 1, 2026 an employer found to have engaged in activities with a "substantial illegal purpose" can lose qualifying status (S28).
- **Bar passage:** first-time takers from ABA schools passed at 84.1% in 2025, up from 83.02%. For 2023 graduates, 92.15% passed within two years (S29).
- **NextGen UBE:** first administered in July 2026 in Connecticut, Guam, Idaho, Maryland, Missouri, the Northern Mariana Islands, Oregon, Palau, the US Virgin Islands, and Washington (S10). Many other jurisdictions begin in July 2028. Several accept NextGen transfer scores from July 2026 (S11).
- **Licensure pathways:** Nevada's Comprehensive Licensing Examination (a closed-book multiple-choice exam, a performance test, and supervised practice) begins February 2027 and is the only pathway, not an alternative (S30, S31). Oregon's Supervised Practice Portfolio Examination requires 675 hours of supervised practice plus a portfolio (S32). Utah's pathway requires 240 hours of supervised practice and a written performance exam (S33).
- **Employment:** NALP Class of 2025: 92.8% employed, a $100,000 national median, 84.6% in jobs requiring bar admission. The class shrank 7.0% to 36,206, and entry-level jobs fell 7.7% to 32,619 (S4, S5, S34).
- **Federal hiring fell:** NALP's press release title cites a sharp drop in federal government and public interest hiring (S35).
- **Lawyers (BLS):** 863,700 jobs, $159,670 median (May 2025), 5% growth 2025-35, about 28,700 openings a year, 11% self-employed, typical entry education a professional degree (S36).
- **Lawsky 2026:** 146 tenure-track hires at 96 schools in the final version posted October 12, 2026. Of them 97 (about 66%) had a fellowship, 60 (about 41%) a clerkship, 84 (about 58%) a higher degree (S14). A June 2026 TaxProf post cited 105 hires from a draft (S37).
- **AI sanctions:** Bloomberg Law reported an estimated 712 decisions worldwide on hallucinated content in court filings as of late 2025, about 90% written in 2025 (S38).

## Undergraduate

### What the pre-law years are for

- **No required major.** The ABA recommends none. Political science supplies the largest single share of applicants (about 18% in the latest breakdown found, 2015-16) (S15, S16).
- **Majors and LSAT scores.** LSAC's 2017-18 figures show philosophy applicants averaging 157.2 on the LSAT (83.0% admitted) and criminal justice 145.9 (61.2% admitted), with political science at 153.6 (80.5%) (S39). Those are applicant averages by major, not proof the major causes the score.
- **GPA.** Law schools see the GPA LSAC calculates from every undergraduate transcript, which can differ from the school's own GPA. LSAC's policy page says it calculates a GPA per institution and a cumulative GPA of all undergraduate work (S66). Graduate coursework is not counted (S67). For the 2027-28 cycle and later, college credits earned in high school are excluded (S66).

### The LSAT

- Structure and in-person change as in Key Findings (S17, S18).
- LSAC's own blog shows the August 2026 test moved to a new testing interface (S40).
- LSAT volume stayed strong despite the in-person change: registrations for September 2026 were more than 40% above September 2025 (S3).
- Score inflation: the 2026 cycle again grew fastest in the 165 to 180 band (S3). Plan for the possibility of retaking.

### Applying

- Rolling admissions, with earlier applying becoming the norm. LSAC reported a significant increase in applications submitted before January in the 2026 cycle (S42).

### Patent bar route

- The USPTO's General Requirements Bulletin sets three categories (A: listed bachelor's degrees, B: other degrees with scientific coursework, C: practical experience such as passing the Fundamentals of Engineering exam). In August 2026 it moved Biomedical Science from B to A (S43, S44). A technical bachelor's is the entry ticket to the patent bar, which is why STEM majors have an edge in patent practice.

## Master's (the JD)

For this field the "master's stage" is the three-year JD, a professional degree. A master's of law (LLM) is a separate add-on.

### Curriculum and experience

- **1L year:** the core doctrinal courses. The retrieved sources do not name particular weeder courses, so none is claimed.
- **Experiential learning:** six credits required under ABA Standard 303 (clinics, externships, simulations) (S20).
- **Recruiting timeline:** the LSAC study of accelerated Big Law recruitment reports that some firms now recruit as early as the first semester of law school (S45). The retrieved page gives no figure for the share of offers made outside school-run interview programs, so the earlier JSX figure was removed.
- **Summer associate pipeline:** the same study cites roughly 96 to 98% of summer program participants receiving return offers (S45).
- **Law review, moot court, journals** are standard credentials. The retrieved sources do not quantify them.

### Cost and financing

- Figures in Key Findings (S7, S8, S23, S24, S25).
- A federal court stayed the Education Department's narrowed "professional degree" definition on June 24, 2026, in a case brought by nurse practitioner groups and others. Law was already among the ten fields in the pre-existing regulatory definition, and the court's stay did not halt the loan caps themselves (S9, S46).
- Scholarships and net tuition vary widely by school. LawHub publishes school-level net tuition (S47).

### Licensure

- Bar exam, NextGen transition, and alternative pathways as in Key Findings (S10 to S12 and S29 to S33).
- Foreign-trained lawyers: New York and California let some foreign-educated applicants sit their bar exams. In New York this runs through Court of Appeals Rule 520.6, which allows an LLM to cure a durational or substantive deficiency (S48). Foreign-educated candidates were 36% of July 2025 New York test takers (S48).

## PhD / Research

Law has no research doctorate that most lawyers pursue. The advanced stage is:

- **LLM:** a one-year master of laws, used by foreign-trained lawyers for US bar eligibility and by US graduates to specialize (tax, for example). Eligibility to sit a state bar is not guaranteed by an LLM (S48).
- **SJD or JSD:** the research doctorate for aspiring professors. Lawsky's data show many tenure-track hires hold a higher degree or fellowship (S14).
- **Fellowships:** most reported tenure-track hires had held a fellowship (about 66% in 2026) (S14). Stanford Law's own guidance says most schools hesitate to hire straight out of law school without a PhD (S49).
- **Active scholarship areas:** AI and the profession, bar exam reform, and the legal-education accreditation fights (S20, S21, S38). The retrieved sources do not rank research frontiers more specifically.

## Tools and AI

- **Research platforms:** Westlaw (with CoCounsel / Westlaw Advantage) and Lexis (with Protégé) are standard. Westlaw Advantage was fully deployed to lawyers at 50% of surveyed firms (S13).
- **Firm adoption (ILTA 2026):** 94% of 500+ responding firms using or exploring generative AI, up from 80%. Microsoft 365 Copilot 76%, CoCounsel 44%, Claude 44%, Harvey 43% (S12, S13).
- **Deployment trails adoption:** only Copilot (52%) and Westlaw Advantage (50%) had been fully deployed among lawyers at half or more of respondent firms (S13). In the 2025 survey, only 8% of firms with Copilot had issued it to all employees (S50).
- **Hallucinated citations:** courts have sanctioned lawyers for filing fabricated cases. Bloomberg Law counted an estimated 712 decisions by late 2025 (S38). Q1 2026 saw at least $145,000 in sanctions in the US, per JD Supra tracking (S51). The Lindell lawyers were fined $3,000 each (S52).
- **The duty is verification.** NPR's reporting stresses lawyers remain responsible for the accuracy of filings however generated (S52). No retrieved source says the NextGen bar exam tests AI verification, so that claim was removed from the JSX.
- **Bloomberg's reading of the market:** with AI handling more rote tasks, associates are expected to do more sophisticated work earlier, and firms need tech-savvy associates to supervise AI output (S6).

## Career Paths

### 1. Large law firms

- **Titles:** Summer associate, Associate, Counsel, Partner.
- **Reality:** Firms of 500+ lawyers start at a median $225,000 (S5). Milbank's June 2026 scale starts at $235,000 and reaches $455,000 in year eight, and more than a dozen firms had adopted it two weeks later (S6). Revenue per lawyer at Milbank was $2.085 million and profits per equity partner $7.6 million (S6). Hiring runs through summer programs that start recruiting early (S45).
- **Salary:** $225,000 median (NALP, firms of 500+); $235,000 to $455,000 on the Milbank scale. Not every firm matches it.
- **Sources:** S5, S6, S45.

### 2. Small firms and solo practice

- **Titles:** Associate, Solo practitioner, Of counsel, Partner.
- **Reality:** The broadest share of practice: family, criminal defense, personal injury, real estate, estates, immigration. About 11% of lawyers are self-employed (S36). Entry is more open but pay is modest and income depends on building clients.
- **Salary:** $84,000 median for firms of 1 to 10 lawyers (NALP Class of 2025) (S5). $159,670 median for all lawyers (BLS, May 2025) (S36).
- **Sources:** S5, S36.

### 3. Government

- **Titles:** Assistant district attorney, Agency attorney, JAG officer, US attorney.
- **Reality:** Prosecutors, attorneys general, city law departments, federal agencies, and military JAG. NALP's Class of 2025 release flags a sharp drop in federal government hiring (S35). PSLF can apply, but the employer rule changed on July 1, 2026 (S28).
- **Salary:** $81,000 median starting salary in government jobs (NALP, Class of 2025) (S5).
- **Sources:** S5, S28, S35.

### 4. Public interest and legal aid

- **Titles:** Staff attorney, Public defender, Supervising attorney, Legal director.
- **Reality:** Legal aid, public defenders, advocacy organizations. Caseloads are heavy and pay grows slowly, so PSLF and school repayment programs often decide affordability (S27, S28).
- **Salary:** $75,000 median for public interest jobs (NALP Class of 2025) (S5). In NALP's 2023 public service survey: public defender entry $69,600, civil legal services entry $64,200, public defenders at 11 to 15 years about $106,883 (S53).
- **Sources:** S5, S53.

### 5. Judicial clerkships and the bench

- **Titles:** Law clerk, Staff attorney, Administrative law judge, Judge.
- **Reality:** Clerkships are fixed-term, one to two years, and feed academia, appellate practice, and top firms. Judges "must be appointed or elected," a process that often requires political support (S54).
- **Salary:** federal law clerk base starts at JSP-11, step 1: $74,678 outside high-cost localities (S55). NALP's median for judicial clerkships among the Class of 2025 is $72,533 (S5, see Uncertainties). Administrative law judges, adjudicators, and hearing officers: $117,860 median (BLS, May 2025). Federal district judges: $249,900 in 2026 (S56, S57).
- **Sources:** S5, S54, S55, S56, S57.

### 6. In-house counsel and compliance

- **Titles:** Counsel, Senior counsel, Associate general counsel, General counsel.
- **Reality:** Usually a second job after several years at a firm. Compliance officers form a related route that does not always require a JD (BLS lists a bachelor's as typical entry education) (S58).
- **Salary:** MLA's 2026 report: senior counsel or counsel median total cash $270,600; general counsel $500,000 (private) and $739,000 (public) in total cash; chief compliance officer $468,000 (S59). Compliance officers overall: $80,730 median (BLS, May 2025) (S58).
- **Sources:** S58, S59.

### 7. Academia

- **Titles:** Research fellow, Visiting assistant professor, Assistant professor, Professor.
- **Reality:** The final Lawsky 2026 report counts 146 tenure-track hires at 96 schools. Of them 66% had a fellowship, 41% a clerkship, 58% a higher degree (S14). Hiring has run above its long-run average: 92 per year across 2014 to 2026, 122 per year across 2022 to 2025 (S37).
- **Salary:** Payscale 2026 self-reported averages: about $115,000 for assistant professor of law, about $152,000 for professor of law (range $111,000 to $312,000), $128,000 for "law professor" (S60). Law professors historically had the highest average academic pay (2007-08 Chronicle data) (S61).
- **Sources:** S14, S37, S60, S61.

### 8. Legal operations, legal tech, and dispute resolution

- **Titles:** Legal operations specialist, E-discovery or legal tech manager, Mediator or arbitrator, Head of legal operations.
- **Reality:** Jobs where a JD helps but a license is not the point. Robert Half calls legal operations specialist one of the fastest-growing roles as teams adopt AI (S62). Mediation and arbitration is small: 10,500 jobs and about 300 openings a year (S63).
- **Salary:** Robert Half 2026 national ranges: legal operations manager $84,750 to $119,250, legal operations specialist about $74,750 to $85,500 mid-range, legal operations director $152,000 to $200,500 (S62). Mediators and arbitrators: $75,530 median (BLS, May 2025) (S63).
- **Sources:** S62, S63.

## Uncertainties and Regional Variation

- **US only.** Everything here is US-centered. Law degrees and licensure are different elsewhere, and Panama and most civil-law countries start law as an undergraduate degree.
- **Salary figures mix sources.** NALP medians cover full-time, long-term jobs among graduates who reported salaries. BLS figures cover all lawyers. Payscale and Robert Half figures are self-reported or recruiter-projected.
- **Clerkship pay.** NALP's $72,533 median for judicial clerkships (S5) is lower than the federal JSP-11 base of $74,678 (S55) because it includes state and local clerkships and locality varies.
- **Federal hiring drop.** NALP's press release title confirms a sharp federal and public interest hiring drop (S35). No percentage is quoted in the roadmap.
- **Lawsky counts.** A draft in June 2026 listed 105 hires (S37). The final October 2026 version lists 146 (S14). The final figure is used.
- **Political science share.** The 18% figure is a decade old.
- **Loans in flux.** The professional-degree definition is under litigation and the Department says it will defend its rule (S9, S46). Law is covered either way.
- **ABA experiential credits.** Withdrawn in August 2025, with no new vote found. The council could revive it.
- **NextGen timing varies by state.** Some adopt in 2026, some 2027, most 2028, and Florida, Georgia, and Hawaii give their own exams (S11, S64).
- **Law professor pay.** No AAUP law-specific figure was retrieved, so Payscale averages stand in. AAUP's all-field data show full professors averaging $163,836 (S65).
