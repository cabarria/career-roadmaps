# AI resilience research prompt

Give this prompt to a research agent with access to the Career Roadmaps repository and the web.

## Assignment

Research how AI could change the work, hiring pathways, and career preparation described across all 28 Career Roadmaps. Produce a cited assessment for every roadmap and a reproducible candidate occupation-level dataset for the **AI resilience** tab on `/careers` if the evidence supports one. Write for students choosing a field and for maintainers who need to reproduce the research.

Use the United States as the comparison geography, since the site's featured occupations use U.S. labor data. State the evidence cutoff and distinguish current evidence from a three- to five-year scenario. Focus on generative AI, including tools that can carry out multistep workflows. Identify robotics, traditional automation, and other AI systems separately where they matter. Label evidence from other countries and explain how much it can tell us about U.S. work.

Define **AI resilience** as an occupation's capacity to retain demand for meaningful human work as AI changes its tasks. Examine which tasks can be delegated, where humans still add value or bear responsibility, who can adopt the technology, and how productivity could affect demand. Exposure, use, productivity, and employment effects are different outcomes. A role can have high AI use and healthy employment demand, while its junior hiring or training pathway still changes.

## Research procedure

1. **Map occupations.** Inventory each roadmap's featured occupation and every career card. For each path, record the displayed title, career stage, typical setting, best-fit classification code and edition, and mapping quality (exact, narrower, broader, proxy, or unavailable). Explain what a proxy covers and what it misses. Keep the website's stable IDs. Where a source splits a broad occupation across codes, document the component definitions and any defensible aggregation weights. Keep unmatched paths visible with a reason.
2. **Build a task profile.** For each featured occupation identify four to eight tasks or task clusters that drive the result, with source task IDs and published frequency or importance where available. For the other paths, identify the tasks and constraints that materially change the assessment. For each significant task, record what AI demonstrably does, the level of supervision and verification needed, and the work that still requires a person. Use explicit anchors such as human-led, AI-assisted, and delegated with review. State where time shares are unavailable. Counts or importance ratings alone do not establish time shares.
3. **Triangulate evidence.** For each featured occupation use an occupational task source plus independent evidence on real use, capability, or labor outcomes wherever available. Prefer primary data, studies with a clear design, and sector or regulator evidence. For material claims record author, title, URL, date and observation period, location, population or sample, exact table or page, and limitations. Distinguish experiments and observed employment data from vendor usage, surveys, forecasts, and expert judgment. Report material disagreements and gaps alongside the finding.
4. **Assess roles and pathways.** Explain what current tools change, what could change in three to five years under stated adoption assumptions, and what remains uncertain. Consider implementation costs, data access, quality checks, privacy, accountability, licensure, physical presence, and interpersonal trust wherever evidence makes them relevant. Examine junior versus senior tasks and differences across settings. Give two or three concrete preparation actions per roadmap, tied to the researched tasks and appropriate education stages.
5. **Evaluate scoring before ranking.** Prefer a current, transparent, reproducible U.S.-relevant method whose construct fits the definition of resilience. If proposing a composite, define indicators, their scales, normalization, weights, and minimum evidence before inspecting the final rankings. Report technical exposure, adoption, continuing human responsibilities, and demand evidence separately. Explain which belong in the score and why. Keep scenario assumptions visible and show which scores or rankings change substantially under alternate weights or adoption assumptions. A score of 100 must mean greater estimated resilience on the chosen scale and 0 less resilience. Explain the endpoints and avoid interpreting a score as a probability of keeping a job. Treat a reversal of an exposure index as an exposure-derived measure with the original construct and limitations. Publish a resilience score only when the method and occupation match support it.

### Starting sources to verify

- Use U.S. occupational task statements and work context to establish job duties. Confirm the data release and occupational taxonomy. [O*NET database](https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html) · [BLS classification and crosswalks](https://www.bls.gov/soc/2018/crosswalks.htm)
- Use U.S. employment, requirements, and projections for labor context. Projections alone do not identify the effect of AI. [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/) · [BLS Employment Projections Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) · [BLS Occupational Requirements Survey](https://www.bls.gov/ors/)
- Compare published approaches to technical exposure and occupational transition. Check their data vintage, taxonomy, evidence for U.S. applicability, and whether ranking inputs can be reproduced. [ILO occupational exposure study](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure) · [OpenAI AI Jobs Transition Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/the-ai-jobs-transition-framework_report.pdf)
- Use real-world usage and task-success studies as evidence about the studied tools and populations. Compare their findings with independent studies and original evidence from affected sectors. [Anthropic Economic Index methods and evidence](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)

## Deliverables and checks

Save the research in `research/ai-resilience/` as you work. Start with a coverage ledger so another researcher can resume from the last completed group.

- `report.md` explains the definitions, methods, findings across groups, limitations, and what students can do. Link a profile under `roadmaps/<slug>.md` for each of the 28 roadmaps. Each profile lists every career path, its occupation match, core task evidence, current and scenario findings, uncertainty, and preparation recommendations.
- `evidence.csv` records the occupation or path ID, roadmap slug, task or claim, evidence type, classification and code, source URL, publication and access dates, observation period, geography, locator, and caveats. `coverage.csv` records each roadmap and career path, match quality, assessment status, and reason for scores withheld. Include score inputs and calculation steps if you estimate scores. A reviewer should be able to trace a score back to the source observations.
- When justified, include a separate `candidate-dataset.json` containing one dataset object with `id`, `label`, `metric` set to `ai-resilience`, `source` with `name` and `url`, `geography`, `period`, `methodology`, and `scores`. Each score uses an existing `careerId` and a finite numeric `value` from 0 to 100. Use `sourceTitle` and `sourceUrl` where they clarify the original occupation label and evidence. Keep the comparison on one common method and period. Omit unsupported scores and explain omissions in the coverage ledger. Keep the candidate separate from the site's production data for review.

Before handing off, match the profile and coverage lists against every catalog slug and Career Paths tab. Confirm unique candidate IDs and occupation rows, valid score bounds, traceable evidence links, clear proxy labels, and consistent scope. Reproduce at least one proposed score directly from the recorded inputs and verify that uncertainty and sensitivity are visible. If the evidence supports profiles but no comparable dataset, deliver the complete profiles and ledger with a concrete explanation of what evidence would close the gap.
