## Featured occupation

**Environmental engineers** — stable career ID `environmental-engineers`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `17-2081`.

Environmental engineers; other scientists, technicians and treatment operators are not included.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 17-2081.00 Environmental Engineers.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 39,100 jobs in 2025; +6.3% projected change, 2025–35; 2,300 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 17-2081](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

EPA's 2024 water guidance reports limited AI use at that date and describes possible monitoring and process applications, mostly broader AI/control automation. Generative tools assist drafts and data summaries, not field sampling or regulatory responsibility. NCEES requires responsible oversight of licensed work. ASCE's 2026 AI RACE guidance likewise retains professional responsibility for infrastructure decisions.

**Independent sources:** [Guidance on Improving Cybersecurity at Drinking Water and Wastewater Systems](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-08/epa-guidance-on-improving-cybersecurity-at-drinking-water-and-wastewater-systems-1.pdf), [Policy Manual, PS 6.10 Responsible Use of AI in Engineering and Surveying](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Policy-manual_August-2025_web.pdf), [The AI Jobs Transition Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/the-ai-jobs-transition-framework_report.pdf), [AI Roadmap for Application in Civil Engineering (AI RACE)](https://www.asce.org/initiatives/ai-race).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Workflow agents may speed environmental reports, permit preparation and model comparisons. Demand is also shaped by public infrastructure, climate and remediation obligations; physical site variability and regulatory decisions remain gates. Utilities face procurement, reliability and data-access costs.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.
