# Business Administration: AI resilience

Evidence cutoff **2026-11-25** · United States · scenario horizon **2029–31**.

## Featured occupation

**Management analysts** — stable career ID `management-analysts`; BLS 2025 National Employment Matrix `13-1111`.

Management analysts cover consulting and organizational analysis; not C-suite executives or every business graduate.

**O*NET-SOC 2019 matches (release 31.0):** 13-1111.00 Management Analysts.

**Labor context (not AI's effect):** 1,077,100 jobs in 2025; +10.1% projected change, 2025–35; 94,100 average annual openings ([BLS Table 1.2, row 13-1111](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)).

## Current evidence

In a randomized field experiment involving 758 consultants, AI improved speed and quality on selected tasks within its capability frontier, while outcomes vary outside that frontier. Research and slide production are exposed; stakeholder diagnosis, interviews, negotiation and implementation remain human-led. Operating roles have different responsibility than external analysts.

**Independent sources:** [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/), [The AI Jobs Transition Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/the-ai-jobs-transition-framework_report.pdf), [How Does AI Change Labor Demand? Evidence from 41 Countries](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/how-does-ai-change-labor-demand/), [Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about Recent Employment Effects of AI](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf).

## 2029–31 conditional scenario

Agents could execute research, modeling and presentation workflows, making teams smaller or expanding coverage. International affiliate evidence suggests junior workforce shares may decline mainly through senior growth, not necessarily layoffs. Client data, accuracy, procurement and implementation constraints affect adoption. Managers remain accountable for resource and people decisions.

This scenario assumes approved data access, higher agent reliability on bounded workflows, and human review appropriate to risk. A slower rollout preserves more manual work; faster adoption could compress routine junior tasks. Neither establishes employment probabilities.
