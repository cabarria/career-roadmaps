## Numerical data available for comparison

| Data | What the sources measure | Conditions for comparing values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occupational pay | Occupational median, mean, 10th/90th percentiles, and some industry or regional snapshots. Commercial surveys and postings add starting, base, bonus, and total compensation estimates. | Match occupation, statistic, currency, geography, period, and compensation basis. Keep cohorts, postings and self-reported totals in their own datasets. |
| Occupational outlook | Baseline employment, projected employment change, annual openings, and some sector employment, growth, and self-employment shares. | Match occupation and projection release. Annual openings include replacements and career exits as well as growth. Record estimates as published. |
| Education and funding | Degree lengths, credits, tuition, total attendance costs, stipends, debt and borrowing. | Retain program, cohort, and units. Track the start and end points of durations and the time basis of money figures. |
| Admissions, credentials and placement | Admissions or match rates, exam counts and passage, experience hours, attrition, licensure time, job placement and faculty outcomes. | Keep denominator, cohort, time window and jurisdiction. First-time pass rates and repeat-test rates answer different questions. |
| AI use and labor effects | Use and sentiment surveys, task-exposure estimates, occupational rankings, and reported workforce changes. | Keep the population and method with each number. A comparable AI resilience score needs a documented occupation match, common scale, and consistent methodology. |
| Job satisfaction | CareerExplorer career happiness ratings, GSS satisfaction estimates, and PayScale satisfaction percentages. | Compare values within each survey. Each survey measures a different population or question. [Survey comparison notes](satisfaction-and-degree-regret.md) |

## Shared summary and JSON

Every website roadmap displays median annual wage, projected job growth, projected annual openings, and baseline jobs for one named U.S. occupation near the top. The occupation is a path the roadmap covers. For broader fields, the caption identifies where the occupation covers only one slice of the field or includes workers outside it. The website replaces overlapping figures in the original summary areas and keeps the field-specific figures. Standalone React artifacts still display their original summaries.

The shared numbers use [BLS Employment Projections Table 1.2](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm), checked November 24, 2026. It pairs 2025 employment and May 2025 wage estimates with 2025–35 projections. The JSON keeps the table’s one-decimal-place growth rates, which can differ from the rounded figures in the Occupational Outlook Handbook. Employment and annual openings are converted from thousands to counts at the precision published by BLS. Some research notes use the previous 2024–34 edition or different occupation groupings. The table also supplies the occupational data gaps in the Molecular Biology and Medicine research notes.

[`src/career-data.json`](../src/career-data.json) stores the following:

- `careers` gives each occupation a stable ID, BLS National Employment Matrix code, title, and related roadmap slugs.
- `roadmapSummaries` maps each roadmap slug to the occupation shown in its four metrics. A `note` explains the scope of a representative occupation where useful.
- `degreeFields` gives each surveyed academic major field a stable ID, title, and links to related roadmaps.
- `datasets` contains earnings and the [job satisfaction and degree regret surveys](satisfaction-and-degree-regret.md) used by `/careers`. Each `scores[].value` is a JSON number in the dataset’s unit.
- `employmentOutlook` records the shared source, geography, projection years, linked earnings dataset ID, and values keyed by occupation ID. `jobs` and `annualOpenings` are counts. `growthPercent` is signed and uses percent units (`4.3` means 4.3%).

The comparison page displays earnings, survey-based job satisfaction, and degree regret by academic major. Outlook values appear on the individual roadmap snapshots and alongside the AI exposure indicators on the AI resilience tab. That tab shows [Anthropic observed exposure and Microsoft AI applicability](ai-resilience/comparison-indicators.md) as separate source-specific measures; it does not equate either with a job-resilience score. The [AI resilience research report](ai-resilience/report.md) explains why a composite 0–100 resilience estimate is withheld.
